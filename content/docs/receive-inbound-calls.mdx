---
title: Receive incoming calls with your AI agent
description: Assign an active number, verify the voice path and connect signed call events to your application.
icon: PhoneIncome
---

Give an existing AI agent an incoming telephone line through Skysay. You need
both a number that receives calls and a configured voice path. Connecting an
MCP client alone does not make that client a realtime voice agent.

## Choose where the conversation runs

| Mode | Who runs the voice conversation? |
| --- | --- |
| Hosted voice AI agent | Skysay runs the AI agent's configured conversation engine and workflow. |
| External text | Your service supplies text replies through the documented integration; Skysay provides the voice pipeline. |
| External audio | Your service handles the realtime audio connection and its voice behavior. |

Follow [wire an AI agent](/docs/wire-an-agent) for the exact supported mode and
transport. A LangGraph planner that can call an MCP tool is not automatically
an audio-stream consumer. Do not point a carrier number at an ordinary chat
webhook and expect a spoken conversation.

## Prepare the number and AI agent

Choose an available number type with incoming-call capability in the required
country. Complete its verification and activation. **100+ countries covered**
does not mean every number type in every country supports voice and SMS.
Check the selected number's actual capability and pricing.

For a hosted AI agent, configure and test its voice, language, first message,
instructions and any workflow/tools before routing live calls to it. For an
external integration, test its authentication, connection lifecycle and supported
audio/text contract. Keep customer secrets out of prompts and call context.

Use the [browser test](/docs/trial-calls) first. It proves the browser media
path for that configuration, not the carrier leg or number routing.

A hosted AI agent understands a caller in any language its transcriber can
recognise, not only its own: callers do not choose their language to suit the
agent. It still answers in its own published languages. To trade that for
slightly better accuracy in the agent's own language, lock listening to it (see
[Hearing the other party in any language](/docs/multilingual-outbound#hearing-the-other-party-in-any-language)).

## Route your number

In the workspace, open the number's routing controls and choose the AI agent
that should answer. Review Number rules and any time-based routing, default or
fallback behavior. The controls are also available from the AI agent's Phone
section; [number routing](/docs/wire-an-agent#3-point-a-number-at-it) describes
how assignment works.

Confirm you are changing a number in the intended workspace and understand
what its current callers will reach. Do not replace a working business line's
route as an exploratory test. Use an isolated test number first.

A stopped or unready AI agent cannot answer normally just because it is assigned
to a number. If routing fails, inspect the workspace's readiness message rather
than repeatedly changing carrier settings.

## Prove one incoming call

Call the test number from a phone you control, with any required notices and
recording consent configured. Telephone and AI usage can be charged.

Check these separately:

1. The correct number and AI agent received the call.
2. The caller heard the intended first message and could exchange turns.
3. The intended language, interruption and escalation behavior worked.
4. The call ended as expected and produced the available call evidence and cost.
5. Any promised external action actually occurred in its destination system.

A webhook arriving or a call appearing in the workspace is not proof of two-way
audio. A browser conversation is not proof of an incoming carrier call. Keep
these acceptance results distinct.

## Send events to your application

Register a project-scoped webhook endpoint if your app needs to react to call
completion, failure or transcript readiness. Follow
[webhook registration and verification](/docs/webhooks) rather than inventing a
callback format. Registration requires `agents:write`; reading call results uses
`calls:read`, with `recordings:read` for recording/transcript content.

A webhook endpoint reports events; it does not become the speech engine unless
you separately configure a supported external-agent integration. Webhook
registration creates a companion external-audio agent, so assign only the AI
agent you intended to use.

Verify the signature and timestamp over the raw request body, reject invalid
or stale deliveries, acknowledge promptly, and process work asynchronously.
Handle repeated deliveries idempotently and events that arrive out of order.
Do not log a signing secret, raw customer transcript or phone number in public
monitoring output. See the [signed delivery example](/docs/webhooks).

## If the call does not reach the AI agent

Check activation, inbound capability, the selected route and Number rules,
AI-agent readiness and the external service's connectivity if applicable.
Inspect the call's recorded reason in [call evidence](/docs/read-call-evidence).
Escalate carrier-route problems with the call ID through a private support
channel; do not publish customer numbers or recordings.

This documentation is checked against the public routing, voice-mode and webhook
contracts. No carrier test call is run as part of this docs PR; complete the
controlled phone test above with your own configuration before relying on it.
