A workspace that holds a phone number, bought or imported, can place outbound
calls and send SMS straight away, for any purpose. There is no approval step
and no list of blocked words: you call your own customers.

Every call is still checked before it dials. A call is refused for a
suspension, a sanctioned destination, a destination outside your workspace's
own country list, a caller number that still needs its carrier registration,
or a caller ID that is not one of your workspace's numbers. Scopes fail
closed, and an agent should still check the decision and cost before it
places a live call.

## Your workspace capabilities

Every workspace has one **capabilities** record from the moment it is created.
It says what the workspace may do outbound: the use-case profiles, the channels
(voice, SMS), the destinations, the caps, and — for a customer application —
the calling rules. `authorize_outbound` checks it on every attempt.

Read and manage it on the **Capabilities** page in the workspace, or
programmatically: `GET /v1/organizations/{organization_id}/capabilities`
(`onboarding:read`) and `POST /v1/organizations/{organization_id}/capabilities`
(`onboarding:write`), or the `get_capabilities` / `request_capabilities` MCP
tools.

### Active from the start

A new workspace's record is active the moment the workspace is created. It
starts on the default profile (business support & callbacks) with its catalog
caps, both channels (voice and SMS), and **every destination except sanctioned
countries**. Sanctioned destinations are blocked for every workspace in every
state. Business verification is not an outbound step: it is what a phone
number whose country requires carrier registration needs before that number
can call out. Operators can tighten from there; you can change your selection
below.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Active | Outbound calls and SMS are authorized within the profiles, channels, destinations and caps on the record. Every workspace starts here. |
| Pending review | A request needs an Skysay operator (the custom implementation-services profile). Nothing new is authorized yet; what was active stays active. |
| Suspended | An operator paused outbound for this workspace. |
| Not activated | The workspace's record is missing. Outbound is refused; contact support. |

### Destination controls

The Capabilities page shows your workspace's available destinations first.
Ordinary business and personal calls do not require a separate use-case
application. Workspace limits, acceptable-use rules, recipient permissions,
number-specific requirements and carrier availability still apply.

Use **Restrict destinations** to open **Optional destination restrictions** and narrow the destinations your
workspace can call or text. Choose all currently available destinations, or
search and select specific countries. This setting is separate from your
workspace's authorization: removing your restriction cannot undo an operator
restriction or activate a suspended workspace.

For API clients, read `destination_revision` from
`GET /v1/organizations/{organization_id}/capabilities`, then send
`POST /v1/organizations/{organization_id}/capabilities/destination-restrictions`
with `onboarding:write`:

```json
{"countries": ["EE", "GB"], "revision": 0}
```

Use `countries: null` to remove only your optional restriction. An empty list
is refused. Use uppercase supported two-letter country codes within your
workspace's available destinations. A stale revision is refused; reload before
saving instead of overwriting another user's change. The restriction is checked
for calls, SMS and campaign admission; it does not hang up an existing call.

Syria is no longer on Skysay's blanket country block. This does not remove
targeted sanctions, acceptable-use rules, number restrictions, carrier routing
requirements or pricing checks.

### Workspace limits and higher-limit requests

**Workspace limits** separates **Calling & messaging** from **Spending safeguards**.
It shows current operator spend/volume controls and the effective
plan or operator allowance for simultaneous calls. Calls per day and
simultaneous calls are different limits. Daily controls use a rolling 24-hour
window.

- **Wallet spending** limits posted wallet spending plus a proposed operation.
  It is not your wallet balance.
- **Voice spending** is an admission estimate for voice operations. Final
  settlement uses actual metered usage; it is not an additional fee.
- **Simultaneous calls** limits calls active together. A phone number or carrier
  may impose a lower limit.
- **Unique destinations** counts distinct recipients in the rolling window.

Use **Request higher limits** to describe the volume or concurrency you need.
This creates an operator review without raising a cap, changing prices,
reserving funds or approving any calls. The API equivalent is
`POST /v1/organizations/{organization_id}/capabilities/limit-requests`
(`onboarding:write`) with a `reason` of 10–2,000 characters. It returns the
review ID and status.

The existing profile-request API remains available for explicit configuration
and custom implementation-service requests. It is not required to choose an
ordinary use case on the Capabilities page. Existing operator restrictions and
custom review requirements remain in force.

### Calling rules

An operator can attach **calling rules** to your record for a customer
application (today the `payment_reminder` rule set: effective period, local
calling windows, per-case cadence and attempt cap, script version, and the
stop / wrong-party / dispute actions). `GET .../capabilities` returns them as
`calling_rules` and whether they are enforced right now as
`calling_rules_active`. A self-serve request cannot set or clear them.

### Purpose and authorization are optional

`purpose`, `categories` and `user_authorization` are optional on every call and
SMS. When you send them, Skysay records them with the call or message; it never
refuses a call for leaving them out, and never refuses one for what they say.
`sales`, `telemarketing`, `lead_generation` and `mass_outreach` are ordinary
purposes. The purpose catalog in the API reference is a list of suggestions,
not an allowlist.

### A call held for review

A call your workspace capabilities hold, for example to a destination outside
your country list or a batch under the custom implementation-services
profile, is answered with `status: "needs_review"` and is never dialled. A
`call.failed` event with failure stage `outbound_authorization` follows at
once, so your integration can treat it as refused. The request stays with
Skysay for review, but nothing will place the call later. A held call reserves
nothing from your wallet and does not count toward your voice spend cap. It
does count toward your call-count limits, because it was a submitted call. To
clear it, cancel it with `POST /v1/calls/{call_id}/cancel`.

### The caller number's own limits

Each number's Number rules can narrow what it may call out to, on top of your
workspace capabilities. In the workspace they are under **Numbers →** the
number **→ Advanced call policy**; through the API they are the `outgoing`
object of `PATCH /v1/phone-numbers/{number_id}/number-rules`:

- `can_be_used_as_caller_id`: `false` stops the number from placing calls.
- `allowed_countries`: when not empty, the number may only call these
  countries (ISO 3166-1 alpha-2, decided from the destination number). An
  empty list, the default, adds no limit.
- `allowed_agents`: when not empty, only these AI agents may call from it.
- `max_concurrent_calls`: how many calls the number may have live at once
  (`0` means no number limit).

A call these rules refuse is answered `403` and no call is created. The
message names the number, its list and the destination country, for example:

```text
+15551230001 may only place outbound calls to EE, US, AE, PL under its Number rules, so this call to destination country IT was refused. Change Allowed countries under Numbers > Advanced call policy (outgoing.allowed_countries in PATCH /v1/phone-numbers/num_8a2fQ1xYz/number-rules), or clear the list to remove the limit.
```

The route in the message carries that number's own id, so you can copy it.

### Why a call was refused before dialling

A call is checked twice before it dials, in this order:

1. **Workspace capabilities** (`outbound_authorization`): destination,
   channel, use-case profile, review signals and caps.
2. **The per-call policy** (`outbound_policy`): no suspension, a caller
   number that is ready to call out (its carrier registration is complete
   where its country needs one), and a valid destination.

<img src="/diagrams/outbound-checks.svg" alt="An outbound call request passes, in order, Number rules (caller ID, countries, agents), workspace capabilities (country list, channels, caps), the per-call policy (suspension, number registration) and a funds hold before it dials. A Number rules refusal is a 403 with no call record; a capabilities refusal is needs_review or policy_blocked; a per-call policy refusal is policy_blocked. The simultaneous-call limit and do-not-call list are also checked, and a refused call is never dialled or retried for you." />

A call refused at either check ends with `status: "policy_blocked"` (or
`"needs_review"`) and is never dialled. Every read of it (the response to
`POST /v1/calls`, `GET /v1/calls/{call_id}`, `GET /v1/calls` and the MCP call
tools) carries a `refusal` object saying why:

```json
"refusal": {
  "stage": "outbound_policy",
  "decision": "blocked",
  "reason_codes": ["number.registration_not_ready"],
  "required_actions": ["complete_number_registration"],
  "message": "The caller number is not ready to call out yet: finish its carrier registration first."
}
```

`refusal` is `null` on every call that was not refused before dialling. Fix
what `message` names and place the call again; a refused call is never
retried for you.

### Check before you call

`POST /v1/policies/authorize-outbound` (`policy:evaluate`), or the
`authorize_outbound` MCP tool, asks the same two questions without creating
anything. For a voice call it answers with the first check that refuses,
naming it in `stage`. When both checks pass, it answers `allowed` with
`stage: "outbound_authorization"` and the policy evaluation in `policy`.

When you send `from_number_id`, it first asks that number's own limits, as
the call does: whether the number may be used as caller ID and whether its
`allowed_countries` cover the destination. A refusal there answers `blocked`
with `stage: "outbound_number_rules"`, `reason_code`
`number_rules.caller_id_disabled` or `number_rules.country_not_allowed`, and
the same sentence `POST /v1/calls` would refuse with in `customer_reason`.
The number's `allowed_agents` are not checked, because the preflight does not
name an AI agent.

It is a preflight, not a full admission: concurrency (the plan's and the
number's `max_concurrent_calls`), maintenance windows and the wallet
reservation are not checked. Spend caps are checked only against the
`amount_minor` you send. Caller-number readiness and the number's limits are
checked only when you send `from_number_id`; the call itself always needs one.

### Your own batches [#authorized-application-batches]

Any active workspace may run its own batches through the single-call API;
there is no per-profile campaign switch. Send each call with `bulk: true` and
your own batch label as `campaign_id`; it is an ordinary call. A
`user_authorization` or a `relationship` is recorded when you send one and is
never required. The custom implementation-services profile holds every batch
for review (`needs_review.custom_always_review`). Every other check still
applies: sanctioned destinations, your country list, caps and calling rules.

### Campaigns

Campaigns are part of what every active workspace may do. A launch is checked
against your capabilities record and the outbound policy, in this order: a
sanctioned destination in the list blocks it (`blocked_country`); a suspended
workspace blocks it (`workspace_suspended`); a workspace whose record is
missing holds it (`workspace_not_approved`); voice missing from your channels holds it
(`channel_not_enabled`); a destination outside your allowlist holds it for the
usual first-use review (`new_country_requires_review`); a missing consent basis blocks it
(`consent_basis_required`); and on the custom implementation-services profile
every launch is reviewed (`needs_review.custom_always_review`). The
`campaigns_not_in_profile` reason code no longer exists. Your caps are
enforced per call while the campaign runs, never at launch: the wizard's
review step shows each of these checks for the draft and how many days the
list takes under your daily call cap. See [Campaigns](/docs/campaigns).

## Self-hosted gateways use the same gate

A self-hosted gateway in `local` outbound mode does not call
`create_outbound_call`; it calls
`POST /v1/gateway-deployments/{gateway_id}/outbound-calls` with its own
deployment-bound token. That route runs the identical admission — the same
`authorize_outbound` decision, the same kill switches and caps, the same wallet
reserve — and only then mints the single-use authorization the host needs to
reach media. A blocked decision returns `authorized: false` with no
authorization, and the call is recorded as `policy_blocked` exactly as it would
be for a remote request. See [Deployment modes](/docs/deployment-modes) for how
a deployment chooses its originator.

## What is never refused

There is no blocked-category list. Skysay does not refuse a call or SMS for its
purpose, its categories, or for leaving out `user_authorization`: a workspace
calls its own customers, for sales, telemarketing, lead generation, debt
collection or anything else. Your workspace and your numbers stay bound by the
acceptable-use policy, and an operator can suspend a workspace that abuses it.
