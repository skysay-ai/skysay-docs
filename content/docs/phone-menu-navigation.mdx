An outbound call to a company often reaches an automated menu ("press 2 for
English") and then a hold queue, not a person. With **phone menu navigation**
on, the AI agent listens to each menu, presses the key that fits the call's
task (or answers a menu that asks you to speak), waits quietly on hold, and
starts talking when a person picks up. It is on by default for every AI agent,
and you turn it off per AI agent.

It works on **outbound calls only**: the calls your AI agent places and
campaign calls. Inbound calls, including the phone test in the agent editor
(you dial in to it), browser tests, [Simulations Lab](/docs/simulations-lab),
chat and SMS never navigate.

**A call can last at most 29 minutes, including time in menus and on hold. If nobody answers by then, the call ends as “Hold limit reached”** (`ended_reason` `ivr_timeout`, `reason_detail` `deadline`). No setting raises this limit.

This page is the whole picture in one place. The reference for each part is in
[Answer detection](/docs/answer-detection) (the setting, the menu hint and the
languages), [Call variables](/docs/call-variables) (numbers the agent may type)
and [Post-call results](/docs/post-call-results#why-a-call-ended) (the ending
values). Campaign retries are described in [Campaigns](/docs/campaigns).

## Greetings and background music

A short greeting or an unclear spoken menu fragment does not immediately take
control away from the conversation. Clear keypad choices, multiple spoken
choices, queue announcements, or further menu instructions provide stronger
evidence. A speech menu that only asks an open question may hear the agent's
opening before later instructions establish that it is a menu.

After reaching a person, a menu phrase or repeated-recording signal alone
starts bounded listening without muting the conversation. Further menu or queue
instructions can confirm a transfer. When the separate navigation listener is
available, its transcription supplies that confirmation; conversation captions
can request listening but cannot count the same announcement again. Delayed
captions, source outages, and a short menu that finishes before listening starts
can delay or prevent detection.

There are at most four hint-triggered listening windows per call, each lasting
up to 60 seconds. Hints do not extend an open window. Once a listener-confirmed
menu or queue label is awaiting a second confirmation, agent actions such as
ending the call, sending an SMS, or transferring can be temporarily refused.
The conversation stays audible; a new person response or the window's expiry
releases that pending check. Listening during a confirmed menu, hold, or music
is separate from this hint budget.

If an attempted greeting produces no audible speech, the agent can retry it
once while the person's greeting is still recent. An unanswered, silent
attempt is not evidence that nobody is there. For AI agents on Cascade or
Gemini Live that do not run a conversation workflow, an unheard first reply
after pickup can also be retried once after the person speaks again, provided
no other reply is still playing or has been heard. AI agents that run a
conversation workflow do not retry that reply. Provider failures can still
leave the person without a reply; these retries are bounded and do not
guarantee recovery.

After a person has been recognised, **music alone does not put the conversation
on hold**, even during a long pause. The agent keeps listening normally. This
protects conversations with background music, but an unannounced transfer
straight to music may be detected late or missed. Ordinary conversation silence
rules still apply in that case. Confirmed menu and queue instructions can still
establish a new episode; music inside an established menu retains hold handling.
Music at the initial answer, before a person is recognised, can still indicate
a hold.

This is not voice isolation or a guarantee of perfect recognition. Loud music
and singing can hide a person's words; if no person is recognised, the initial
music policy can still classify the call as a hold. Misclassified menu or queue
speech can also affect navigation. Test representative greetings, transfers and
background audio before an outbound campaign. Menu and hold time limits remain
the limits configured below.

## Which agents can use it

Phone menu navigation works for AI agents on the **Cascade** engine, including
AI agents that run a conversation workflow, and for AI agents on **Gemini Live**.
AI agents on **GPT-Live** do not navigate yet.

Nothing is refused because an AI agent cannot navigate yet. A GPT-Live AI agent
keeps its navigation setting, saves and publishes as usual, and its calls run
without navigation. It starts navigating by itself when GPT-Live is supported,
with no change on your side. In the workspace the setting stays as you left it,
with a line under it saying why it is not active. Through the API,
`GET /v1/agents/{agent_id}/voice-stack` reports `navigation.eligible` `false`
with `ineligible_reason` `engine_not_supported` (see
[Read what an AI agent will do](#read-what-an-ai-agent-will-do)). Each engine in
the agent catalog (`GET /v1/agent-catalog`) says whether its calls navigate in
`supports_navigation`.

The same holds for an AI agent whose languages the transcribers cannot all
listen to (see [Languages](#languages)), and for one whose own tools leave no
room for navigation (see [Your own tools](#your-own-tools)).

## Turn it off or change its limits [#turn-it-on]

Every AI agent navigates unless you turn it off. An AI agent that never made a
choice follows the platform default, which is on.

In the workspace, open the AI agent, then **Tools**. **Navigate phone menus and
wait on hold** is ticked by default. Untick it to turn navigation off for this
AI agent. That stores an explicit off: every later save keeps it, and a change
of the platform default never reaches it. Tick it again to turn navigation back
on. Two limits appear under it, with notes on the menu languages and on which
voices may say a value (see [Languages](#languages) and
[Typed and spoken values](#typed-and-spoken-values)):

| Setting | API field | Default | Range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Max hold time** | `navigation.max_hold_seconds` | 600 seconds (10 minutes) | 10 seconds up to 29 minutes: a whole call lasts at most 29 minutes, menus and hold included |
| **Max time in menus** | `navigation.max_navigation_seconds` | 180 seconds (3 minutes) | 10 seconds up to 15 minutes |

Time in menus and time on hold have separate limits: a long hold never uses up
the time allowed for menus, and the other way round. The API schema allows up to
3600 seconds for hold and 900 seconds for menus, but a hold or a menu cannot
outlast the call, so the 29-minute call maximum caps both.

Through the API the same setting is the `navigation` section of
`PATCH /v1/agents/{agent_id}/voice-stack` (MCP tool `set_agent_voice_stack`).
`enabled` takes one of three values:

| `enabled` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `true` | On. |
| `false` | Off. Every save keeps it, and a change of the platform default never reaches it. |
| `null` | Follow the platform default, which is on. |

To turn navigation off for an AI agent:

```bash
curl -X PATCH https://api.skysay.ai/v1/agents/agt_123/voice-stack \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"navigation": {"enabled": false}}'
```

To follow the default and allow 15 minutes on hold:

```bash
curl -X PATCH https://api.skysay.ai/v1/agents/agt_123/voice-stack \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"navigation": {"enabled": null, "max_hold_seconds": 900}}'
```

Every write of the section must say what `enabled` is. An empty object
(`{"navigation": {}}`), `"navigation": null`, or a section without `enabled` is
refused with `400` `navigation_enabled_required`, and the response names the
field `navigation.enabled`. An older client sent `{}` to mean off; it is refused
rather than guessed at. The section is replaced whole: a limit you leave out
goes back to its default, so send every limit you want to keep.

You can send back the `navigation` section that
`GET /v1/agents/{agent_id}/voice-stack` returned, as it is: the read-only fields
`effective`, `eligible` and `ineligible_reason` are accepted and ignored on
write (see [Read what an AI agent will do](#read-what-an-ai-agent-will-do)).
Any other unknown field is refused with `400`.

While navigation is on, by your choice or by default, neither limit can be
longer than the AI agent's maximum call length (`turn_taking.max_call_seconds`,
1740 seconds = 29 minutes unless you shorten it), because a hold or a menu
cannot outlast the call that contains it. A longer limit is refused with `400`,
and so is shortening the maximum call length below a limit you set, or turning
navigation back on beside such a limit. A limit you keep beside
`enabled: false` is kept as it is.

The limits belong to the AI agent and are not changed per call. Time in menus
and time on hold are counted separately across the whole call, and the call
also ends at its overall maximum length. The setting applies when a call
starts; saving never changes a call in progress, and no republish is needed.

### Other routes that write it

- **Agent create** (`POST /v1/agents`, MCP tool `create_agent`) accepts the
  same section as `metadata.navigation`, under the same rules: `enabled` is
  required in it. Leave the section out and the new AI agent follows the
  default.
- **Agent update** (`PATCH /v1/agents/{agent_id}`, MCP tool `update_agent`)
  keeps the stored section when its `metadata` replacement leaves `navigation`
  out, so an explicit off survives any metadata replace. A different
  `metadata.navigation` is refused with `400` naming the voice-stack route, as
  `follow_caller` and `opening` are.

### Read what an AI agent will do

`GET /v1/agents/{agent_id}/voice-stack` always returns `navigation`:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `enabled` | The stored choice: `true`, `false`, or `null` when the AI agent never chose and follows the platform default. |
| `effective` | Whether the setting is on: the stored choice, or the platform default (on) when it is `null`. |
| `max_hold_seconds` | The hold limit, with the default applied. |
| `max_navigation_seconds` | The menu limit, with the default applied. |
| `eligible` | Whether this AI agent's outbound calls may navigate. |
| `ineligible_reason` | Why `eligible` is `false`, else `null`. |

For a GPT-Live AI agent that never chose:

```json
{
  "navigation": {
    "enabled": null,
    "effective": true,
    "max_hold_seconds": 600,
    "max_navigation_seconds": 180,
    "eligible": false,
    "ineligible_reason": "engine_not_supported",
    "ineligible_languages": []
  }
}
```

| `ineligible_reason` | Why its calls do not navigate | What changes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `turned_off` | `enabled` is `false`. | Send `true` or `null`, or tick the box. |
| `not_released` | Navigation is switched off platform-wide. | Nothing on your side. |
| `engine_not_supported` | The AI agent's conversation engine, GPT-Live today, does not navigate yet. | It starts by itself when the engine is supported, or switch to Cascade or Gemini Live. |
| `languages_uncovered` | No transcriber Skysay can use for phone menus listens in one of the AI agent's languages. | Remove that language. |
| `too_many_sockets` | Listening in all of the AI agent's languages would need more than two transcriber connections. | Remove a language. |
| `no_language` | The AI agent has no language selected. | Select a language. |
| `transcriber_unverified` | The AI agent's own transcriber (on Gemini Live, its captions; for a workflow, its listener) was measured to fail on phone menus in one of its languages. | Choose another transcriber. |
| `listener_uncovered` | The published workflow's listener does not listen in one of the AI agent's or the workflow's languages. | Add that language to the listener, or remove it from the AI agent. |
| `controller_lines_missing` | A workflow AI agent answers menus with prepared lines, and one of its languages has none yet. | Its calls run without navigation until the lines exist. |
| `tool_name_conflict` | One of the AI agent's own tools is named `press_digits`, `wait_on_hold` or `abandon_navigation`. | Rename that tool. |
| `tool_budget` | The AI agent's own tools leave no room for the three navigation tools. | Remove a tool. |

`ineligible_languages` names the languages a language reason is about (base
codes such as `fi`), and is empty for every other reason.

`eligible` describes the AI agent, not one call. Each call is still decided
when it starts, on its direction and the configuration it runs with, and the
call's timeline shows what happened.

The workspace shows the same reasons as a line under the
**Navigate phone menus and wait on hold** row, which stays operable. For
GPT-Live it reads: "Not active on GPT-Live yet. This AI agent's calls run
without it, and it switches on by itself when GPT-Live is supported."

## What the agent does

It has four jobs, and nothing else changes while it is in a menu or a queue:

- **Answering menus.** It listens to each menu and answers it the way the menu
  asks. On a keypad menu it presses the key that fits the call's task and listens
  again. On a menu that asks you to speak ("say billing") it says the answer.
  While it is in a menu or a queue, silence, hold music or a recorded "goodbye"
  never hangs the call up. A menu is often read in pieces with short pauses,
  and a line such as "To hear these options again," can arrive on its own before
  its key ("press nine"). The agent keeps the pieces of one menu together and
  presses a key only once the menu has finished, choosing among all of its
  options; this can add a few seconds before the key is sent. A new question
  that follows a menu ("Please say your account number") is answered on its own.
- **Waiting on hold.** It stays silent through the whole queue: hold music,
  announcements, and "press 1 to stay in the queue", which it answers by
  pressing the key. Only the two limits or the call's maximum length end the wait
  early.
- **Declining callback offers.** When a menu offers to call you back instead of
  holding, the agent declines it automatically, by key or by voice. Silence never
  accepts a callback either. If the offer has an option to stay in the queue, it
  takes that one. On Gemini Live, a callback offer that can only be answered by
  speech gets no spoken decline: the callback is never taken, and the call stays
  in the queue.
- **Giving up on a menu.** When no option fits the call's task, the menu asks
  for something the call does not carry, or the key presses change nothing, it
  ends the call instead of guessing. The call ends as **Menu had no matching
  option** and is not redialled.

**Hold music and answer detection.** While a call is in a menu or on hold, the
non-human-audio ending of [Answer detection](/docs/answer-detection#the-ending-it-is-paired-with)
does not end it, even when the AI agent's `answer_detection.non_human_audio` is
`end_call`: hold music is what a queue sounds like. If an AI agent must hang up
on hold music, turn navigation off for it.

When something that may be a person answers, the agent first confirms it with a
short check question, such as whether it is speaking with a person. Only then
does it start the conversation.

**Tell it what to expect.** If you know the menu, pass `navigation_hint` in the
call's `call_context` or as a campaign column (for example `Press 2 for English,
then 1 for billing.`). It is plain text of at most 500 characters, reaches the
agent as the built-in variable `call.navigation_hint`, and a longer or non-text
hint refuses the call with `call_variables_invalid`. It is a hint, not a script:
the agent still answers the menu it actually hears.

## Numbers the menu asks for

A menu often asks for a number: a card number, a PIN, a government ID, a date of
birth, an account or booking reference. These come **only from the call's own
data**, as a [keypad variable](/docs/call-variables): you declare the variable
with `keypad` and a class (`card`, `pin`, `government_id`, `date_of_birth`,
`reference` or `other`) and send its value with the call. The agent never
makes one up, and a menu that asks for a number the call does not carry gets
nothing.

- **The model never sees the value.** It is not in the prompt, the
  conversation, a tool request or a webhook. The agent can ask for the field to
  be entered; the platform fetches the value at that moment and sends it.
- **Transcripts show the field, not the digits.** A typed value reads as
  `Entered {field}` (for example `Entered {account_number}`) in transcripts, the
  timeline and logs.
- **The value is deleted at the end of the call.** It is not kept in the call
  record afterwards, so a retry needs the value sent again with the new call.

### Typed and spoken values

Most menus take a number on the keypad. Typed values work on every supported
engine, everywhere keys are accepted, on agents whose every language is one
Skysay can recognise numbers in: English, German, French, Spanish, Italian,
Dutch, Portuguese and Russian (see [Languages](#languages)). Some menus accept a number only by voice
("say your account number"). The platform then speaks the digits itself, in the
agent's own voice, without the model.

- **Spoken values** (a stored value said aloud to a prompt that asks for it to be
  spoken) are supported on Cascade agents only, workflow agents included, and
  only with these voices: Azure AI Speech in English, German, French, Spanish,
  Italian, Dutch, Portuguese and Russian; Gemini TTS in English, German, French,
  Spanish, Dutch and Russian; ElevenLabs Flash v2.5 in English. A workflow
  agent's voice must also be one a spoken workflow accepts, which today leaves
  Azure AI Speech and ElevenLabs Flash v2.5 in English (see
  [Spoken workflow calls](/docs/agent-workflows#which-voices-and-listeners-a-spoken-workflow-accepts)).
  With any other voice or language, the value is entered on the keypad only, and a
  question that can be answered by speech alone ends the call.
- **On Gemini Live,** a prompt that takes speech only ends the call as
  **data not authorised** (`ended_reason` `ivr_timeout`, `reason_detail`
  `data_not_authorised`) before the value is read. Prompts that take keys, take
  either, or don't say how to answer still get the value as keypad tones.

## Languages

There is no separate list of menu languages. Phone menu navigation works in
each of an AI agent's languages that a transcriber Skysay can use for phone
menus listens in, as long as all of the agent's languages can be listened to
with at most two connections (see below). How well a menu is understood
depends on the transcriber and the language: Skysay has tested English, German
and Polish end to end, while in Estonian and Finnish some menus and greetings are
still misread. Those calls navigate on a best-effort basis. A keypad value is
not given in Estonian or Finnish yet (see [Typed and spoken values](#typed-and-spoken-values)).

Skysay tests each transcriber on recorded phone-menu lines, language by
language. A transcriber that fails those tests in a language is not used for it:
another transcriber that passes takes its place, and an AI agent whose own
transcriber failed in one of its languages does not navigate
(`ineligible_reason` `transcriber_unverified`, naming the language) until you
choose another one. Today that is Deepgram Nova-3 in Estonian: it misheard the
words to say to Estonian speech menus, so an Estonian AI agent on Deepgram
navigates once it uses another transcriber, such as Azure or Soniox.

The line's language is not the AI agent's to choose: an outbound call reaches
whatever menu and whatever person answers. So a call listens to the line in
more than the agent's own languages. When the transcriber listening to the line
can hear several languages at once without a list of candidates (Deepgram
Nova-3's multilingual mode), the call listens in every language of that mode,
less any it failed Skysay's phone-menu tests in. A menu, a queue or a person in
a language the agent does not speak is then still heard: an English-only agent
answers a Polish menu, and takes the "for English, press 2" read out inside it.
The agent still speaks only its own languages.

A call that holds a keypad value listens in the agent's own languages only,
because a value read back on the line is hidden only in a language Skysay
recognises numbers in.

If a reply that would have pressed a key is cut off because the menu was still
talking, the agent is shown the menu again once the line goes quiet.

Agents that have a language Skysay cannot recognise numbers in yet, such as
Polish, navigate their menus but cannot enter keypad values
(`value_language_unsupported`). That applies to the whole agent: a typed value
is not entered on any call of an agent that has such a language, and the menus
are still navigated. When such a call reaches a question that asks for a value
it carries, such as a PIN or a card number, the call ends as **data not
authorised** (`ended_reason` `ivr_timeout`, `reason_detail`
`data_not_authorised`).

To listen to the far end the agent uses at most two speech-to-text connections,
chosen from the transcribers Skysay can use for phone menus for the agent's
selected languages. If the AI agent's languages cannot be covered that way,
nothing is refused: its
calls run without navigation, and the voice-stack response says why in
`ineligible_reason`: `languages_uncovered` (no supported transcriber hears one of
its languages) or `too_many_sockets` (every language is covered, but not by two
connections). Remove a language to use navigation. Details are in [Answer detection](/docs/answer-detection#languages).

## Your own tools

Navigation gives the call three tools of its own: `press_digits`,
`wait_on_hold` and `abandon_navigation`. These names are reserved: a tool of
yours with one of them is refused with `400`. An AI agent that already has a
tool with one of these names does not navigate (`ineligible_reason`
`tool_name_conflict`) until you rename it.

A call carries at most 32 tool names: the AI agent's own tools, `end_call`, a
slot always kept for `search_knowledge_base` (whether or not a knowledge base is
attached), and the three navigation tools. An AI agent whose own tools leave no
room for the three does not navigate (`ineligible_reason` `tool_budget`), so an
AI agent that should navigate can have at most 27 tool names of its own.
Remove a tool to make room.

## Lines that carry keys

Pressing keys needs a carrier line where key delivery has been proven. Skysay
records this per line and sends keys only on a line that is proven: DIDWW is
proven today, and every other line is refused until it is. A customer-owned trunk
and, today, the DIDLogic line do not send keys. The agent does not pretend: the
timeline says **Keys not supported on this line**, and a menu that needs keys
ends the call as **Menu had no matching option**. Menus you can answer by
speaking, and holds, work on every line.

A self-hosted (open-source) worker does not send keys either. The table that says
which carrier lines can carry key presses is not part of the open-source export,
so a self-hosted worker refuses key presses and reports **Keys not supported on
this line**. See [Deployment modes](/docs/deployment-modes).

## What makes it reliable

Navigation is not left to prompt instructions. It rests on these parts.

- **A detector, not prompt instructions.** A detector tells a menu, a queue, a
  recording and a person apart, rather than relying on instructions in the
  agent's prompt.
- **A person check before the agent talks.** When something answers, the agent
  confirms a real person with a short check question, then continues the
  conversation.
- **Carrier-proven key delivery.** Keys are sent only on carriers proven to
  deliver them.
- **Sensitive values isolated from the model.** Card, PIN, government ID and date
  of birth come only from the call's own data, and the model never sees them.
- **Automatic callback decline.** Callback offers are declined automatically,
  and silence never accepts one.
- **Separate time limits for menus and holds.** A long queue cannot use up the
  time allowed for menus, and the other way round, and the whole call is still
  capped at 29 minutes.
- **Tested before every release.** Every release is tested against about 40
  call scenarios, on each supported engine where the scenario applies.

## What you see in the workspace

**Calls list.** The list shows short chips. A call that went through a menu gets
one quiet chip in the Outcome column, for example **Menu → hold 6:40**. A call
that stopped there also gets a short ending chip:

| Chip | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Hold limit** | nobody picked up within the max hold time, or the call reached its maximum length while it waited |
| **No menu match** | the menu had no option that fits the call's task, or asked for a number the call did not carry |
| **Menu time limit** | the agent used the time allowed for menus without reaching a queue or a person |
| **Hung up on hold** | the other side hung up while the call was on hold |
| **Answered by a machine** | the number answered with a recorded message only: no menu, no person |

The **Failure reason** filter has the first three, under Telephony, with their
full names (**Hold limit reached**, **Menu had no matching option**, **Time
limit in phone menus reached**).

**Call details.** The header splits the call's duration into **Menu**, **Hold**
and **Conversation**, and says in one sentence how the call ended. The timeline
shows, in order: each menu the agent detected, each key it sent, a typed value
as `Entered {field}`, each interval on hold, **Keys not supported on this line**
where a line cannot carry keys, and the moment a person answered, with the short
check that confirmed them, before the conversation itself.

## How these calls end

These endings are reported on the call resource as `ended_reason` with a
`reason_detail`. A campaign gives each one a disposition and retries it under the
same per-outcome rules as other calls (see [Campaigns](/docs/campaigns)):

| `ended_reason` | `reason_detail` | Campaign disposition | Retried |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ivr_timeout` | `hold_limit` | `no_answer` | yes, when no person was reached earlier |
| `ivr_timeout` | `deadline` | `no_answer` | yes, when no person was reached earlier |
| `remote_party_disconnected` | `during_hold` | `no_answer` | yes, when no person was reached earlier |
| `ivr_timeout` | `menu_no_path` | `connected_completed` | no, terminal |
| `ivr_timeout` | `navigation_limit` | `connected_completed` | no, terminal |
| `ivr_timeout` | `data_not_authorised` | `connected_completed` | no, terminal |
| `no_human_after_answer` | `recorded_message_only` | `no_answer` | yes, like any other machine answer |
| `media_session_lost` | `navigation_detector_lost` | `connected_completed` | no, terminal |

The retried endings are the ones another attempt can change: the hold queue was
too long, the call ran out of time, or the other side hung up before a person
came on. The terminal ones are dead ends: the same menu will be there next time.
A line that only plays a recorded message shows as **Answered by a machine**.

`media_session_lost` with `navigation_detector_lost` is not a limit or a menu
outcome: it means the call could no longer follow the line, because none of the
sources that listen to the far end delivered anything for 30 seconds, so the
platform ended the call. The Calls list files it under the media failures as
**Media session lost**. It has no campaign rule of its own: the call connected,
so it takes `connected_completed` unless the agent or its workflow declared
another outcome, and a campaign does not retry it.

`person_reached` on the call resource says whether a person was reached earlier
in the call; it is `null` when the call did not report it, and a missing value
never causes a retry. These calls connected, so the carrier reports them as
completed, and the ending says why nothing came of the call.

## Cost

Time in menus and on hold is billed at your normal call rate, as part of one
call. Navigation adds no surcharge and there is no separate hold price; the split
in the call details is for reading, not for billing.

Waiting still costs call time. With the default limits, one call can spend up
to 180 seconds in menus and 600 seconds on hold before it ends, and all of it
is billed. When no person was reached earlier in the call, a campaign retries
three endings: `ivr_timeout` with `hold_limit` (the hold limit ran out) or
`deadline` (the call reached its maximum length), and
`remote_party_disconnected` with `during_hold`. So one contact can be billed
for several long waits. `ivr_timeout` with `navigation_limit` (the menu limit
ran out), `menu_no_path` or `data_not_authorised` is final and is not retried
(see [How these calls end](#how-these-calls-end)). Lower **Max hold time**, or turn navigation off for the AI
agent a campaign uses, if that matters more than reaching a person.

## Limits worth knowing

- A menu that changes its mind, forwards the line elsewhere or asks for
  something unexpected is answered as heard; the agent ends the call at a menu
  with no matching option rather than guess.
- How well the agent understands a menu is measured language by language before
  it is claimed (see [Answer detection](/docs/answer-detection)); a language that
  works on keys can still have a menu the agent misreads.
- Once a person answers, everything they say is an ordinary conversation, even
  if they read a number back.
