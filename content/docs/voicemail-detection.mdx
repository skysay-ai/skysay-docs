The phone network does not tell the caller when a call goes to voicemail. A
voicemail box answers the line exactly as a person does, so the only way to
know is to listen to the first seconds after the answer. Skysay does that on
every outbound call, for every AI agent and every conversation engine, and
records what it concluded.

It runs around the AI agent, not through it: detection is not part of the
agent's prompt or its workflow, and the agent never decides on its own that it
reached a voicemail box.

## Unsure means a person

A wrong "voicemail" verdict is worse than a missed one. Hanging up on a person
is an abandoned call, and in some countries that is regulated. So the rule is
lopsided on purpose:

- only evidence that is specific to a mailbox counts as proof;
- everything else only raises suspicion, and suspicion never acts alone;
- any sign of a person wins, and a call Skysay is not sure about is treated as
  a person.

## How a mailbox is recognised

### Proof: the only evidence that can make the agent act

There are two kinds of proof, and nothing else counts:

- **A record beep after a spoken greeting, in any language.** A single,
  clean tone that follows a greeting which was recognised as speech with at
  least three words in it. It listens to the sound and counts the words, but
  never needs to understand them. Words are counted by the spaces and
  punctuation between them, so in Chinese, Japanese, Thai and other scripts
  written without spaces a short greeting is undercounted, and a beep after
  it only raises suspicion. A tone that repeats, a keypad tone, a
  fax tone or a network information tone is not a record beep. Neither is a
  tone that overlaps the AI agent's own voice, or a tone after music or
  silence rather than speech.
- **A mailbox instruction** such as "please leave a message after the tone",
  only in a language whose phrase list has been verified. Today that is
  **English**, and English is checked on every call, whatever the call's
  language. A person talking *about* a message ("did you leave me a
  message?", "please don't leave a message") is not an instruction.

### Suspicion: signals that never act alone

- the call being answered unusually fast;
- the shape of the greeting: a long greeting that never pauses for a reply;
- music or tones with no speech in them, such as a caller tune;
- a beep after music or silence rather than after speech. A caller tune
  followed by network beeps sounds much like a mailbox, and often is not one.

Recognising known mailbox recordings by their sound, so-called fingerprints,
is planned and is not used today.

Phone menus, call screening services and "the number you have dialled is not
available" announcements are recognised as their own kinds of answer. They are
never treated as a mailbox by themselves, and a phone menu heard around a
mailbox phrase stops that phrase from counting.

Our own voice is excluded. When the AI agent's words come back down the line,
for example from a speakerphone, they are recognised as echo and never count
as evidence.

### Languages

| Language | Mailbox phrases |
| --- | --- |
| English | Verified: an instruction can prove a mailbox. Checked on every call. |
| Estonian, German, Spanish, French, Russian | Candidates, not yet verified: an instruction is shown in the call's evidence but never proves a mailbox. |
| Any other language, including Sinhala and Tamil | No phrase list. |

In a language without a verified phrase list, detection still works: a record
beep after a spoken greeting proves a mailbox in any language, including
Sinhala and Tamil, and an English mailbox instruction still counts. Each call's evidence says when its
language has no verified list. A candidate list becomes verified once a native
speaker or real local mailbox recordings have confirmed it, and this table
will change when that happens.

## The four settings

`answer_detection.voicemail` is set per agent, in the **When a voicemail
answers** card of the agent editor or through
`PATCH /v1/agents/{agent_id}/voice-stack`.

| Value | In the editor | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `shadow` | Watch only | Keeps talking as now and records what it would have done. |
| `hang_up` | Hang up | Ends the call without a message on proof of a mailbox. |
| `leave_message` | Leave a message | Plays your approved message after the beep, then ends the call. |
| `off` | Off | Detects nothing and records nothing. |

<Callout type="warn">
**Today every agent watches only.** `hang_up` and `leave_message` are accepted
and saved now, but they start acting only after the detector has passed its
accuracy check on real calls. Until then an agent set to either of them
behaves exactly like `shadow`, and each call's evidence shows both the setting
you chose and the one that ran.
</Callout>

`shadow` is what an agent with no stored value gets. As with
[non-human audio](/docs/answer-detection), a value you send is stored even when
it equals the default, so an agent you explicitly set to `shadow` keeps it if
the default for new agents changes later.

```bash
curl -X PATCH https://api.skysay.ai/v1/agents/agt_123/voice-stack \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"answer_detection": {"non_human_audio": "shadow", "voicemail": "hang_up"}}'
```

The `answer_detection` object is replaced as a whole on save, so send every key
you want to keep.

### Messages for Leave a message, one per language

`answer_detection.voicemail_messages` holds the messages left when the setting
is `leave_message`, one per language, keyed by language code:

```json
{
  "answer_detection": {
    "voicemail": "leave_message",
    "voicemail_messages": {
      "en": "Hello, this is Example Lending. Please call us back at the number shown. Thank you.",
      "et": "Tere, helistab Example Lending. Palun helistage meile tagasi kuvatud numbril. Täname."
    }
  }
}
```

- The message played is the one for the language the call is actually in. A
  call in a language with no message is ended without one; it is never given
  a message in another language.
- `leave_message` needs a message for at least one language. In the editor
  there is one box per language the agent speaks.
- Each message is plain text of at most 400 characters. A locale such as
  `de-DE` is stored as `de`, and an empty text removes that language's
  message.
- Messages are kept when you switch to another setting, and when you remove a
  language from the agent, so switching back loses nothing.

A message is played exactly as approved, never improvised by the AI agent, and
it is the same on every conversation engine. Each language's message is
approved on its own. Keep them neutral: who is calling and how to call back,
with no account, amount or other personal details, because a voicemail box
can be heard by someone other than the person you meant to reach. A saved
message is not played by any call today; see the note above.

## What happens on a call today

With `shadow`, and with the other two settings until they are released:

- the call runs exactly as it would without detection. The agent's opening,
  its conversation, its workflow and how the call ends are unchanged;
- nothing about detection appears in the call's `answer_classification`,
  `ended_reason`, webhooks or campaign results;
- the verdict and the evidence behind it are recorded with the call.

## Reading the evidence

Watch only exists so that you can see, call by call, what detection would have
done before it is allowed to do it. Open a call in **Calls**. An outbound call
that detection ran on shows a **Voicemail detection** block:

- the verdict: Voicemail, Voicemail? (suspicion only), Person, Not sure, Phone
  menu or queue, Call screener, or Unavailable announcement;
- the setting you chose and the one that ran, for example "Set to Hang up —
  watching only until the accuracy check";
- what the agent would have done and when, marked "not applied". For an agent
  on `leave_message` this is when the message would have started, measured
  from the end of the beep;
- whether a person was heard after that moment. This is the case the accuracy
  check exists to catch: a call where acting would have cut somebody off;
- each piece of evidence, marked as proof, suspicion, a sign of a person, or
  ignored, with when it was heard. A mailbox phrase that did not count says
  why, for example that its language's phrase list is not verified yet.

Inbound calls, and calls from before this feature, show no block. That means
nothing was checked, not that no voicemail was found.

## What comes next

When `hang_up` and `leave_message` are released, calls they end will carry
their own ended reason, and this page will list it. Retries stay with your
campaign's retry rule, and minutes up to the end of the call are billed as
usual.
