After a call's transcript is stored, Skysay writes a short AI summary of it.
It is on for every agent unless you turn it off, and it is free. Every field
is an **AI estimate**: read it as a pointer to the call, not as a record of it.
Skysay itself never acts on it. It does not retry, block, bill, or change
do-not-call because of anything in a summary.

## What a summary contains

| Field | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `summary` | One to three sentences about the call. The summary, `follow_up` and `goal_reason` are written in English, whatever language the call was held in. |
| `follow_up_needed`, `follow_up` | Whether someone still has to act after the call, and what, in one line. |
| `goal_met` | `yes`, `partly`, `no` or `unclear`, with a one-line `goal_reason`. |
| `goal_source` | What the call was judged against: `goal_line` (the agent's call goal) or `agent_instructions` (the agent's own published instructions, when it has no call goal). |
| `sentiment` | The other party's overall sentiment, not the agent's: `positive`, `neutral`, `negative` or `unclear`. |

`quality_flags` says what the summary was written from: `transcript_truncated`
when only the first part of a very long call fit the request (200 turns or
24,000 characters), and `transcript_partial` when the transcript was missing
the text of an agent reply.

Call facts such as the call's status, who answered, and its duration are not
part of the summary. They come from the platform, and the AI never decides
them.

## Read it

```text
→ get_call_summary(call_id="cl_...")   # recordings:read
GET /v1/calls/{call_id}/summary        # recordings:read
```

The summary is derived from the transcript, so it needs the same
`recordings:read` scope as the transcript. `calls:read` alone is refused.
`GET /v1/calls/{call_id}` and `get_call` are unchanged and do not include it.

| `status` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `ready` | `result` holds the summary. |
| `pending` | The transcript is stored and the summary is being written, usually within seconds. |
| `skipped` | No summary was written, and no AI request was made. `reason` is `no_conversation` (the other party said nothing, the call never connected, or the call was a simulated test such as `POST /v1/web-calls`, whose transcript is sample text rather than anything said) or `insufficient_transcript` (the other party spoke but none of it was transcribed). |
| `failed` | The summary could not be written after two attempts. |
| `not_available` | There is no summary for this call: summaries were off for its agent, its transcript was deleted or has not arrived yet, or nothing on the call was transcribed. |

There is no webhook event for summaries. Read them when you need them.

## Set the call goal, or turn summaries off

Each agent has one setting, `call_summary`:

```json
PATCH /v1/agents/{agent_id}
{ "call_summary": { "enabled": true, "goal": "Book a cleaning appointment and confirm the time." } }
```

- `goal` is optional, at most 4,000 characters. When it is set, `goal_met`
  judges the call against it. When it is empty, `goal_met` judges the call
  against the agent's published instructions as you wrote them, never against
  the values a call filled in.
- `enabled: false` turns summaries off for that agent's future calls. Calls
  already summarised keep their summary.

The same setting is accepted by `POST /v1/agents` and the MCP `update_agent`
tool, and `GET /v1/agents/{agent_id}` returns it. Your own
[post-call extraction](/docs/post-call-results) is a separate feature. A
summary never changes your extraction schema, its result, or its
`call.extraction.ready` event.

## Who processes the transcript

The summary is written by Google Gemini (Google LLC), which processes the
transcript as a sub-processor for this post-call analysis. Each request is
bounded: up to 24,000 characters of transcript and 4,000 characters of goal.
Google returns the analysis and Skysay stores it with the call.

## Deleting a transcript

Deleting a call's recording and transcript deletes that call's summary in the
same step. It also deletes your post-call extraction result for that call.

One case is not instant. If a summary request is already on its way to Google
when the transcript is deleted, that request still completes, usually within
about 30 seconds. Skysay then discards its answer and never stores it.

A corrected recording that replaces a call's transcript replaces its summary
too: the old summary is deleted with the old transcript, and a new one is
written from the new transcript.
