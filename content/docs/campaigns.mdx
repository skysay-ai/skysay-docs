A campaign is one contact list, one hosted agent, one or more caller-ID
numbers, a set of local calling hours and a per-outcome retry policy. Skysay
places every attempt as an ordinary outbound call, so the same policy, wallet,
queue and evidence rules apply as for a single `POST /v1/calls`.

Use a campaign when your list is ready to be called and you want Skysay to
own the scheduling. Use a [customer application](/docs/customer-applications)
when your own system owns the cadence and must decide each call.

## Who you may call

Campaigns are self-serve for two consent bases, declared per campaign:

| `consent_basis` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `existing_customer` | People with an active relationship with your business |
| `explicit_consent` | People who agreed in writing to be called by you |

Cold sales, telemarketing, lead generation and purchased, scraped or listed
contacts stay blocked platform-wide; a campaign with such a purpose is refused
when it is created and again at launch. Campaigns are open to every active
workspace -- there is no per-profile campaign switch -- and launch runs one
authorization against your workspace capabilities: a sanctioned destination
blocks it, a suspended or not-yet-active workspace or a record without voice
holds it, a destination outside your allowlist holds it for the usual
new-country review, a missing consent basis blocks it, and on the custom
implementation-services profile every launch goes to an operator
(`needs_review.custom_always_review`). The full order and reason codes are in
[Policy & outbound](/docs/policy-and-outbound#campaigns). Your caps (calls per
day, per minute, unique destinations, spend) are enforced per call while the
campaign runs: the wizard's review step shows each check for your draft and
how many days the list takes under the tighter of your daily call cap and
your unique-destinations cap -- a 5,000-contact list runs for 25 days under a
200-per-day cap, 34 under the default profile's 150 unique destinations per
day.

## Contact lists

Create a list and import rows in one request. A row needs `phone_e164`; every
other field is optional.

The workspace wizard builds the same request from a **CSV or an `.xlsx`
workbook**: it reads the file in your browser — the first sheet, the first row
as column names — maps the columns, shows you every row it could not use, and
posts the rows this endpoint takes. The API itself never parses a spreadsheet,
so a workbook and its CSV export produce the same list and the same per-row
report. Date and number cells arrive as the text the sheet displays, not as
Excel serial numbers; keep an account reference or a phone number in a
**text-formatted column**, because a spreadsheet stores a number as a
15-significant-digit float and a longer one has already lost digits before any
export.

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/contact-lists \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "project_id": "proj_123",
    "name": "September renewals",
    "contacts": [
      {"phone_e164": "+15550100", "name": "Ama", "external_reference": "crm-4812",
       "variables": {"plan": "gold", "renewal_date": "2026-09-30"}}
    ]
  }'
```

- `time_zone` defaults from the number's country (`+233…` becomes
  `Africa/Accra`); set it per row when you know better.
- `first_name` is the contact's own first name: at most 80 characters, no
  `{` or `}`, and an empty value clears it. It is stored and returned with the
  contact, separately from `name` and from any custom variable you also call
  `first_name`. On a call the agent reads it as the built-in call variable
  `contact.first_name`. In the workspace wizard a `first_name` (or
  `given_name`) column maps to it, and a sheet whose only name column is a
  first name fills both.
- `variables` are at most 32 keys and 4 KB, never secrets: a row that would
  not fit is rejected at import, never at dial time. They are the call's
  per-contact data. The campaign, its run, the attempt number and the
  contact's name reach the call as built-in call variables (`campaign.id`,
  `campaign.run_id`, `campaign.attempt`, `contact.name`), not as keys among
  yours, so no variable name is reserved.
- A variable is a string or a number, or a **list of flat objects** of them:
  one debtor, several loans. A list holds at most 16 items of at most 16 keys
  each; item keys are letters, digits and underscores (a workflow names them
  in placeholders), values are strings or numbers under 512 characters,
  `true`/`false` is refused, and the 4 KB budget still applies to the whole
  row. A list is spoken only through a workflow section — see
  [Several obligations on one account](/docs/agent-workflows#several-obligations-on-one-account).

  ```json
  {"phone_e164": "+12025550100", "name": "Nimal Perera", "language": "si",
   "variables": {"obligations": [
     {"reference_id": "LN-1", "overdue_amount": "45300.00", "currency": "LKR", "due_date": "2026-08-01"},
     {"reference_id": "LN-2", "overdue_amount": "1200.00", "currency": "LKR", "due_date": "2026-08-28"}
   ]}}
  ```

  In the workspace wizard, turn on **Several rows per phone number** when the
  sheet has one row per loan: rows sharing a number become one contact, the
  first row supplies the contact fields, and every row's other columns become
  one item of that contact's `obligations` list. Grouped rows are reported as
  joined, not as duplicates; a seventeenth row for one number is flagged, never
  dropped. Through the API, aggregate before you send: a second row with the
  same number in one request is still `skipped_duplicates`.
- `source` records where the rows came from and is returned on the list:
  `api`, `csv`, `xlsx`, or `mcp`. It is a label, not a parser — send whichever
  one is true of your own import. It defaults to `api` on this endpoint and to
  `mcp` through the MCP tool.
- A row's `country` is derived from its number; a declared value only fills in
  for a number no calling code attributes, so a mislabelled row cannot steer
  the launch-time country check.
- The response carries a per-row report: `accepted`, `rejected`,
  `skipped_duplicates` and `rejections[]` with the row number and reason.
  Re-uploading the same numbers is idempotent. At most 10,000 rows per request.

Append later with `POST /v1/contact-lists/{contact_list_id}/contacts`. The
workspace [Contacts](/docs/contacts) page shows every list, lets you edit and delete
single contacts, and exports a list as CSV.

### Languages

Each contact's `language` is the language its calls are made in, and the
campaign's `language` is the default for contacts without one.

On an agent whose engine picks its voice and transcription per language, the
agent must publish that language ([multilingual agents](/docs/multilingual-outbound)):
every attempt is admitted against the agent's published profiles and dials
with that language's voice stack, so one campaign can carry `si`, `ta` and
`en` contacts on one multilingual agent. A contact whose language the agent
does not publish is never dialed; it ends `pre_dial_failed` with
`skip_reason` `language_not_published`, and a contact with no language on a
multilingual agent with no campaign default ends `language_required`. Both
appear in the contact page and the export.

A voice-to-voice engine has no language setting to publish: one voice speaks
every language, so there is no per-language profile for a contact to be
matched against. A contact language that Skysay supports is accepted as given
and the call opens in it, with no publishing step first. A language Skysay
does not support still ends `pre_dial_failed` with `language_not_published`.

Two things a language does not change: a conversation workflow speaks the
text its author wrote, word for word, in whichever language it was written —
a workflow campaign that must *sound* different per language uses one agent
(and one campaign) per language; and the calling window is the contact's
time zone, not its language.

## Do-not-call register

`POST /v1/do-not-call` with `phone_numbers[]` adds numbers to the organization
register. It is honoured on every outbound path, campaigns and the direct calls
API alike, and by every campaign at launch (those contacts are marked and never
dialed). Only you add numbers. A hosted agent's `do_not_call` outcome (from the
`set_call_disposition` tool), and a workflow `cease_contact` interrupt when it
is the last request the caller made, record that the caller asked not to be
contacted and finish that contact for the campaign, but they never add the
number to the register: a caller can say anything, so review those calls and
add the numbers you decide to. A call says so in its
[`workflow`](/docs/read-call-evidence#where-a-calls-workflow-ended):
`"cease_contact"` is in `interrupts`, even when a later request followed. `GET /v1/do-not-call` lists the register;
`DELETE /v1/do-not-call/{phone_number}` removes one entry. The
[Contacts](/docs/contacts#do-not-call) page manages the same register.

## Create and launch

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/campaigns \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "project_id": "proj_123",
    "name": "September renewals",
    "agent_id": "agent_123",
    "from_number_ids": ["num_123", "num_456"],
    "contact_list_id": "clist_123",
    "purpose": "customer_outreach",
    "consent_basis": "existing_customer",
    "time_zone_mode": "contact",
    "time_zone": "Africa/Accra",
    "calling_windows": {"monday": [{"start": "08:00", "end": "19:00"}], "tuesday": [{"start": "08:00", "end": "19:00"}]},
    "retry_policy_preset": "sales",
    "max_concurrency": 2,
    "reserved_concurrency": 1,
    "scheduled_start_at": "2026-09-08T08:00:00Z"
  }'
```

Then `POST /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/launch`. The response carries
`authorization.decision` (`allowed`, `needs_review` or `blocked`) and
`reason_code`, plus the run and its counters. After launch the audience and
authorization are fixed; run settings can still change while the campaign is
paused (see [Edit a campaign](#edit-a-campaign)), and
`POST /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/duplicate` copies it into a new draft for a
new audience. `pause`, `resume` and `cancel` control the run; cancel also
cancels the campaign's own attempts that are still queued or held by the
live-call gate (a direct
`POST /v1/calls` request that reuses the campaign id as its
`external_group_reference` is not touched).

### Test one contact, then pause

You can launch a campaign so that it calls exactly one contact first and then
stops for you to check the result before anyone else is called. In the launch
review choose **Test one contact, then pause**, pick a contact from the list
(ideally a number you control), and confirm that this places one real call.
Through the API:

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/launch \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_TOKEN" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"test_contact_id": "contact_123"}'
```

The run's `test.state` is `testing`. Only that contact can be called, until its
call has an outcome, and never alongside another call of the campaign. As soon
as it has an outcome, or can no longer be called (a do-not-call entry, no
calling window, a refusal before dialling), the campaign pauses with
`test.state` `awaiting_review`. A deferral the campaign caused itself (you
paused it, the calling window closed) is not an outcome, and the test contact
may be called again. There is no automatic retry and no next contact in test
mode.

`GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}` then carries `test_review`: the contact
(masked), the call's status, termination cause and
[disposition](/docs/post-call-results#what-happened-on-the-call), whether a
recording, transcript and post-call result exist, the workflow outcome, and the
billing state. A completed call is not a passed test on its own: listen to it.

`POST /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/continue` returns the campaign to normal
calling. It is refused with `409` and `reason: "test_not_ready"` while the test
call is still live or its charge is not settled or released
(`continue_blockers` says which), and repeating it is harmless. `resume` cannot
skip the review (`409`, `test_review_required`). Continuing never calls the
test contact again at once: a retry its policy scheduled waits for its own time.
Pause and cancel work as usual. A test shows how one call went; it does not
promise how the rest will go. MCP: `launch_campaign` takes `test_contact_id`,
and `continue_campaign` continues.

### Check with your system before each call

A contact list is a snapshot: by the time a contact is due, an invoice may be
paid, an appointment cancelled, consent withdrawn or a lead handled elsewhere.
A campaign can ask your own system, just before each call, whether that call is
still appropriate. Set `eligibility_check` on the campaign to a read action of
one of your project's [Integrations](/docs/integrations):

```json
"eligibility_check": {"tool_id": "itool_123", "include_phone_number": false}
```

The action must be active, read-only and need no confirmation, and its
`output_schema` must declare `decision` (or declare no properties). Without
`eligibility_check` nothing changes. The setting works the same through
`POST`/`PATCH /v1/campaigns`, the MCP `create_campaign` and `update_campaign`
tools and the campaign wizard; a scheduled or paused campaign can change or
remove it, and contacts waiting on the old check are asked again on the next
dispatch. Before each attempt Skysay sends the
action's endpoint a signed request (the same signing, network protections and
timeout as every Integration action):

```json
{
  "type": "skysay.campaign_eligibility_check",
  "request_id": "elig_…",
  "campaign_id": "camp_…",
  "run_id": "crun_…",
  "campaign_contact_id": "cc_…",
  "contact_id": "contact_…",
  "external_reference": "crm-123",
  "attempt_no": 1
}
```

`request_id` identifies one question: a repeated delivery of that question
carries the same id, and every new question about the contact (a later check,
a retry after a failure, the next attempt) carries a new one. `phone_e164` is included only with `include_phone_number: true`;
contact variables are never sent. Answer with:

```json
{"decision": "eligible" | "skip" | "defer", "reason": "invoice_paid", "recheck_at": "2026-10-11T09:00:00Z"}
```

- `eligible`: the call goes ahead, if the answer is at most a minute old when
  it is placed. Your answer never overrides Skysay's own checks: the
  do-not-call register, calling windows, capacity and policy are checked again
  and still win.
- `skip`: the contact is skipped (`skip_reason: eligibility_skip`) and your
  `reason` is recorded. Nothing is dialled or charged.
- `defer`: asked again at `recheck_at` (one hour when absent; between five
  minutes and seven days, never after the campaign ends). After ten deferrals the
  contact is skipped (`eligibility_defer_limit`).

`reason` is optional, lower-case letters, digits and underscores, at most 64
characters. No valid answer (a timeout, an error, an unreachable or blocked
endpoint, any other response shape) is never treated as eligible: the contact
is asked again after 5, 15, 45 minutes and so on (at most six hours), and after
five failures in a row it ends as `pre_dial_failed` with `skip_reason`
`eligibility_unavailable`, so you can see it. Asking is not an attempt: a skip,
a deferral or a failure spends none of the contact's attempts.

Each contact shows its latest `eligibility_decision`, `eligibility_reason` and
`eligibility_checked_at`, and the campaign page counts the contacts the check
skipped, deferred, is retrying and ended as unavailable. The check is a
customer-policy hook for campaign calls; it does not cover direct
`POST /v1/calls` requests.

### Calling hours

`calling_windows` is `{weekday: [{start, end}]}` in `HH:MM`, at most four
windows a day, never spanning midnight. With `time_zone_mode: "contact"` each
contact's own zone applies and `time_zone` is the fallback; with `"fixed"` the
one zone applies to everyone. A contact outside its window is deferred to the
next open window, never dropped. Defaults follow the destination country
(Ghana: 08:00–19:00 Monday to Saturday).

### Retries

`retry_policy` maps a disposition to `{retry, backoff_seconds[], max_attempts}`,
where `max_attempts` counts the first dial and the last backoff entry repeats.
Only attempts that reached the person count: an attempt refused before the line
rang because the campaign was paused, the contact's calling window had closed
since it was claimed, or the number joined your do-not-call register is
deferred (or, for the register, closed as `do_not_call`) and spends none of
the contact's attempts. So is an attempt whose call was never dialled because
every agent line stayed busy (its call ends `outbound_capacity_unavailable`):
the contact is `scheduled` again a few minutes later, with `skip_reason`
`outbound_capacity_unavailable`.
Presets: `sales` (no answer ×4 over three days, busy ×3, voicemail ×2),
`collections` (more persistent on no answer), `single_attempt`. A completed
conversation, a wrong party, a refusal, an invalid number or a do-not-call
request is never redialed by any policy; a requested callback dials at the
requested time.

### Concurrency

`max_concurrency` caps the campaign's simultaneous calls and never exceeds your
plan's concurrent-call allowance; `reserved_concurrency` keeps lines free for
inbound and other calls.

## Dispositions

Every attempt ends in exactly one disposition, derived from the call's
structured termination cause and the agent's own signal:

`connected_completed`, `connected_wrong_party`, `connected_callback_requested`,
`connected_refused`, `no_answer`, `busy`, `voicemail`, `invalid_number`,
`carrier_rejected`, `do_not_call`, `blocked`, `cancelled`, `failed_technical`.
This is the same [disposition every call carries](/docs/post-call-results#what-happened-on-the-call).
`blocked` means Skysay refused the call before the line rang for a reason that
is not the campaign's own (for example a destination your capabilities do not
allow); it closes the contact and is never redialed.

A hosted agent declares the connected outcomes with the `set_call_disposition`
builtin tool (a callback requires `callback_at`). Telephony outcomes come from
the SIP result and are never model-supplied.

A [conversation workflow](/docs/agent-workflows) also decides a disposition,
from the last global interrupt it took. When that is `cease_contact`, the
attempt is `do_not_call` even if the line then failed or the agent declared
another outcome: the contact is closed and not dialed again by this campaign,
and nothing is added to the register. When it
is `wrong_party` or `callback`, the attempt is `connected_wrong_party` or
`connected_callback_requested` unless the agent declared an outcome. A
workflow callback carries no time, so the contact is finished with that
disposition rather than dialed again; only an agent's declared callback with
`callback_at` schedules a call. The last request wins: a caller who asked not
to be contacted and then asked to be called back is recorded as a callback
request, not `do_not_call`. Every interrupt the call took stays on its
[`workflow.interrupts`](/docs/read-call-evidence#where-a-calls-workflow-ended)
if you want a stop request to win. Only interrupts the workflow runner took
count: a route you named like an interrupt decides nothing, and a run is read
only from your own workspace.

## Read progress

- `GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}` returns the campaign, its latest run and
  contact counters (`pending`, `scheduled`, `dispatched`, `completed`,
  `failed`, `skipped`, `pre_dial_failed`, `do_not_call`, `audience`,
  `remaining`).
- `GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/contacts?status=` pages through the run's
  contacts with disposition, attempt count, next attempt and last call id, which
  opens the ordinary [call evidence](/docs/read-call-evidence);
  `GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/results` adds each contact's last-call
  [business outcome](#business-outcomes).
- Webhooks: `campaign.launched`, `campaign.contact.completed`,
  `campaign.completed`. Per-call events are unchanged and carry
  `external_reference` (the contact's reference) and
  `external_group_reference` (the campaign id).

## Edit a campaign

A draft (or a campaign held for review) accepts every setting through
`PATCH /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}`, the same validation as create. After
launch the audience and the authorization are fixed: agent, contact list,
purpose and consent basis cannot change (duplicate the campaign for a new
audience). While a campaign is `scheduled` or `paused` you can still change:

- `name`, `from_number_ids` and `language`;
- `calling_windows`, `time_zone_mode` and `time_zone`. Contacts that were
  waiting for the old window are re-evaluated on the next dispatcher tick;
  retries waiting on a backoff keep their time;
- `retry_policy` or `retry_policy_preset`, `max_concurrency` and
  `reserved_concurrency`;
- `ends_at`, and `scheduled_start_at` while the run has not started.

A draft may also receive a new audience in the same request: send `contacts`
(the same rows as a contact-list import) and an optional `contact_list_name`
and the list is created inside the edit, so a status that locks the audience
refuses before any list exists.

A `running` campaign must be paused first. Every campaign carries
`editable_fields` for its current status, and the PATCH response adds
`changed`, the settings that actually changed. A setting that is locked in the
current status answers `409`, as does a status change that raced the edit
(pause, resume, cancel or launch): reload and try again. In the workspace,
**Edit** on a campaign opens the same wizard with the locked steps disabled;
**Duplicate** opens the copy in the wizard before it is launched.

## Export results

`GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/export` downloads the latest run as a CSV
attachment with a fixed column set per contact: `phone_e164`, `name`,
`external_reference`, `language`, `time_zone`, `status`, `disposition`,
`attempt_count`, `next_attempt_at`, `callback_at`, `skip_reason`,
`last_call_id`, `last_call_status`, `termination_cause`,
`termination_detail` and `updated_at`, then the last call's business outcome
(below). Rows follow the list order and cover
the whole run up to the list cap (50,000 rows); `X-Skysay-Row-Count`
carries the count and `X-Skysay-Truncated` says whether rows beyond the
cap were left out. `phone_e164` is written verbatim: import that column as text in a
spreadsheet so the leading plus survives. The export is bound to the credential's own
workspace (a caller-selected `organization_id` is ignored). Uploaded variables are not echoed back, and free-text cells that
start with a formula character are prefixed so a spreadsheet does not execute
them. The workspace offers the same file as **Export CSV** on the campaign.

### Business outcomes

Every exported row carries what the contact's last call concluded, read from
the call's own evidence:

- `workflow_status`, `workflow_outcome_type` (`review_candidate`,
  `informational` or `terminal_note`), `workflow_outcome` (the outcome step
  the [conversation workflow](/docs/agent-workflows) landed on, for example
  `promise_review`) and `workflow_outcome_label` (that step's label);
- `workflow_interrupt`: the most recent global interrupt the workflow took, if
  it took one (`wrong_party`, `cease_contact`, `dispute`, `hardship`, `callback`,
  `human_request`, `language_change`). An empty cell with an empty
  `workflow_status` means the call ran no workflow, not that the caller made
  no request; with a status, it means the workflow took no interrupt. For
  every interrupt a call took, read its
  [`workflow.interrupts`](/docs/read-call-evidence#where-a-calls-workflow-ended);
- `extraction_status` (empty when no extraction was requested for the call;
  otherwise `pending`, `running`, `ready` or `failed`),
  `extraction_schema_version` (the schema version the row's cells were
  extracted under; the column layout follows the AI agent's current schema)
  and one `extraction.<field>` column per field of the AI agent's
  [post-call extraction](/docs/post-call-results#optional-post-call-extraction)
  schema, filled only when the status is `ready`. Extraction is advisory
  model output; an empty cell means the field was not extracted, never that
  it was extracted as empty;
- `recording_available` and `transcript_available` (`true`/`false`): whether
  the call's recording and transcript exist, which
  `GET /v1/calls/{call_id}/recording` and the [call evidence](/docs/read-call-evidence)
  serve.

A contact that was never dialed carries the empty outcome. Booleans are
written as `true`/`false`; the outcome label and every extraction cell are
formula-guarded like the other free text.

`GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/results?status=&limit=&offset=` is the same
information as JSON: the contact page of
`GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/contacts` with an `outcome` object per
contact (the fields above, `extraction` as an object,
`extraction_schema_version`) and `extraction_fields`, the AI agent's current
field list. Bounded and paged like the contact page (`has_more`).

## Campaign reports

A report answers questions such as "in the last seven days, how many people
promised to pay, could not be identified, or asked for a callback?" for one
campaign, across its runs. The workspace shows it on the campaign's
**Results** page; the API and MCP read the same report.

- `GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/report` returns complete counts for the
  filtered rows (total, per outcome, per disposition and per analysis state)
  and one page of rows (`limit` up to 200, `has_more`, `next_cursor`; pass the
  cursor back with the same filters).
- `GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/report/export` returns every matching row as
  CSV, with the schema and analysis version of each row; very large exports
  are capped and say so.

Filters combine with AND; the values inside one filter combine with OR, and an
empty filter means everything:

- `run_id` (one run; all runs when absent).
- `from` and `to`: local dates (`YYYY-MM-DD`, inclusive) in `time_zone`, which
  defaults to the campaign's time zone, so a day is the campaign's day, also
  on a daylight-saving change. At most 366 days.
- `mode`: `attempts` counts every call attempt, retries included; `contacts`
  first picks the rows in the date range and run, keeps each contact's latest
  attempt, and only then applies the other filters.
- `outcome` (repeatable): the business outcome your result profile names, or
  `unknown` when the call did not say or has no ready analysis.
- `disposition` (repeatable): how the call ended, in the
  [disposition vocabulary](/docs/post-call-results#what-happened-on-the-call),
  or `unsettled`.
- `analysis` (repeatable): `ready`, `pending`, `failed`, `not_requested`.
- `review` (repeatable): `flagged`, `open`, `resolved`.
- `q`: a contact name, external reference or the end of a phone number.

Each row carries the contact, attempt, run, call, disposition, the typed
[post-call result](/docs/post-call-results#optional-post-call-extraction)
values when the analysis is ready (never a default `false` for a field the
call did not answer), whether a recording and a transcript exist, and the
review state. An outcome is what the caller said, not a confirmed effect: a
caller saying "already paid" is not a payment.

### Result profiles

A result profile tells the report how to read one AI agent's post-call
results: which text field holds the business outcome and how to label its
values, which outcomes count as positive, and optionally which yes/no field
says the person was identified, which date field holds the follow-up date, and
which yes/no field asks for a person's review. Profiles are configuration only
and reusable across that AI agent's campaigns; the mapping is checked against
the AI agent's published results when you save it. Built-in starting points
cover collections, sales qualification and appointment reminders.

`GET`/`POST /v1/campaign-result-profiles`,
`GET`/`PATCH`/`DELETE /v1/campaign-result-profiles/{profile_id}` (a profile a
campaign still uses cannot be deleted), and `result_profile_id` on a campaign,
which can be set in any status.

### Report-only access

The scope `campaign_reports:read` reads reports, exports and result profiles
and nothing else. Give it to an API key for a reporting tool or a customer's
system: it cannot launch or edit a campaign, resolve reviews, or read
recordings and transcripts (those need `recordings:read`).

## Review inbox

A call joins the review inbox when its workflow ended on a `review_candidate`
outcome, or when its ready post-call result sets the review field of the
campaign's [result profile](#result-profiles); each attempt appears once and
its `sources` say why. A conversation workflow ends most calls that need a person on a
`review_candidate` outcome: a promise to pay, a payment already made, a
dispute, hardship, a callback request, a language request, a wrong party.
The review inbox lists those attempts for a campaign's latest run with the
call's evidence inline, so a reviewer can decide and record what they did.

- `GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/reviews?state=open|resolved|all&outcome=&language=&limit=&offset=`
  pages through the candidates, newest first. Each item carries the attempt
  (`attempt_id`, `attempt_no`, `call_id`, `disposition`, `termination_cause`,
  `settled_at`), the contact (`phone_e164`, `name`, `external_reference`,
  `language`, `status`), the call's `outcome` (the business outcome above,
  including the extraction fields and the recording/transcript availability)
  and its `resolution` (`null` while open; `id`, `note`, `resolved_at` and
  `resolved_by` once resolved). `counts` gives the open and resolved totals;
  `outcomes` (outcome step id, label, count) and `languages` are the facets
  over the whole candidate set, whatever the page's filters;
  `extraction_fields` is the field list.
- `POST /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/reviews/{attempt_id}/resolve` with an
  optional `note` (at most 2,000 characters) resolves one candidate. The
  platform records who resolved it (the signed-in user, or the API key) and
  when, writes a `campaign.review.resolved` audit event with the campaign,
  call, workflow run, outcome and note, and returns the item as the inbox
  lists it. A candidate resolved before answers `409` and keeps the first
  reviewer's note; an attempt that is not a review candidate of this
  campaign answers `404`.

  Send `requeue_contact: true` alongside the note when you disagree with the
  outcome and want the contact called again. The commonest case is a wrong
  party: that verdict comes from one reading of one caller reply and it
  closes the contact, so a reviewer who can see the call was misjudged can
  put the contact back in the queue. Nothing is dialed by resolving. A
  `requeue_contact` that is not a boolean is refused with `400`.

  To see how thin a particular wrong-party verdict was before you decide,
  read the call's
  [`call.wrong_party_signals` event](/docs/read-call-evidence#callwrong_party_signals):
  it records which route reached the verdict, the turn it landed on, and how
  many words the caller had actually said.

  The response tells you what happened, because the audit event is not
  something you can read back. Three fields are on every resolve, requeue or
  not:

  - `requeue_requested` — what you asked for.
  - `contact_requeued` — whether the contact was actually put back in the queue.
  - `requeue_refused_reason` — why it was not, or empty.

  Three reasons refuse a requeue. In each the resolution still succeeds — the
  note and the audit event are recorded — and the contact keeps the status
  and the suppression record it had:

  - `do_not_call_register` — the number is on your
    [do-not-call register](#do-not-call-register), or the contact is already
    marked `do_not_call`. A reviewer cannot undo an opt-out.
  - `contact_phone_unknown` — the contact has no readable number, so there is
    no way to establish it has not opted out. A contact with no number cannot
    be dialed either way.
  - `campaign_run_not_dispatchable` — its campaign run can no longer reach the
    dispatcher. A run is dispatchable while it is `running`, `scheduled` or
    `paused`; a completed or cancelled run is not.

  Otherwise the contact becomes due again and the next dispatch decides,
  applying every rule it normally does — so a number added to the register
  *after* the requeue is marked `do_not_call` rather than called, and a
  contact outside its calling window waits for the window. A contact a
  dispatcher has already claimed keeps that claim and is not reset: that
  answers `contact_requeued: false` with an empty `requeue_refused_reason`,
  because nothing refused you — the contact was already back in play.
- `GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/reviews/export?state=resolved|open|all`
  downloads the set as CSV: the contact, the attempt, the business-outcome
  and extraction columns of the results export, then `resolved_at`,
  `resolved_by` and `note`. Bounded and bound to the credential's own
  workspace like the results export.

In the workspace, a campaign shows **Review inbox (N)** with the number of
candidates still open; the inbox page filters by state, outcome and
language, plays the recording, links the transcript, shows the extracted
fields, and resolves each item with a note. The inbox covers the campaign's
latest run.

The same operations are available over MCP as `create_contact_list`,
`add_contacts`, `add_do_not_call`, `create_campaign`, `update_campaign`,
`launch_campaign`, `pause_campaign`, `resume_campaign`, `cancel_campaign`,
`list_campaigns` and `get_campaign`. The CSV exports, the results page and
the review inbox are REST-only.
