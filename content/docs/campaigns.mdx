A campaign is one contact list, one hosted agent, one or more caller-ID
numbers, a set of local calling hours and a per-outcome retry policy. Skysay
places every attempt as an ordinary outbound call, so the same policy, wallet,
queue and evidence rules apply as for a single `POST /v1/calls`.

Use a campaign when your list is ready to be called and you want Skysay to
own the scheduling. Use a [customer application](/docs/customer-applications)
when your own system owns the cadence and must decide each call.

Launch runs one check on your workspace: allowed campaigns run now or are scheduled for their start time, others need review (fix and launch again) or are blocked. A running campaign can be paused to change hours and retries, then resumed; it completes when every contact is done or its end date passes, and can be cancelled while scheduled, running or paused." />

## Who you may call

Campaigns are self-serve for two consent bases, declared per campaign:

| `consent_basis` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `existing_customer` | People with an active relationship with your business |
| `explicit_consent` | People who agreed in writing to be called by you |

A campaign's purpose is a free-form label, shown on the campaign and recorded
on every call it places; no purpose is refused. Campaigns are open to every
workspace -- there is no per-profile campaign switch -- and launch runs one
authorization against your workspace capabilities: a sanctioned destination
blocks it, a suspended workspace blocks it, a record without voice
holds it, a destination outside your allowlist holds it for the usual
new-country review, a missing consent basis blocks it, and on the custom
implementation-services profile every launch goes to an operator
(`needs_review.custom_always_review`). The full order and reason codes are in
[Policy & outbound](/docs/policy-and-outbound#campaigns). Your caps (calls per
day, per minute, unique destinations, spend) are enforced per call while the
campaign runs: the wizard's review step shows each check for your draft and
how many days the list takes under the tighter of your daily call cap and
your unique-destinations cap -- a 5,000-contact list runs for 25 days under a
200-per-day cap, 34 under the default profile's 150 unique destinations per
day.

## Contact lists

Create a list and import rows in one request. A row needs `phone_e164`; every
other field is optional.

The workspace wizard builds the same request from a **CSV or an `.xlsx`
workbook**: it reads the file in your browser — the first sheet, the first row
as column names — maps the columns, shows you every row it could not use, and
posts the rows this endpoint takes. The API itself never parses a spreadsheet,
so a workbook and its CSV export produce the same list and the same per-row
report. Date and number cells arrive as the text the sheet displays, not as
Excel serial numbers; keep an account reference or a phone number in a
**text-formatted column**, because a spreadsheet stores a number as a
15-significant-digit float and a longer one has already lost digits before any
export.

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/contact-lists \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "project_id": "proj_123",
    "name": "September renewals",
    "contacts": [
      {"phone_e164": "+15550100", "name": "Ama", "external_reference": "crm-4812",
       "variables": {"plan": "gold", "renewal_date": "2026-09-30"}}
    ]
  }'
```

- `time_zone` is an IANA time zone such as `America/New_York`. Case does not
  matter, and another spelling of a zone is stored as the zone it names:
  `US/Eastern` and `america/new york` become `America/New_York`, `Europe/Kiev`
  becomes `Europe/Kyiv`, and `EST` becomes `America/Panama`, which is what
  `EST` means in the IANA database (UTC−5 all year, no daylight saving). An
  offset such as `UTC-5` is refused, and the row's reason gives an example.
  Left empty, it comes from the number when the number has only one zone
  (`+233…` becomes `Africa/Accra`); set it per row when you know better. The
  same rules apply to a campaign's `time_zone`.
- `first_name` is the contact's own first name: at most 80 characters, no
  `{` or `}`, and an empty value clears it. It is stored and returned with the
  contact, separately from `name` and from any custom variable you also call
  `first_name`. On a call the agent reads it as the built-in call variable
  `contact.first_name`. In the workspace wizard a `first_name` (or
  `given_name`) column maps to it, and a sheet whose only name column is a
  first name fills both.
- `variables` are at most 32 keys and 4 KB, never secrets: a row that would
  not fit is rejected at import, never at dial time. They are the call's
  per-contact data. The campaign, its run, the attempt number and the
  contact's name reach the call as built-in call variables (`campaign.id`,
  `campaign.run_id`, `campaign.attempt`, `contact.name`), not as keys among
  yours, so no variable name is reserved.
- A variable is a string or a number, or a **list of flat objects** of them:
  one debtor, several loans. A list holds at most 16 items of at most 16 keys
  each; item keys are letters, digits and underscores (a workflow names them
  in placeholders), values are strings or numbers under 512 characters,
  `true`/`false` is refused, and the 4 KB budget still applies to the whole
  row. A list is spoken only through a workflow section — see
  [Several obligations on one account](/docs/agent-workflows#several-obligations-on-one-account).

  ```json
  {"phone_e164": "+12025550100", "name": "Nimal Perera", "language": "si",
   "variables": {"obligations": [
     {"reference_id": "LN-1", "overdue_amount": "45300.00", "currency": "LKR", "due_date": "2026-08-01"},
     {"reference_id": "LN-2", "overdue_amount": "1200.00", "currency": "LKR", "due_date": "2026-08-28"}
   ]}}
  ```

  In the workspace wizard, turn on **Several rows per phone number** when the
  sheet has one row per loan: rows sharing a number become one contact, the
  first row supplies the contact fields, and every row's other columns become
  one item of that contact's `obligations` list. Grouped rows are reported as
  joined, not as duplicates; a seventeenth row for one number is flagged, never
  dropped. Through the API, aggregate before you send: a second row with the
  same number in one request is still `skipped_duplicates`.
- `source` records where the rows came from and is returned on the list:
  `api`, `csv`, `xlsx`, or `mcp`. It is a label, not a parser — send whichever
  one is true of your own import. It defaults to `api` on this endpoint and to
  `mcp` through the MCP tool.
- A row's `country` is derived from its number; a declared value only fills in
  for a number no calling code attributes, so a mislabelled row cannot steer
  the launch-time country check.
- The response carries a per-row report: `accepted`, `rejected`,
  `skipped_duplicates` and `rejections[]` with the row number and reason.
  Re-uploading the same numbers is idempotent. At most 10,000 rows per request.

Append later with `POST /v1/contact-lists/{contact_list_id}/contacts`. The
workspace [Contacts](/docs/contacts) page shows every list, lets you edit and delete
single contacts, and exports a list as CSV.

### Languages

Each contact's `language` is the language its calls are made in, and the
campaign's `language` is the default for contacts without one.

On an agent whose engine picks its voice and transcription per language, the
agent must publish that language ([multilingual agents](/docs/multilingual-outbound)):
every attempt is admitted against the agent's published profiles and dials
with that language's voice stack, so one campaign can carry `si`, `ta` and
`en` contacts on one multilingual agent. A contact whose language the agent
does not publish is never dialed; it ends `pre_dial_failed` with
`skip_reason` `language_not_published`, and a contact with no language on a
multilingual agent with no campaign default ends `language_required`. Both
appear in the contact page and the export.

A voice-to-voice engine has no language setting to publish: one voice speaks
every language, so there is no per-language profile for a contact to be
matched against. A contact language that Skysay supports is accepted as given
and the call opens in it, with no publishing step first. A language Skysay
does not support still ends `pre_dial_failed` with `language_not_published`.

Two things a language does not change: a conversation workflow speaks the
text its author wrote, word for word, in whichever language it was written —
a workflow campaign that must *sound* different per language uses one agent
(and one campaign) per language; and the calling window is the contact's
time zone, not its language.

## Do-not-call register

`POST /v1/do-not-call` with `phone_numbers[]` adds numbers to the organization
register. It is honoured on every outbound path, campaigns and the direct calls
API alike, and by every campaign at launch (those contacts are marked and never
dialed). Only you add numbers. A hosted agent's `do_not_call` outcome (from the
`set_call_disposition` tool), and a workflow `cease_contact` interrupt when it
is the last request the caller made, record that the caller asked not to be
contacted and finish that contact for the campaign, but they never add the
number to the register: a caller can say anything, so review those calls and
add the numbers you decide to. A call says so in its
[`workflow`](/docs/read-call-evidence#where-a-calls-workflow-ended):
`"cease_contact"` is in `interrupts`, even when a later request followed. `GET /v1/do-not-call` lists the register;
`DELETE /v1/do-not-call/{phone_number}` removes one entry. The
[Contacts](/docs/contacts#do-not-call) page manages the same register.

## Create and launch

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/campaigns \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "project_id": "proj_123",
    "name": "September renewals",
    "agent_id": "agent_123",
    "from_number_ids": ["num_123", "num_456"],
    "contact_list_id": "clist_123",
    "purpose": "customer_outreach",
    "consent_basis": "existing_customer",
    "time_zone_mode": "contact",
    "time_zone": "Africa/Accra",
    "calling_windows": {"monday": [{"start": "08:00", "end": "19:00"}], "tuesday": [{"start": "08:00", "end": "19:00"}]},
    "retry_policy_preset": "sales",
    "max_concurrency": 2,
    "reserved_concurrency": 1,
    "scheduled_start_at": "2026-09-08T08:00:00Z"
  }'
```

Then `POST /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/launch`. The response carries
`authorization.decision` (`allowed`, `needs_review` or `blocked`) and
`reason_code`, plus the run and its counters. After launch the audience and
authorization are fixed; run settings can still change while the campaign is
paused (see [Edit a campaign](#edit-a-campaign)), and
`POST /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/duplicate` copies it into a new draft for a
new audience. `pause`, `resume` and `cancel` control the run; cancel also
cancels the campaign's own attempts that are still queued or held by the
live-call gate (a direct
`POST /v1/calls` request that reuses the campaign id as its
`external_group_reference` is not touched).

### Test call on a draft

`POST /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/test-call` with `{"contact_id": "contact_123"}`
places one real, billed call to one contact of a draft's list before you
launch. It uses the campaign's AI agent, the caller ID its first attempt would
use, and that contact's own values, so the call says what the campaign's call
would say.

- It is an ordinary outbound call with every check `POST /v1/calls` makes: the
  do-not-call register (403), your workspace's policy and caps, and the balance.
- The campaign's calling hours are checked first, in the contact's zone. Outside
  them the answer is 409 with `reason: "outside_calling_window"` and
  `next_window_at`.
- The campaign stays a draft. There is no run and nothing is counted, and the
  contact is not marked done, so launching calls them again unless you remove
  them from the list.
- Only a draft can do this (409 `campaign_not_draft`). A draft has one test
  call at a time: while one is unfinished, another, to any contact, is refused
  (409 `test_call_in_progress`).
- The call carries the campaign id as its `external_group_reference`, so the
  **Calls** page's campaign filter finds it.

### Calling hours

`calling_windows` is `{weekday: [{start, end}]}` in `HH:MM`, at most four
windows a day, never spanning midnight. With `time_zone_mode: "contact"` each
contact's own zone applies and `time_zone` is the fallback; with `"fixed"` the
one zone applies to everyone. A contact outside its window is deferred to the
next open window, never dropped. Defaults follow the destination country
when the whole list is in one (Ghana: 08:00–19:00 Monday to Saturday; Sri
Lanka: 09:00–17:00 Monday to Friday and 09:00–13:00 on Saturday); anywhere
else, 09:00–18:00 Monday to Saturday.

### Retries

`retry_policy` maps a disposition to `{retry, backoff_seconds[], max_attempts}`,
where `max_attempts` counts the first dial and the last backoff entry repeats.
Only attempts that reached the person count: an attempt refused before the line
rang because the campaign was paused, the contact's calling window had closed
since it was claimed, or the number joined your do-not-call register is
deferred (or, for the register, closed as `do_not_call`) and spends none of
the contact's attempts. So is an attempt whose call was never dialled because
every agent line stayed busy (its call ends `outbound_capacity_unavailable`):
the contact is `scheduled` again a few minutes later, with `skip_reason`
`outbound_capacity_unavailable`.
Presets: `sales` (no answer ×4 over three days, busy ×3, voicemail ×2),
`collections` (more persistent on no answer), `single_attempt`. A completed
conversation, a wrong party, a refusal, an invalid number or a do-not-call
request is never redialed by any policy; a requested callback dials at the
requested time, up to the campaign's [call-back limit](#call-backs).

Your retry policy decides whether it is called again after the policy's gap (possible for no answer, busy, voicemail, technical failure or carrier rejected); otherwise it ends completed or failed. Waiting while paused, outside hours or for a free line uses no attempt, and a contact on your do-not-call register or in a language the agent can't use is never dialled." />

### Call-backs

A person who asks to be called back is called at the time they named, whatever
the retry policy says. The agent records the request with
`set_call_disposition` (`connected_callback_requested` with `callback_at`) as
soon as the person asks, with no read-back: like a request to speak to someone,
the person's own request is the confirmation. A workflow's Action step that
sets it still confirms it.

On a [conversation workflow](/docs/agent-workflows), taking the workflow's
`callback` global interrupt schedules the call-back at the time the person
named, under the same rules and campaign limits below. The turn that takes the
interrupt reads the time from what the caller said, and the call's event log
records it as [`call.workflow_callback_time`](/docs/read-call-evidence#callworkflow_callback_time),
with the reason when there is no time to schedule. An authored route to the
interrupt's target step schedules nothing: only the interrupt records a
call-back request.

- A `callback_at` without a UTC offset is read in the call's local time zone,
  the one the agent's `call.local_time` is given in (the contact's time zone,
  else the campaign's, else the one for the person's number, else UTC). When
  the call's zone is not known, such a time is refused (`callback_at_offset_required`) and the agent is asked
  to send it with its offset.
- A time more than a minute in the past, or more than 30 days ahead, is
  refused (`callback_at_in_past`, `callback_at_too_far_ahead`) and nothing is
  recorded. The agent is told the current local time so it can ask again. On a
  workflow, the refusal is the event's `reason` and the contact is finished
  with `connected_callback_requested`, as when no time was named.
- A time at or after the campaign's `ends_at` is recorded, but the campaign
  ends first, so it is not placed: the result carries `callback_scheduled`
  `false`, as below.

`max_callbacks` sets how many call-backs one contact may
be granted in a campaign: 0 to 10, default 2. In the workspace it is
**Call-back requests per contact** on the **Schedule & retries** step.

- Only call-backs the campaign actually scheduled count. A retry after a busy
  line or no answer is not a call-back, and asking twice on one call is one
  request.
- One request more than the limit ends that person's calls in the campaign:
  the contact finishes as `completed` with disposition
  `connected_callback_requested` and `skip_reason` `callback_limit_reached`
  (**No call-backs left** on the campaign page). With `max_callbacks` 0, the
  first request does this.
- The AI agent is told in the same turn. On a campaign call the result of
  `set_call_disposition` for `connected_callback_requested` carries
  `callback_scheduled`. When it is `false`, no call-back will be placed and
  `callback_at` is empty, so the agent does not promise one. The field is
  absent on calls outside a campaign.
- You can change `max_callbacks` on a draft and on a scheduled or paused
  campaign. The value in force when a call settles is the one that applies.
  Campaigns created before this setting existed have the default.

#### A call-back a mailbox asked for

Some voicemail greetings say "call me back later". An agent that hears one
may record a call-back as if a person had asked. Skysay checks for this when
the attempt settles. It looks at what the call's voicemail detection heard
from the other end:

- one voicemail greeting that ended in a record beep;
- nothing else before that greeting or after the beep;
- and the whole call heard.

When all of these hold, a `connected_callback_requested` attempt settles as
`voicemail`. This applies whether the agent declared the call-back or a
workflow took its `callback` interrupt. The requested time is dropped. Your
voicemail retry policy applies, and the call-back does not count against
`max_callbacks`. The call keeps its own record of what was said: the agent's
declaration and the workflow's interrupts are unchanged.

Anything a person could have said keeps the call-back. That includes speech
before the greeting, any sound after the beep, or a stretch of the call that
was not heard. For example, Dana's mailbox answers a campaign call: "Hi, it's
Dana, I can't take your call, please call me back later", then the beep. The
agent records a call-back. Nothing is heard after the beep, so the attempt
settles as `voicemail` and your voicemail retry policy decides when Dana is
called again, instead of the campaign calling her mailbox back at the time
the agent recorded. Limits:

- The check needs voicemail detection. An AI agent whose
  [voicemail setting](/docs/voicemail-detection#the-four-settings) is `off`
  hears nothing, so every call-back it records stands. `shadow`, `hang_up`
  and `leave_message` all watch.
- A greeting with no record beep is never treated this way, so a call-back it
  "asked for" stands.
- Speech within about 12 seconds before the beep is taken to be the greeting.
- Faint sound after the beep, even line noise, keeps the call-back. Some
  mailbox call-backs therefore still stand.
- The check reads the voice worker's own end-of-call report. If the attempt
  settled before that report arrived, it keeps its outcome.

The terminal [`call.ended`](/docs/webhooks) event's `details` carries
`recording_evidence: true` on such calls, as described in
[Post-call results](/docs/post-call-results#a-recording-that-asked-for-a-call-back).

### Follow-up calls

A retry or a call-back does not have to start from nothing. Every campaign
call carries the built-in call variable `campaign.call_kind`, which says why
the contact is being called now:

| `campaign.call_kind` | The call is |
| --- | --- |
| `first_contact` | the first call to this contact in the campaign |
| `retry` | another try after a call that did not reach the person |
| `callback` | the call-back the person asked for |
| `reopened` | a call to a contact you put back in the queue from the review inbox |

Set `followup_context` to `summary` and a follow-up call is also told what was
said the last time the campaign reached this person. In the workspace it is
**Tell follow-up calls what was said last time** on the **Schedule & retries** step. The
default is `none`.

| Built-in | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `campaign.previous_summary_status` | whether there is a summary; see below |
| `campaign.previous_summary` | the [call summary](/docs/call-summaries) of that conversation |
| `campaign.previous_follow_up` | the follow-up that summary named, if it named one |

- "Last time" is the last call that reached the person, not the last dial. A
  busy line or an unanswered call after a real conversation does not erase it.
- The call is never held back or refused for a summary.
  `campaign.previous_summary_status` says what the call was given:

  | Status | Meaning |
  | --- | --- |
  | `available` | the summary is in `campaign.previous_summary` |
  | `no_previous_conversation` | the campaign has not reached this person before |
  | `pending` | the summary of the last conversation is still being written |
  | `unavailable` | that conversation has no summary: call summaries are off for the AI agent, the summary failed, or the call left no transcript |
  | `withheld` | this AI agent is not given what was said last time: it enters a value on a phone keypad, or it does not protect the summary itself and the earlier call kept a value back until it had confirmed the person (or entered one on a keypad), or Skysay cannot tell what that call kept back |
  | `omitted_too_large` | the contact's own values already fill the call's data, or a line that says the summary would be too long with it; if the data is still too large without the summary, this status and `campaign.call_kind` are left out too |

- Each text is at most 512 characters; a longer one is cut at a word and ends
  with an ellipsis. For the conversation's own words, see
  [the previous transcript](#the-previous-transcript).
- On a [workflow that protects a value](/docs/call-variables#protected-variables),
  the summary and the follow-up are protected too, because they can repeat
  anything the last call said. The workflow may say them only after its
  confirming step, and after the strongest check any of its protected values
  needs: if one value needs a `knowledge_match`, so does the summary. A
  workflow with a line, or a read step, that could reach them earlier cannot
  be published, and the workflow's greeting and instructions cannot name them.
  Before that step the status already reads `available`; the summary itself
  stays out of the call until the check is passed.
- Your AI agent reads these like any other call variable. To open differently
  on a follow-up call, write both openings into the first message:

  ```text
  {{#campaign.previous_summary}}Hi again, this is Acme calling you back as agreed.{{/campaign.previous_summary}}{{^campaign.previous_summary}}Hi, this is Acme calling about your delivery.{{/campaign.previous_summary}}
  ```

- You can change `followup_context` on a draft and on a scheduled or paused
  campaign. Campaigns created before this setting existed have `none`.

#### The previous transcript

Set `followup_transcript` to `true` and a follow-up call of a free-form AI
agent is also given the transcript of the last conversation with the person.
In the workspace it is **Give follow-up calls the last transcript** on the
schedule step. The default is `false`, and it does not depend on
`followup_context`: a campaign can give the summary, the transcript, both or
neither.

- The transcript is added to the AI agent's instructions after the call's
  variables, as quoted text it is told never to take as instructions;
  Skysay's own tool instructions come after it. Each line is one turn, labelled
  `Agent:` or `Person:`, with the words in the language spoken. It is never
  read as template syntax, so `{{name}}` in what someone said stays as it was
  said.
- It holds at most 4,000 characters. When the conversation was longer, the
  newest turns are kept and the transcript starts with
  `[earlier turns left out]`. When the newest turn alone is longer than that,
  it is cut, at a word where it can be, and ends with an ellipsis.
- The transcript makes the AI agent's instructions longer, so every follow-up
  call that has it uses more input tokens: about 3,800 more per call on the
  Standard engine in our measurement. Reply time did not change measurably.
- "Last time" is the same conversation the summary uses: the last call that
  reached the person.
- The AI agent may repeat details from the last conversation to whoever
  answers the follow-up call, even before they ask: in our test calls it
  named the delivery slot in its first reply. Tell it in its instructions what
  it may bring up on its own. Anything that may only be said once the person
  has been confirmed belongs in a
  [protected variable](/docs/call-variables#protected-variables), which needs a
  workflow agent; a call that kept such a value back gives no transcript to the
  next call (`withheld` below).
- When the call is placed, the campaign records only whether the call may have
  the transcript. The Calls drawer shows it under **Previous transcript
  (eligibility at admission)**:

  | Eligibility | Meaning |
  | --- | --- |
  | `eligible` | the call is given the transcript of that conversation |
  | `no_previous_conversation` | the campaign has not reached this person before |
  | `withheld` | this AI agent enters a value on a phone keypad, or the earlier call kept a value back until it had confirmed the person (or entered one on a keypad) |
  | `not_supported` | the AI agent runs a workflow, or uses Eleven Realtime, whose instructions have a size limit |

- The words are read each time the call's configuration is fetched, never
  copied into the call. A call-back placed before the earlier call's
  transcript was ready gets it if it is ready when the call starts. A
  transcript deleted in the meantime is not given, and a call is never held
  back or refused because of the transcript.
- `GET /v1/calls/{call_id}/realtime-config` includes it only for Skysay's own
  voice worker, the gateway that placed the call, or an API key holding
  `recordings:read`. Any other key gets the same configuration without it. A
  self-hosted worker that reads configurations with an API key needs
  `recordings:read` on that key to give the transcript to its calls.
- You can change `followup_transcript` on a draft and on a scheduled or paused
  campaign. Campaigns created before this setting existed have `false`.

### Concurrency

`max_concurrency` caps the campaign's simultaneous calls and never exceeds your
workspace's concurrent-call limit: your plan's allowance, or the limit Skysay
has set for your workspace when that differs. `reserved_concurrency` keeps lines
free for inbound and other calls and is at most one less than that limit. The
campaign wizard shows the limit that applies.

If the limit is lowered below a campaign's saved `max_concurrency` or
`reserved_concurrency`, the campaign keeps them. Editing its other settings
still works, and a value you change must fit the new limit. The campaign never
starts a call that would take the workspace past the limit. A duplicate fits
the limit in force: the copy's `max_concurrency` is at most the limit and its
`reserved_concurrency` at most one less. Any setting lowered to fit is listed in
the response's `clamped` (empty when the copy matches the original), and the
dashboard says so too.

### Add contacts to a running campaign

A campaign's audience is copied from its contact list when you launch it. To
add people afterwards, enroll them:
`POST /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/contacts` (MCP: `enroll_campaign_contacts`),
with the same rows as a contact-list import, at most 10,000 a request.

```json
{ "contacts": [{ "phone_e164": "+14155550123", "name": "Dana Reyes" }] }
```

- The rows join the campaign's own contact list and its queue in one step.
  They are called inside their calling hours, like the contacts the campaign
  launched with.
- The campaign must be scheduled, running or paused. A draft takes its
  contacts from its list at launch; a completed or cancelled campaign answers
  `409`, so duplicate it to call more people.
- Enrolling is safe to repeat. A contact already in the campaign is not added
  twice, and the response says so in `already_enrolled`. A number already in
  the list but not yet in the campaign is enrolled.
- A number on your do-not-call register is enrolled marked `do_not_call` and
  is never dialled.
- The campaign is checked as at launch with the new contacts in it. If the
  check would not allow them, for example a destination country that needs a
  review, the request answers `409` and nothing is added.
- A campaign does not complete for running out of contacts while an enrolled
  contact is still to be called. It still ends at its end date, and contacts
  not yet called are then skipped.

The response is the list import's report (`accepted`, `rejected`,
`skipped_duplicates`, `rejections`) plus `campaign_id`, `run_id`, `enrolled`,
`already_enrolled` and `do_not_call`.

## Dispositions

Every attempt ends in exactly one disposition, derived from the call's
structured termination cause and the agent's own signal:

`connected_completed`, `connected_wrong_party`, `connected_callback_requested`,
`connected_refused`, `no_answer`, `busy`, `voicemail`, `invalid_number`,
`carrier_rejected`, `do_not_call`, `cancelled`, `failed_technical`.

A hosted agent declares the connected outcomes with the `set_call_disposition`
builtin tool (a callback requires `callback_at`, and on a campaign call the
result says whether the [call-back was scheduled](#call-backs)). Telephony outcomes come from
the SIP result and are never model-supplied.

A [conversation workflow](/docs/agent-workflows) also decides a disposition,
from the last global interrupt it took. When that is `cease_contact`, the
attempt is `do_not_call` even if the line then failed or the agent declared
another outcome: the contact is closed and not dialed again by this campaign,
and nothing is added to the register. When it
is `wrong_party` or `callback`, the attempt is `connected_wrong_party` or
`connected_callback_requested` unless the agent declared an outcome. A
workflow callback is dialed again at the time the person named, as a
[call-back](#call-backs); when no time was named or the time was refused, the
contact is finished with that disposition. The last request wins: a caller who
asked not to be contacted and then asked to be called back is recorded as a
callback request, not `do_not_call`, and called back at the time they named. A
call-back recorded on a call that only reached a voicemail greeting with a beep
settles as `voicemail` (see
[A call-back a mailbox asked for](#a-call-back-a-mailbox-asked-for)). Every interrupt the call took stays on its
[`workflow.interrupts`](/docs/read-call-evidence#where-a-calls-workflow-ended)
if you want a stop request to win. Only interrupts the workflow runner took
count: a route you named like an interrupt decides nothing, and a run is read
only from your own workspace.

## Read progress

- `GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}` returns the campaign, its latest run and
  contact counters (`pending`, `scheduled`, `dispatched`, `completed`,
  `failed`, `skipped`, `pre_dial_failed`, `do_not_call`, `audience`,
  `remaining`).
- `GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/contacts?status=` pages through the run's
  contacts with disposition, attempt count, next attempt and last call id, which
  opens the ordinary [call evidence](/docs/read-call-evidence);
  `GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/results` adds each contact's last-call
  [business outcome](#business-outcomes).
- `GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/contacts/{contact_id}/attempts` returns one
  contact's [history](#contact-history): every attempt in order, its result
  and what the campaign decided next.
- Webhooks: `campaign.launched`, `campaign.contact.completed`,
  `campaign.completed`. Per-call events are unchanged and carry
  `external_reference` (the contact's reference) and
  `external_group_reference` (the campaign id).

### On the campaign page

The workspace campaign page shows the same run. Before launch, a draft counts
the contacts in its list, the number the wizard's review step showed; once
launched it counts the run's contacts.

The contacts table has four tabs, each with its count:

| Tab | Contact statuses |
| --- | --- |
| All | every contact |
| To call | `pending`, `scheduled`, `dispatched` |
| Done | `completed` |
| Not called | `failed`, `skipped`, `pre_dial_failed`, `do_not_call` |

Every row shows its status and, when there is one, the reason
(`skip_reason`), for example that the number is on your do-not-call register,
that no calling hours were left, or that the AI agent does not publish the
contact's language.

The **Result** column shows the AI agent's
[result fields](/docs/post-call-results#optional-post-call-extraction) that
have a closed set of answers, read from the contact's last call: a field with
`enum` choices, or a yes/no (`boolean`) field. Required fields come first, up
to three; the export carries every field. A field the AI agent left empty
shows **not stated**, never "no". A call whose result is still being written
shows **Result pending**, and a call made before a field existed shows a dash.

Click a call outcome or a result answer to show only the contacts with it;
click it again, or the chip above the table, to clear it. Filters combine
with the tab. **Export CSV** then downloads exactly the rows the table shows,
in the same order, and its label gives the count.

### Contact history

Every contact has a history: each attempt the campaign made, in order, with
its result and what the campaign decided next. On the campaign page, click the
number in a contact's **Attempts** column to open it under the row. **Open this
person in Contacts** shows the same number on the
[Contacts](/docs/contacts#all-contacts) page, with its lists and its most
recent calls and SMS conversations.

A history opened from **Contacts**, or from a link someone shared, opens above
the table when the contact's row is not on the page of the table you are
looking at. The page address keeps the open history, so reloading the page or
sharing the link shows it again.

| Column | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Kind | why the contact was called: **First call**, **Retry**, **Call-back**, or **Called again after review** |
| Result | the attempt's [disposition](#dispositions), or a dash when there is none: the call is in progress, or the attempt was postponed |
| Next step | **Retry scheduled for** a time, **Call-back scheduled for** a time, **Postponed to** a time (the attempt was not made, for example because no line was free), or **No further calls**, with the reason when the campaign named one |
| Open | the call's [evidence](/docs/read-call-evidence) |

The API and MCP return the same history:
`GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/contacts/{contact_id}/attempts` and the MCP
tool `list_campaign_contact_attempts`. `contact_id` is the `id` of a row of
`GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/contacts`.

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `attempt_no` | 1 for the first attempt |
| `kind` | `first_contact`, `retry`, `callback` or `reopened` |
| `started_at`, `settled_at` | when the attempt was made and when its result was recorded; `settled_at` is `null` while the call is in progress |
| `disposition`, `termination_cause` | how the attempt ended |
| `call_id` | the call, when one was placed |
| `decision` | `retry`, `callback`, `deferred` or `finished` |
| `next_at` | when the next attempt was scheduled for |
| `finished_reason` | on the attempt that finished the contact, the reason when the campaign named one, for example `callback_limit_reached` |

Attempts are returned oldest first, 50 a page (`limit` up to 200, `offset`,
`has_more`). A value the campaign did not record is `null`: attempts made
before contact history existed have no `kind` and no `decision`, and the
campaign page shows them as not recorded.

## Edit a campaign

A draft (or a campaign held for review) accepts every setting through
`PATCH /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}`, the same validation as create. After
launch the audience and the authorization are fixed: agent, contact list,
purpose and consent basis cannot change (duplicate the campaign for a new
audience). While a campaign is `scheduled` or `paused` you can still change:

- `name`, `from_number_ids` and `language`;
- `calling_windows`, `time_zone_mode` and `time_zone`. Contacts that were
  waiting for the old window are re-evaluated on the next dispatcher tick;
  retries waiting on a backoff keep their time;
- `retry_policy` or `retry_policy_preset`, `max_concurrency`,
  `reserved_concurrency` and `max_callbacks`;
- `ends_at`, and `scheduled_start_at` while the run has not started.

A draft may also receive a new audience in the same request: send `contacts`
(the same rows as a contact-list import) and an optional `contact_list_name`
and the list is created inside the edit, so a status that locks the audience
refuses before any list exists.

A `running` campaign must be paused first. Every campaign carries
`editable_fields` for its current status, and the PATCH response adds
`changed`, the settings that actually changed. A setting that is locked in the
current status answers `409`, as does a status change that raced the edit
(pause, resume, cancel or launch): reload and try again. In the workspace,
**Edit** on a campaign opens the same wizard with the locked steps disabled;
**Duplicate** opens the copy in the wizard before it is launched.

## Delete a campaign

`DELETE /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}` deletes a campaign that can no longer
place calls: a `draft`, `needs_review`, `blocked`, `completed` or `cancelled`
one. It answers `200` with `{"id": "...", "deleted": true}` and the campaign
leaves every campaign listing and read.

- **What stays.** Its calls, recordings, transcripts, billing records and
  call evidence stay in Calls and Billing, and a call it placed still opens.
  Contact lists, contacts and your [do-not-call register](#do-not-call-register)
  are not changed. Calls already in progress finish normally.
- **What goes.** The campaign, its per-contact results and its review inbox
  are no longer shown or returned: `GET`, the contact page, `/results`,
  `/reviews`, `/export`, `PATCH`, `launch` and `duplicate` answer `404`
  afterwards. Export the CSV first if you need it.
- **Opt-outs.** A caller who asked not to be contacted is recorded as a
  `do_not_call` outcome, never added to the register automatically. Check
  those numbers are on your register before you delete the campaign.
- **A campaign that can still dial is refused.** A `launching`, `scheduled`,
  `running` or `paused` campaign answers `409` with
  `"reason": "campaign_active"` and its `status`. Cancel it first
  (`POST /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/cancel`), then delete it.
- **A repeat is a `404`**, the same answer as an id that never existed or
  belongs to another workspace. Scope: `calls:write`.

A deleted campaign's id is never reused as an ordinary call-group label: a
direct call that names it as its `campaign_id` is refused.

In the workspace, **Delete** sits next to **Duplicate** on the campaign. It
lists what stays and asks you to confirm; on a campaign that can still place
calls it explains that you cancel it first.

## Export results

`GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/export` downloads the latest run as a CSV
attachment with a fixed column set per contact: `phone_e164`, `name`,
`external_reference`, `language`, `time_zone`, `status`, `disposition`,
`attempt_count`, `next_attempt_at`, `callback_at`, `skip_reason`,
`last_call_id`, `last_call_status`, `termination_cause`,
`termination_detail` and `updated_at`, then the last call's business outcome
(below). Rows follow the list order and cover
the whole run up to the list cap (50,000 rows); `X-Skysay-Row-Count`
carries the count and `X-Skysay-Truncated` says whether rows beyond the
cap were left out. `phone_e164` is written verbatim: import that column as text in a
spreadsheet so the leading plus survives. The export is bound to the credential's own
workspace (a caller-selected `organization_id` is ignored). Uploaded variables are not echoed back, and free-text cells that
start with a formula character are prefixed so a spreadsheet does not execute
them. The workspace offers the same file as **Export CSV** on the campaign.

The export takes the same filter as the campaign page's contacts table, so a
file holds exactly the rows the table showed, in the same order and under the
same cap. Every parameter is optional and they combine:

- `status` (one contact status) or `group` (`to_call`, `done`,
  `not_called`), not both;
- `disposition`: the contact's latest call outcome;
- `result_field`: one of the AI agent's result fields with a closed set of
  answers (`enum` choices, or a `boolean`), named with ASCII letters, digits
  and underscores. Give it `result_value` (a choice, or `true` / `false`) for
  the contacts whose last call's result is ready and states that answer, or
  `result_not_stated=true` for those whose result is ready and leaves the
  field empty. A field that was not stated never matches `false`.

An unknown value, a field without a closed set of answers, or a
`result_value` outside the field's choices answers `400`.

```bash
curl -G https://api.skysay.ai/v1/campaigns/camp_123/export \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_TOKEN" \
  --data-urlencode "group=done" \
  --data-urlencode "result_field=payment_outcome" \
  --data-urlencode "result_value=promised" \
  -o promised.csv
```

### Business outcomes

Every exported row carries what the contact's last call concluded, read from
the call's own evidence:

- `workflow_status`, `workflow_outcome_type` (`review_candidate`,
  `informational` or `terminal_note`), `workflow_outcome` (the outcome step
  the [conversation workflow](/docs/agent-workflows) landed on, for example
  `promise_review`) and `workflow_outcome_label` (that step's label);
- `workflow_interrupt`: the most recent global interrupt the workflow took, if
  it took one (`wrong_party`, `cease_contact`, `dispute`, `hardship`, `callback`,
  `human_request`, `language_change`). An empty cell with an empty
  `workflow_status` means the call ran no workflow, not that the caller made
  no request; with a status, it means the workflow took no interrupt. For
  every interrupt a call took, read its
  [`workflow.interrupts`](/docs/read-call-evidence#where-a-calls-workflow-ended);
- `extraction_status` (empty when no extraction was requested for the call;
  otherwise `pending`, `running`, `ready` or `failed`),
  `extraction_schema_version` (the schema version the row's cells were
  extracted under; the column layout follows the AI agent's current schema)
  and one `extraction.<field>` column per field of the AI agent's
  [post-call extraction](/docs/post-call-results#optional-post-call-extraction)
  schema, filled only when the status is `ready`. Extraction is advisory
  model output; an empty cell means the field was not extracted, never that
  it was extracted as empty;
- `recording_available` and `transcript_available` (`true`/`false`): whether
  the call's recording and transcript exist, which
  `GET /v1/calls/{call_id}/recording` and the [call evidence](/docs/read-call-evidence)
  serve.

A contact that was never dialed carries the empty outcome. Booleans are
written as `true`/`false`; the outcome label and every extraction cell are
formula-guarded like the other free text.

`GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/results?status=&limit=&offset=` is the same
information as JSON: the contact page of
`GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/contacts` with an `outcome` object per
contact (the fields above, `extraction` as an object,
`extraction_schema_version`) and `extraction_fields`, the AI agent's current
field list. Bounded and paged like the contact page (`has_more`).

## Review inbox

A conversation workflow ends most calls that need a person on a
`review_candidate` outcome: a promise to pay, a payment already made, a
dispute, hardship, a callback request, a language request, a wrong party.
The review inbox lists those attempts for a campaign's latest run with the
call's evidence inline, so a reviewer can decide and record what they did.

- `GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/reviews?state=open|resolved|all&outcome=&language=&limit=&offset=`
  pages through the candidates, newest first. Each item carries the attempt
  (`attempt_id`, `attempt_no`, `call_id`, `disposition`, `termination_cause`,
  `settled_at`), the contact (`phone_e164`, `name`, `external_reference`,
  `language`, `status`), the call's `outcome` (the business outcome above,
  including the extraction fields and the recording/transcript availability)
  and its `resolution` (`null` while open; `id`, `note`, `resolved_at` and
  `resolved_by` once resolved). `counts` gives the open and resolved totals;
  `outcomes` (outcome step id, label, count) and `languages` are the facets
  over the whole candidate set, whatever the page's filters;
  `extraction_fields` is the field list.
- `POST /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/reviews/{attempt_id}/resolve` with an
  optional `note` (at most 2,000 characters) resolves one candidate. The
  platform records who resolved it (the signed-in user, or the API key) and
  when, writes a `campaign.review.resolved` audit event with the campaign,
  call, workflow run, outcome and note, and returns the item as the inbox
  lists it. A candidate resolved before answers `409` and keeps the first
  reviewer's note; an attempt that is not a review candidate of this
  campaign answers `404`.

  Send `requeue_contact: true` alongside the note when you disagree with the
  outcome and want the contact called again. The commonest case is a wrong
  party: that verdict comes from one reading of one caller reply and it
  closes the contact, so a reviewer who can see the call was misjudged can
  put the contact back in the queue. Nothing is dialed by resolving. A
  `requeue_contact` that is not a boolean is refused with `400`.

  To see how thin a particular wrong-party verdict was before you decide,
  read the call's
  [`call.wrong_party_signals` event](/docs/read-call-evidence#callwrong_party_signals):
  it records which route reached the verdict, the turn it landed on, and how
  many words the caller had actually said.

  The response tells you what happened, because the audit event is not
  something you can read back. Three fields are on every resolve, requeue or
  not:

  - `requeue_requested` — what you asked for.
  - `contact_requeued` — whether the contact was actually put back in the queue.
  - `requeue_refused_reason` — why it was not, or empty.

  Three reasons refuse a requeue. In each the resolution still succeeds — the
  note and the audit event are recorded — and the contact keeps the status
  and the suppression record it had:

  - `do_not_call_register` — the number is on your
    [do-not-call register](#do-not-call-register), or the contact is already
    marked `do_not_call`. A reviewer cannot undo an opt-out.
  - `contact_phone_unknown` — the contact has no readable number, so there is
    no way to establish it has not opted out. A contact with no number cannot
    be dialed either way.
  - `campaign_run_not_dispatchable` — its campaign run can no longer reach the
    dispatcher. A run is dispatchable while it is `running`, `scheduled` or
    `paused`; a completed or cancelled run is not.

  Otherwise the contact becomes due again and the next dispatch decides,
  applying every rule it normally does — so a number added to the register
  *after* the requeue is marked `do_not_call` rather than called, and a
  contact outside its calling window waits for the window. A contact a
  dispatcher has already claimed keeps that claim and is not reset: that
  answers `contact_requeued: false` with an empty `requeue_refused_reason`,
  because nothing refused you — the contact was already back in play.
- `GET /v1/campaigns/{campaign_id}/reviews/export?state=resolved|open|all`
  downloads the set as CSV: the contact, the attempt, the business-outcome
  and extraction columns of the results export, then `resolved_at`,
  `resolved_by` and `note`. Bounded and bound to the credential's own
  workspace like the results export.

In the workspace, a campaign shows **Review inbox (N)** with the number of
candidates still open; the inbox page filters by state, outcome and
language, plays the recording, links the transcript, shows the extracted
fields, and resolves each item with a note. The inbox covers the campaign's
latest run.

An item also offers **Add to do-not-call**. It adds that contact's number to
your [do-not-call register](#do-not-call-register), the same as adding it on
the Contacts page, and the item then shows **On do-not-call list**. It is a
person's decision: no outcome, review or call adds a number on its own.
Resolving an item and adding its number are separate steps.

The same operations are available over MCP as `create_contact_list`,
`add_contacts`, `add_do_not_call`, `create_campaign`, `update_campaign`,
`launch_campaign`, `pause_campaign`, `resume_campaign`, `cancel_campaign`, `delete_campaign`,
`list_campaigns` and `get_campaign`, and contact lists are readable with
`list_contact_lists`, `get_contact_list` and `list_contacts` (`calls:read`). The CSV exports, the results page and
the review inbox are REST-only.
