A call ends in one of three ways:

- the other party hangs up;
- the AI agent ends it with the `end_call` tool;
- Skysay ends it under one of its safety rules.

This page covers the second, and the one safety rule that watches for a
conversation that has gone quiet. The complete set of reasons a call can end
with is in [Post-call results](/docs/post-call-results#why-a-call-ended).

<Diagram name="end-call.svg" alt="The agent calls end_call when the caller is done and its goodbye plays (Skysay speaks it on Standard, the model's own on live engines). If the caller does not speak again, the call ends with agent_completed, and after about 15 seconds it ends anyway; if the caller speaks, the hang-up is cancelled and a later ending needs a new end_call." />

One ending comes from a provider rather than a person or a rule: when the
call's transcriber refuses the request it is opened with (most often because it
does not take the call's language), Skysay ends the call with `ended_reason`
`model_session_lost` and `reason_detail` `transcriber_refused_request` instead
of leaving the line open with nobody listening. See [When the transcriber refuses the request](/docs/post-call-results#when-the-transcriber-refuses-the-request).

If the caller leaves while Skysay is still preparing the call, Skysay checks
that the original caller is still connected before starting providers and
reporting media startup. A departure detected at those checks stops setup; it
is not recorded as a completed conversation.
Inbound phone calls also require confirmation that SIP actually answered
before Skysay reports an answered call and starts the conversation model.

## The agent ends the call with `end_call`

**New agents have `end_call`.** A hosted AI agent you create with
**New AI Agent** in **Agents**, or through `POST /v1/agents` without a `tools`
field, gets the built-in `end_call` tool, so it can hang up when the
conversation is over. A `tools` list you send is taken as written: to create
an agent that never ends a call itself, send a list without `end_call` (an
empty list is allowed). On an existing agent, switch **End call** on or off
under **Tools** in the agent editor. Without the tool an agent can say goodbye,
but the line stays open until the other side hangs up or a safety rule ends
the call.

When the conversation is over, the agent calls the built-in `end_call` tool.
Skysay then plays its goodbye and hangs up if the caller does not resume
speaking before the close is committed. When that agent-requested ending
completes, the call reports `ended_reason` `agent_completed`. A safety rule
that takes over first reports its own ending reason instead.

Where the goodbye comes from depends on the conversation engine:

- **Standard** (speech-to-text, model, text-to-speech): the agent passes its
  goodbye as the tool's `message`, and Skysay speaks it in the agent's voice.
  The agent does not say a separate goodbye first.
- **GPT-Live 1 and Gemini Live**: these engines speak for themselves, so there
  is no separate voice for Skysay to speak a text line with. The agent says its
  own short goodbye and then calls `end_call` in the same turn. Skysay lets that
  goodbye finish playing, then hangs up. The tool's `message` is not spoken on
  these engines, and it is not recorded as something the caller heard. On these
  engines `end_call` only ends the call after the agent's goodbye; see
  [On live engines, `end_call` needs the agent's goodbye](#on-live-engines-end_call-needs-the-agents-goodbye).

Either way, the wait for the goodbye is bounded. If it does not finish within
about 15 seconds, the call is ended anyway.

### On live engines, the spoken goodbye ends the call

A live voice model (GPT-Live 1, Gemini Live, or Eleven Realtime when enabled)
says its goodbye in its own voice, and GPT-Live 1 often does not follow it
with `end_call`. So on these engines Skysay does not wait for the tool: when an agent that has `end_call` enabled says its goodbye, Skysay hangs
up for it.

- **After a goodbye, Skysay waits until the line has been quiet for about
  2 seconds**, then ends the call with `ended_reason` `agent_completed`. A
  "bye" said back only restarts that wait. If the other side says something
  that needs an answer, the agent answers and the call stays open.
- **If the agent says goodbye again after the other side has spoken, the call
  ends then**, whatever the other side is playing. This is what stops an agent
  repeating "goodbye" to a voicemail box or a recording that never pauses.
- Any other line from the agent starts the count again.

Skysay judges what the line does, not particular words, so this works in any
language, and a word that can greet or take leave, such as "ciao", is judged
by its place in the call: a greeting at the start is not a goodbye. It applies to incoming, outgoing and
browser test calls. It is paused while the agent is working through a phone
menu or waiting on hold, and it is off for an agent without `end_call`.

You do not need to instruct the agent to call `end_call` after its goodbye on
these engines. On GPT-Live 1, instructions that forbid speaking, or repeat
"call end_call", do not make the ending more reliable. Tell the agent *when* the
conversation is over; the goodbye does the rest.

This is a judgement about one spoken line, so it can be wrong. An agent that
says goodbye while it still expects an answer is disconnected if the other
side takes more than about 2 seconds to start replying, so do not have an
agent end a line with a goodbye unless the conversation is over.

### On live engines, `end_call` needs the agent's goodbye

A live voice model decides for itself when to call `end_call`, and it can call
it at the wrong moment: in testing, a Gemini Live agent answered a caller's
first sentence with "How can I help you today?" and called `end_call` in the
same breath. So on GPT-Live 1, Gemini Live and Eleven Realtime, Skysay checks
the call before it hangs up.

<Diagram name="end-call-goodbye-check.svg" alt="On live engines, when the agent calls end_call Skysay lets the line it is speaking finish and judges whether the agent's latest line was its goodbye. Yes: the hang-up goes ahead and the call ends with agent_completed unless the caller speaks first. No: the call stays open and the agent is told it has not ended. Could not be judged: the hang-up goes ahead as before." />

- **The agent's latest line was its goodbye:** the hang-up goes ahead as
  described above. A goodbye said one turn earlier counts too, so an agent
  that says "goodbye", hears "bye" and then calls `end_call` without another
  word still ends the call.
- **It was anything else, or the agent has not said anything yet:** the call
  stays open. (A first line still being spoken is waited for and judged.) The agent is told the call has not ended and carries
  on. It does not say anything about this to the caller.
- **The caller had just spoken and the agent answered with `end_call` and no
  words:** the call stays open, and the agent is asked to say one short goodbye
  if the caller is finished, or to answer them if they are not. That goodbye
  then ends the call, as in the section above. This happens once for each
  thing the caller says.
- **Skysay could not judge the line** (the judgement was unavailable or took
  longer than 2.5 seconds, the caller cut the line off, or a later stretch of
  the agent's speech produced no transcript): the hang-up goes ahead, as it did
  before this check existed.

What this means for you:

- **An agent on a live engine cannot end a call without saying goodbye.** If
  your prompt tells the agent to end a call silently (for example on reaching a
  voicemail box), have it say a short goodbye first. A refused `end_call` does
  not end the call later on its own.
- **A hang-up can come a moment later than before.** Skysay waits for the
  line the agent is speaking to finish, then for the judgement, which usually
  takes under a second and is never waited for longer than 2.5 seconds.
- **An apology is not a goodbye.** "I apologise for the interruption." does
  not end a call; "I apologise for the interruption. Goodbye." does. On these
  engines the [standing instruction](#the-standing-call-ending-instruction)
  therefore tells an agent that reached the wrong person to apologise, give
  its one short goodbye and then end the call. Standard agents are not
  affected: their goodbye travels with the tool.
- **After a refused `end_call`, the silence recovery described under
  [Speaking again during a goodbye](#speaking-again-during-a-goodbye) applies:**
  if nobody speaks, the agent checks in and the call then ends in the usual way.

Like the spoken-goodbye rule, this is a judgement about one spoken line, in any
language, and it can be wrong in both directions: a goodbye judged as something
else keeps the call open until the agent says goodbye again, and a line that
only sounds like a goodbye lets the hang-up through. It applies to incoming,
outgoing and browser test calls. Standard agents are not affected: there the
goodbye is the tool's `message`, which Skysay speaks itself.

### Speaking again during a goodbye

An agent's pending hang-up can be canceled when Skysay detects the caller
speaking again. On Standard agents, this stops the pending goodbye and keeps
caller input open so the agent can answer the new request. A later legitimate
ending needs a fresh `end_call` request; the canceled one cannot disconnect
the continued conversation. GPT-Live 1 and Gemini Live also protect the
caller's turn during a pending close.

What counts as speaking again depends on the speech-to-text the call uses.

On a Standard phone call whose speech-to-text is Deepgram Nova-3 in English,
with [`min_interruption_words`](/docs/wire-an-agent#interruptions-and-barge-in) at its default or above
`0`, Skysay listens for the caller's **words**. Background noise on the line
that carries no words does not stop the goodbye and does not cancel the
hang-up, so a caller who says goodbye on a noisy line is still disconnected
promptly. While such noise is on the line, Skysay waits at most 1.5 seconds for
words before going ahead: once when the agent asks to end the call, and again
just before it hangs up after the goodbye, so a caller who starts saying
"wait" over the end of the goodbye is still heard. Listening noises such as
"mm-hm" are not words. Intelligible background speech, such as a television or
someone nearby talking, can still be heard as words and keep the call open.

On every other speech-to-text, and on GPT-Live 1 and Gemini Live, this
protection depends on speech detection, not on recognizing a particular
word such as "no"; recognized words can also cancel a Standard agent's close
when the acoustic detector misses them. Missed speech may not cancel a
hang-up, and noise mistaken for speech can cancel it unnecessarily.

When a hang-up is declined because the caller is speaking, the agent does not
say goodbye again on its own: it answers what the caller says next, and can
end the call on a later turn. Even a
spoken response to the goodbye can cancel that ending attempt. On phone calls
and browser tests, the cancelled attempt activates the existing
silence recovery: normally, after 30 seconds of quiet the agent asks whether
the caller is still there, then ends after a further 15 seconds without an
answer. An already-running quiet interval can count toward that wait.
Scripted workflow agents and calls that have used their three check-ins end
after 45 seconds of quiet without another check-in. Each
new caller turn receives a fresh quiet interval, including when a native
engine delivers its caption after its reply. An active tool, transfer or
other protected operation can postpone that check-in. The public homepage
demo has its own session deadline instead of this silence recovery.

Cancellation cannot undo a goodbye already heard or reopen a call
after disconnection. Call-length, balance and operator limits still end the
call even if the caller is speaking.

The agent cannot end a call while a [transfer to a human](/docs/transfer-to-a-human)
is being placed or after it has connected, and once the agent has asked to end
the call it cannot start a transfer. Call-length, balance and operator limits
still apply to both.

## The standing call-ending instruction

Every agent that has `end_call` enabled receives the same instruction from
Skysay, added after your own prompt on every call:

> To end the call, invoke end_call. *(One sentence on where the goodbye goes,
> as described above for your engine.)* An end_call request is a revocable
> proposal, not a completed hang-up. If it is refused or canceled, the call is
> still open: listen and answer any caller continuation, then invoke end_call
> again when the caller needs nothing else. Instructions to end only once mean
> one completed hang-up, not one attempt; they never prohibit retrying a canceled
> request. Do not repeat goodbyes while the caller is still telling you something.
> If you are calling a specific person and whoever answers says they are not
> that person, apologise once, share nothing about the matter, and invoke end_call.
> Apart from the wrong person answering, invoke end_call only when the caller
> has said goodbye or has confirmed they need nothing else; never end it because
> you cannot help with their request, and never while they are still telling
> you something.

On GPT-Live 1, Gemini Live and Eleven Realtime the wrong-person sentence asks
for the goodbye in so many words: "... apologise once, share nothing about the
matter, then give your one short goodbye and invoke end_call." On these engines
the tool only ends the call after the agent's goodbye, and an apology alone is
not one. Standard agents receive the sentence as quoted above.

It is written in English for every language. The agent follows it in whatever
language the conversation is in, and it never becomes part of what the caller
hears.

**It cannot be switched off for an agent that has `end_call`.** Turning
`end_call` off is how you opt out: an agent without the tool receives none of
this instruction. The instruction stays on for an agent with `end_call`
because it is meant to prevent four failures that show up in real calls:

- an agent that said goodbye and then kept the line open for minutes;
- an agent that kept pursuing someone who had said they were the wrong person;
- an agent that answered every follow-up question with another goodbye;
- an agent that hung up on a caller who was still explaining their problem in
  short sentences, often because it had decided it could not help.

So the agent is instructed to end a call only when one of these happens:

- the caller says goodbye;
- the caller confirms they need nothing else;
- the person who answers says they are not the person you are calling.

It is also instructed, when it cannot help with a request, to say so and stay
on the line. This is an instruction to the model, not a guarantee: the model
decides when to invoke `end_call`, and it can still request an ending earlier
than these rules allow. On live engines such a request is carried out only
after the agent's goodbye (see
[above](#on-live-engines-end_call-needs-the-agents-goodbye)). Speech detection protects a pending close, but does
not prove the caller intended to finish. Whatever the
agent does, the call also ends when the caller hangs up or when it reaches its
maximum length.

The wrong-person rule matters most when you call people about their own
accounts. Your own prompt still decides everything else about the
conversation.

The instruction also reaches every language profile a
[language-following](/docs/language-following) agent can switch to. On
GPT-Live 1 it also reaches the backend model that decides when to call tools.

## The tool honesty instruction

Every agent with a prompt of its own also gets a short instruction from Skysay,
added after your prompt on every call and in the builder's **Chat** test. It
names the tools the agent actually has on that call, says that these are the
only actions it can take, and tells it never to say it will check, try or do
something unless it calls the tool for it, nor that an action happened unless
the tool's result says so. The list is the tools the agent was really given on
the call, so a tool that could not be set up for that call is left out. On
GPT-Live 1 the backend model reads the same list, and the list stays the same
when a language-following agent switches language. It cannot be switched off.
On Eleven Realtime, which limits the size of an agent's instructions, it is
left out when adding it would take the instructions over that limit.
It **reduces**, but does not prevent, an agent promising to look something up,
book or transfer when it has no tool to do so: the model can still get it
wrong, and it can still follow a line your prompt scripts, such as "I've
marked this as urgent", when no tool did that. Agents that run a conversation
flow, and SMS conversations, do not get this instruction.

## When both sides go quiet

> **Rolling out.** Ordinary silence remains in observation unless enabled
> for the deployment. A refused or cancelled hang-up on a phone call or
> browser test activates recovery for that call, so declining a hang-up cannot leave a
> quiet line waiting indefinitely. If an operator has explicitly disabled
> silence recovery, it stays disabled; the call-length and balance limits
> remain in force. The public homepage demo uses its own session deadline.

Sometimes the agent speaks, and then nothing more comes from either side. The
caller may have put the phone down, walked away, or lost the connection in a
way the network never reported. Without this rule, such a line stayed open
until the call reached its maximum length. Where recovery is active (see the
note above), it works like this:

- **After 30 seconds of mutual silence, the agent checks in once.** It asks one
  short question, in the language of the conversation, whether the caller is
  still there. On a Standard agent in English it is *"Are you still there?"*.
  On GPT-Live 1 and Gemini Live the agent's own model says it.
- **If the caller answers, the conversation simply continues.** One word is
  enough, and "yes" counts. A later silence gets a new check-in.
- **If nobody answers within 15 seconds after the check-in has played, the
  agent says a short goodbye and the call ends.** It reports `ended_reason`
  `caller_unresponsive`.

Ordinarily the clock runs while the agent had the last word. After a
cancelled hang-up, fresh caller words restart a full quiet interval even if
no further model reply arrives. During continuous conversation those words
keep postponing the check-in. Separately, if the caller spoke last and the
agent owes a reply, the model-response safety rule can end an unresponsive
engine earlier. See
[When the conversation engine is lost](/docs/post-call-results#when-the-conversation-engine-is-lost).

The clock also does not run in these cases:

- while one of the agent's tools is running;
- while a scripted workflow step is on its way;
- while a language switch is being applied;
- after a [transfer to a human](/docs/transfer-to-a-human) has connected the
  caller to your team;
- while the call is already ending.

Line noise without words pauses the clock for at most 20 seconds, so a noisy
line cannot hold a call open for ever. Background sound on its own never counts
as the caller answering.

At most three check-ins are made on one call. After that, a silence still ends
the call only after the same 45 seconds, just without asking first.

**Agents that run a workflow** do not get this check-in. The caller's answer
to it ("yes, I'm here") would otherwise reach the workflow's current step as the
answer to that step's own question. A workflow asks its own question again
instead. See
[When the caller isn't understood](/docs/agent-workflows#when-the-caller-isnt-understood).
A workflow call can also end with `caller_unresponsive`, with `reason_detail`
`not_understood`, when the caller was heard three times in a row after a
question but no words were recognised. A silence after the caller has spoken
does not end a workflow call yet; the call's maximum length still applies.

### Why 30 and 15 seconds

The values come from real calls. Across more than 2,000 silences in the middle
of real conversations on Skysay:

- none lasted 45 seconds;
- one lasted 30 seconds, and the caller spoke again at 34 seconds. They would
  have heard one harmless "are you still there?".

Silent tails that ran on for minutes, or for a quarter of an hour, would each
have ended about 45 seconds into the silence.

These values are the same for every agent and cannot be changed per agent.

## Where you see it

`caller_unresponsive` appears wherever `ended_reason` does:

- on `GET /v1/calls/{call_id}` and in `GET /v1/calls` list results;
- in the terminal `call.completed` webhook;
- in the campaign contact export, as the call's ending.

In the workspace, the call's transcript in **Calls** ends with a **Call ended**
row, and its **Closing details** name this reason as **Caller stopped
responding**.

A campaign contact whose call ended this way is treated like any other
completed call. It is not retried automatically.

Retrying a contact whose call ended this way is your decision, not the
platform's. The precise reason is there for your integration or campaign
settings to act on.
