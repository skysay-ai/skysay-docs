A call ends in one of three ways:

- the other party hangs up;
- the AI agent ends it with the `end_call` tool;
- Skysay ends it under one of its safety rules.

This page covers the second, and the one safety rule that watches for a
conversation that has gone quiet. The complete set of reasons a call can end
with is in [Post-call results](/docs/post-call-results#why-a-call-ended).

If the caller leaves while Skysay is still preparing the call, Skysay checks
that the original caller is still connected before starting providers and
reporting media startup. A departure detected at those checks stops setup; it
is not recorded as a completed conversation.
Inbound phone calls also require confirmation that SIP actually answered
before Skysay reports an answered call and starts the conversation model.

## The agent ends the call with `end_call`

When the conversation is over, the agent calls the built-in `end_call` tool.
Skysay then plays its goodbye and hangs up if the caller does not resume
speaking before the close is committed. When that agent-requested ending
completes, the call reports `ended_reason` `agent_completed`. A safety rule
that takes over first reports its own ending reason instead.

Where the goodbye comes from depends on the conversation engine:

- **Standard** (speech-to-text, model, text-to-speech): the agent passes its
  goodbye as the tool's `message`, and Skysay speaks it in the agent's voice.
  The agent does not say a separate goodbye first.
- **GPT-Live 1 and Gemini Live**: these engines speak for themselves, so there
  is no separate voice for Skysay to speak a text line with. The agent says its
  own short goodbye and then calls `end_call` in the same turn. Skysay lets that
  goodbye finish playing, then hangs up. The tool's `message` is not spoken on
  these engines, and it is not recorded as something the caller heard.

Either way, the wait for the goodbye is bounded. If it does not finish within
about 15 seconds, the call is ended anyway.

### Speaking again during a goodbye

An agent's pending hang-up can be canceled when Skysay detects the caller
speaking again. On Standard agents, this stops the pending goodbye and keeps
caller input open so the agent can answer the new request. A later legitimate
ending needs a fresh `end_call` request; the canceled one cannot disconnect
the continued conversation. GPT-Live 1 and Gemini Live also protect the
caller's turn during a pending close.

This protection depends on speech detection, not on recognizing a particular
word such as "no"; recognized words can also cancel a Standard agent's close
when the acoustic detector misses them. Missed speech may not cancel a
hang-up, and noise mistaken for speech can cancel it unnecessarily. Even a
spoken response to the goodbye can cancel that ending attempt. On workspace
calls, including browser tests, the cancelled attempt activates the existing
silence recovery: after 30 seconds of quiet the agent asks whether the caller
is still there, then ends after a further 15 seconds without an answer. Each
new caller turn receives a fresh quiet interval, including when a native
engine delivers its caption after its reply. An active tool, transfer or
other protected operation can postpone that check-in. The public homepage
demo has its own session deadline instead of this silence recovery. It cannot undo a goodbye already heard or reopen a call
after disconnection. Call-length, balance and operator limits still end the
call even if the caller is speaking.

## The standing call-ending instruction

Every agent that has `end_call` enabled receives the same instruction from
Skysay, added after your own prompt on every call:

> To end the call, invoke end_call. *(One sentence on where the goodbye goes,
> as described above for your engine.)* An end_call request is a revocable
> proposal, not a completed hang-up. If it is refused or canceled, the call is
> still open: listen and answer any caller continuation, then invoke end_call
> again when the caller needs nothing else. Instructions to end only once mean
> one completed hang-up, not one attempt; they never prohibit retrying a canceled
> request. Do not repeat goodbyes while the caller is still telling you something.
> If you are calling a specific person and whoever answers says they are not
> that person, apologise once, share nothing about the matter, and invoke end_call.
> Apart from the wrong person answering, invoke end_call only when the caller
> has said goodbye or has confirmed they need nothing else; never end it because
> you cannot help with their request, and never while they are still telling
> you something.

The wrong-person rule matters most when you call people about their own
accounts. Your own prompt still decides everything else about the
conversation.

The instruction also reaches every language profile a
[language-following](/docs/language-following) agent can switch to. On
GPT-Live 1 it also reaches the backend model that decides when to call tools.

## When both sides go quiet

> **Rolling out.** Ordinary silence remains in observation unless enabled
> for the deployment. A refused or cancelled hang-up on a workspace call
> activates recovery for that call, so declining a hang-up cannot leave a
> quiet line waiting indefinitely. If an operator has explicitly disabled
> silence recovery, it stays disabled; the call-length and balance limits
> remain in force. The public homepage demo uses its own session deadline.

Sometimes the agent speaks, and then nothing more comes from either side. The
caller may have put the phone down, walked away, or lost the connection in a
way the network never reported. Without this rule, such a line stayed open
until the call reached its maximum length.

- **After 30 seconds of mutual silence, the agent checks in once.** It asks one
  short question, in the language of the conversation, whether the caller is
  still there. On a Standard agent in English it is *"Are you still there?"*.
  On GPT-Live 1 and Gemini Live the agent's own model says it.
- **If the caller answers, the conversation simply continues.** One word is
  enough, and "yes" counts. A later silence gets a new check-in.
- **If nobody answers within 15 seconds after the check-in has played, the
  agent says a short goodbye and the call ends.** It reports `ended_reason`
  `caller_unresponsive`.

Ordinarily the clock runs while the agent had the last word. After a
cancelled hang-up, fresh caller words restart a full quiet interval even if
no further model reply arrives. During continuous conversation those words
keep postponing the check-in. Separately, if the caller spoke last and the
agent owes a reply, the model-response safety rule can end an unresponsive
engine earlier. See
[When the conversation engine is lost](/docs/post-call-results#when-the-conversation-engine-is-lost).

The clock also does not run in these cases:

- while one of the agent's tools is running;
- while a scripted workflow step is on its way;
- while a language switch is being applied;
- after a [transfer to a human](/docs/transfer-to-a-human) has connected the
  caller to your team;
- while the call is already ending.

Line noise without words pauses the clock for at most 20 seconds, so a noisy
line cannot hold a call open for ever. Background sound on its own never counts
as the caller answering.

At most three check-ins are made on one call. After that, a silence still ends
the call only after the same 45 seconds, just without asking first.

**Agents that run a workflow** do not get the check-in. The caller's answer to
it ("yes, I'm here") would otherwise reach the workflow's current step as the
answer to that step's own question. For these agents, a silence of 45 seconds
ends the call politely with `caller_unresponsive`.

### Why 30 and 15 seconds

The values come from real calls. Across more than 2,000 silences in the middle
of real conversations on Skysay:

- none lasted 45 seconds;
- one lasted 30 seconds, and the caller spoke again at 34 seconds. They would
  have heard one harmless "are you still there?".

Silent tails that ran on for minutes, or for a quarter of an hour, would each
have ended about 45 seconds into the silence.

These values are the same for every agent and cannot be changed per agent.

## Where you see it

`caller_unresponsive` appears wherever `ended_reason` does:

- on `GET /v1/calls/{call_id}` and in `GET /v1/calls` list results;
- in the terminal `call.completed` webhook;
- in the campaign contact export, as the call's ending.

The workspace Calls page does not show a call's ending reason yet, for any
reason. Until it does, read it from the API or the webhook.

A campaign contact whose call ended this way is treated like any other
completed call. It is not retried automatically.

Retrying a contact whose call ended this way is your decision, not the
platform's. The precise reason is there for your integration or campaign
settings to act on.
