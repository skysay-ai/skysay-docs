title: Bring your own SIP trunk
description: Connect your own carrier's SIP trunk to Skysay for incoming and outgoing calls with your own numbers.

---

Use a SIP connection when your numbers live at a carrier Skysay has no
automatic import for. You keep your carrier account and its bill. Skysay
uses one generic form for every carrier: you copy your carrier's SIP settings
into it, and point your carrier at Skysay.

A SIP trunk carries voice only. Numbers on a SIP connection cannot send or
receive SMS.

This guide covers the API. To set up a trunk in the workspace instead, see
[Set up your own SIP trunk](/docs/sip-trunk-setup).

## What you need from your carrier

- The carrier's SIP host and port, and whether it uses UDP or TCP. TLS and
  SRTP are not supported yet.
- How Skysay's calls authenticate: a SIP username and password (digest), or
  an IP allow-list of Skysay's signalling addresses.
- The public IPv4 networks your carrier sends calls from.
- Your numbers, and the format the carrier sends and expects them in. For a
  Turkish number `+90 XXX XXX XX XX` these are: with a plus
  (`+90XXXXXXXXXX`), without one (`90XXXXXXXXXX`), national (`0XXXXXXXXXX`),
  or with `00` (`0090XXXXXXXXXX`).
- Your carrier must not require SIP registration (REGISTER). Carriers that
  only accept registered trunks are not supported yet.

## Create the connection

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/sip-connections \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "name": "My carrier",
    "country": "US",
    "codecs": ["PCMU", "PCMA"],
    "outbound": {
      "host": "sip.carrier.example",
      "port": 5060,
      "transport": "udp",
      "auth": {"mode": "digest", "username": "your-sip-user", "password": "your-sip-password"},
      "dial_format": "e164",
      "caller_format": "e164"
    },
    "inbound": {
      "source_cidrs": ["203.0.113.0/28"],
      "number_formats": ["e164_plus", "e164"]
    }
  }'
```

- `outbound` is how Skysay places calls through your carrier.
  - `dial_format` is the format of the number being called. `national`
    applies only to numbers in the connection's `country`; a call to another
    country is refused rather than sent in the wrong format.
  - `caller_format` is the format of your number as caller ID.
- `inbound` is how your carrier's calls reach Skysay.
  - `source_cidrs` are your carrier's public IPv4 networks, at most 16, none
    wider than `/16`. Host names, private, documentation and other
    special-purpose ranges (and any network containing one), networks of the
    carriers Skysay itself uses, and malformed entries are refused. The
    example's `203.0.113.0/28` is a documentation range: use your carrier's
    own networks.
  - `number_formats` lists every format your carrier may send the called
    number in.
  - Add `"auth": {"mode": "digest", ...}` when your carrier can send a SIP
    username and password with its calls. Use it when your carrier's
    networks are shared with other customers.
- Either block can be left out for a connection that only receives or only
  places calls.

Passwords are write-only: no response contains them. To change a connection,
send `PATCH /v1/sip-connections/{connection_id}` with the full settings and
the `expected_revision` the last response returned. Leave a password out to
keep the stored one; changing a username needs its password again.

The response's `skysay` block lists what to configure at your carrier: the
Skysay SIP host to send calls to, its port and transports, and the addresses
Skysay's calls come from.

## Point your carrier at Skysay

In your carrier's portal, route each number's incoming calls to the Skysay
SIP host from the `skysay` block, on port 5060. If your carrier uses an IP
allow-list for outgoing calls, add Skysay's `signalling_addresses`.

Skysay admits your carrier's networks automatically once a number is
attached for incoming calls. The connection's `setup.network` reads
`admitted` when every Skysay SIP host has applied them.

Until one of the connection's numbers passes its ownership check, the
networks stay admitted for 24 hours after you last save the connection,
attach a number or check again. After that `setup.network` reads
`not_admitted`; save the connection or check again to admit them for
another 24 hours. A confirmed number keeps them admitted.

## Attach a number

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/sip-connections/$CONNECTION_ID/numbers \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"e164": "+12025550123", "agent_id": "'$AGENT_ID'"}'
```

- `agent_id` is the agent that answers the number's incoming calls: a
  published Skysay-hosted agent, or an agent hosted outside Skysay that takes
  the call's audio over its stream (`external_audio`, see
  [Audio stream protocol](/docs/audio-stream-protocol)). A text agent
  (`external_text`) has no audio path into a call and is refused with
  `sip_connection_needs_hosted_agent`.
- `inbound` and `outbound` default to what the connection carries.
- A number can be attached to one connection in all of Skysay. If it is
  already connected in another Skysay workspace, the request is refused;
  contact support.
- Premium-rate, shared-cost and unrecognised number types cannot be
  attached.
- Skysay charges no monthly fee for the number: your carrier bills it, and
  the carrier part of each call is zero.
  - A call with a Skysay-hosted agent is charged for Skysay's platform and AI
    parts only.
  - A call with an outside audio agent costs a flat **$0.05 per minute**,
    incoming and outgoing, in full minutes. The fee is its own line on the
    call's charges.

## Ownership check

Before Skysay places calls with your number as caller ID, it checks that the
number's incoming calls really reach this connection.

1. When you attach the number, Skysay calls it over the public phone network
   through its own carrier.
2. Your carrier routes the call to Skysay, as configured above. Skysay
   answers it by itself and listens for a one-time code it plays on the
   call. Nobody needs to pick up, and the check usually takes under a
   minute.
3. When the code is heard on this connection, the number's
   `ownership.status` becomes `confirmed` and `outbound_allowed` becomes
   `true`.

Until then, outgoing calls with that caller ID are refused with
`sip_ownership_not_confirmed`. Incoming calls work as soon as the number is
attached.

If the check fails, `ownership.last_check` reads `failed` with a reason:

- `not_received`: the check call did not reach this connection. Make sure
  your carrier sends the number's incoming calls to the Skysay SIP host from
  the networks on the connection.
- `code_not_heard`: the call arrived but the code did not. Make sure your
  carrier passes keypad tones on the call, either as RFC 2833 events or as
  audio tones.
- `connection_paused`: the call arrived while the connection was paused
  for unusual call volume, so it was refused like any call. Nothing needs
  fixing on the carrier side: check again once the connection has resumed.

Fix the route, then check again:

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/sip-connections/$CONNECTION_ID/numbers/$NUMBER_ID/ownership-checks \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY"
```

Checks are rate limited per number and per workspace; a refusal says when
to try again. Changing the connection's source networks or carrier host
means every number needs a new check.

A number attached for outgoing calls only cannot be confirmed by a call,
because its incoming calls go elsewhere. Contact support to confirm it.

During a check's two-minute window, a call to the number from a withheld
caller ID or from Skysay's check number is treated as the check call.

## Readiness checks

Three checks help you get a connection working. Each one reports what it saw
and is independent of the others; none of them is a guarantee that every
call will work.

### Reachability

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/sip-connections/$CONNECTION_ID/checks/reachability \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY"
```

Skysay sends one SIP `OPTIONS` request to your carrier host, from the same
address its calls come from, over the connection's transport. The request
returns `202` and the result appears on the connection as
`checks.reachability` within about half a minute:

- `status: done` with the `sip_status_code` and `reason_phrase` your carrier
  answered. Any answer means the host is reachable. A `401` or `407` is the
  carrier asking for credentials, which is normal. When a `401`, `403`, `404`
  or `407` names registration, `registration_hint` reads `likely`: your
  carrier may expect the trunk to register, which Skysay does not do (ask
  your carrier for IP- or digest-authenticated calls instead).
- `status: failed` with `reason: no_response`: nothing answered within a
  few seconds. Check the host, port and transport, and that your carrier
  accepts traffic from Skysay.
- `status: failed` with `reason: probe_not_run` or `probe_lost`: Skysay
  could not run the check. Try again.

A carrier that does not answer `OPTIONS` can still accept calls: use a test
call to be sure. One check runs at a time per connection, and six an hour.

### Test call

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/sip-connections/$CONNECTION_ID/numbers/$NUMBER_ID/test-calls \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"to": "+12025550199", "agent_id": "'$AGENT_ID'", "dial_format": "e164_plus"}'
```

A test call is an ordinary outgoing call from the number to `to`, answered by
the agent `agent_id`: a Skysay-hosted agent, or an outside audio agent
(`external_audio`), which is billed at the flat $0.05 per minute. A text
agent is refused with `sip_connection_needs_hosted_agent`. A test call is
billed like any call and needs the `calls:write` scope and a confirmed number. `dial_format` is optional: it
tries another dialled-number format for this one call without changing the
connection. One test call runs at a time per number, and three an hour.

Read it with
`GET /v1/sip-connections/{connection_id}/numbers/{number_id}/test-calls/{call_id}`:
`status` is `calling`, then `answered` or `failed`, with the call's
`sip_status_code` when your carrier refused it. Once a test call in another
format is answered, save that format for the connection:

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/sip-connections/$CONNECTION_ID/numbers/$NUMBER_ID/dial-format \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"test_call_id": "'$CALL_ID'"}'
```

### Incoming test

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/sip-connections/$CONNECTION_ID/numbers/$NUMBER_ID/inbound-test \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY"
```

This opens a three-minute window. Call the number from any phone: the next
call that reaches it through this connection is noted, with only the last
four digits of the caller's number (`caller_last4`). The call itself is
handled exactly as it would be without the window.

### Verified

Each attached number shows its latest `tests.outbound` and `tests.inbound`,
and `verified: true` once every direction it carries has passed since the
connection's network last changed: an answered test call for outgoing
calls, a received incoming test for incoming calls. Changing the source
networks or carrier host clears it.

## Limits

- Transports: UDP and TCP. No TLS or SRTP.
- Codecs: PCMA and PCMU.
- Source networks: public IPv4 only, at most 16, none wider than `/16`.
- Ports: 5060, or 1024–65535.
- No custom SIP headers, no P-Asserted-Identity and no username-based From.
- `channel_limit` is recorded and shown; your carrier enforces it. A carrier
  that refuses for capacity answers with busy or unavailable.
- The connection's number of numbers is limited to 200, and a workspace can
  have 20 connections.
- A workspace can save its connections (create or change them) 30 times an
  hour in all.

## Paused connections

A connection whose numbers receive far more calls a minute than a real phone
line would is paused: its calls are refused, it places no new calls, and
`setup.network` reads `paused` with the reason `unusual_call_volume`.
`setup.paused.paused_until` says when it resumes by itself: 10 minutes after
the calls stop, then 30 and 60 minutes if it trips again within a day. More
calls while it is paused extend the pause.

To resume it sooner yourself:

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/sip-connections/$CONNECTION_ID/resume \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY"
```

- A connection can be resumed this way three times in any 24 hours. More are
  refused with `sip_connection_resume_rate_limited` (429, with
  `retry_after_seconds`); a connection that isn't paused is refused with
  `sip_connection_not_paused` (409), and a disabled one with
  `sip_connection_disabled` (409).
- Resuming keeps the connection's trip history. If it trips again within a
  day, it pauses for the next, longer window.
- Contact support if you need it resumed beyond that.

## Refusals

Every refusal names its reason in `error`.

- **Creating or changing a connection** (400):
  - `sip_connection_source_invalid`, `sip_connection_source_too_broad`,
    `sip_connection_source_not_public`, `sip_connection_source_is_carrier`,
    `sip_connection_sources_required`, `sip_connection_sources_too_many`:
    the source networks;
  - `sip_connection_host_invalid`, `sip_connection_host_unresolved`,
    `sip_connection_host_not_public`, `sip_connection_port_invalid`,
    `sip_connection_transport_invalid`: the carrier host;
  - `sip_connection_inbound_formats_invalid`, `sip_connection_dial_format_invalid`,
    `sip_connection_caller_format_invalid`, `sip_connection_codecs_invalid`,
    `sip_connection_country_invalid`, `sip_connection_name_invalid`,
    `sip_connection_channel_limit_invalid`, and the `*_auth_invalid`,
    `*_username_invalid` and `*_password_invalid` codes for each direction.
  - More than 30 saves in an hour across the workspace's connections are
    refused with `sip_connection_save_rate_limited` (429, with
    `retry_after_seconds`). Wait, then save again.
  - A change that would leave an attached number unreachable is refused with
    `sip_connection_number_unreachable_inbound` (400). A stale
    `expected_revision` is refused with `sip_connection_revision_conflict`
    (409).
- **Attaching a number:**
  - `sip_connection_number_invalid`, `sip_connection_number_type_unsupported`
    or `sip_connection_number_country_unsupported` (400): the number itself;
  - `sip_connection_number_unreachable_inbound` (400): no configured called
    number format can reach it;
  - `sip_connection_needs_hosted_agent` (400): connection numbers answer with
    a Skysay-hosted agent or an outside audio agent, never a text agent. A
    number-rules change to a text agent is refused the same way;
  - `number_already_connected` or `sip_connection_alias_conflict` (409):
    another connection or workspace already holds the number or its digits;
  - `sip_connection_number_limit_reached` (409).
- **The ownership check:**
  - `sip_ownership_check_in_progress` (409);
  - `sip_ownership_check_rate_limited` or `sip_ownership_check_busy` (429,
    with `retry_after_seconds`).
- **Readiness checks:**
  - `sip_connection_probe_in_progress` (409), `sip_connection_probe_rate_limited`
    or `sip_connection_probe_busy` (429): the reachability check;
  - `sip_connection_disabled`, `sip_connection_paused` or
    `sip_connection_outbound_not_configured` (409): the connection cannot be
    checked or tested now;
  - `sip_connection_number_has_no_outbound` or
    `sip_connection_number_has_no_inbound` (409): the number does not carry
    that direction;
  - `sip_connection_test_call_in_progress` (409) or
    `sip_connection_test_call_rate_limited` (429): test calls;
  - `sip_connection_test_call_not_answered` or
    `sip_connection_test_call_stale` (409): only a test call answered on the
    connection's current network can save its format;
  - a test call is refused like any call, for example with
    `sip_ownership_not_confirmed` or `sip_connection_needs_hosted_agent`.
- **`setup.reason`, when setup could not finish:** an `sip_connection_*`
  reason, for example `sip_connection_host_not_public` when the carrier host
  stopped resolving to public addresses, or `sip_connection_setup_busy`
  while another setup holds the platform's lock (`setup.state` reads
  `pending`; saving again or "Check again" retries).
- **Calls:**
  - outbound from a number that is not confirmed is refused with
    `sip_ownership_not_confirmed`;
  - any call while the connection is paused is refused with
    `sip_connection_paused`;
  - any call on a connection number with a text agent (`external_text`) is
    refused with `sip_connection_needs_hosted_agent`.

## Detach or disable

`POST /v1/sip-connections/{connection_id}/numbers/{number_id}/detach`
disconnects one number; it stays in your workspace and can be attached
again. `POST /v1/sip-connections/{connection_id}/disable` detaches every
number and removes the connection's call routing. Neither changes anything
at your carrier.

The exact request and response fields are in the
[API reference](/docs/api-reference) under **Numbers**.
