---
title: Bring your own SIP trunk
description: Connect your own carrier's SIP trunk to Skysay for incoming and outgoing calls with your own numbers.
icon: AntennaSignal
---

Use a SIP connection when your numbers live at a carrier Skysay has no
automatic import for. You keep your carrier account and its bill. Skysay
uses one generic form for every carrier: you copy your carrier's SIP settings
into it, and point your carrier at Skysay.

A SIP trunk carries voice only. Numbers on a SIP connection cannot send or
receive SMS.

This guide covers the API. The workspace setup page follows in a later
release.

## What you need from your carrier

- The carrier's SIP host and port, and whether it uses UDP or TCP. TLS and
  SRTP are not supported yet.
- How Skysay's calls authenticate: a SIP username and password (digest), or
  an IP allow-list of Skysay's signalling addresses.
- The public IPv4 networks your carrier sends calls from.
- Your numbers, and the format the carrier sends and expects them in. For a
  Turkish number `+90 XXX XXX XX XX` these are: with a plus
  (`+90XXXXXXXXXX`), without one (`90XXXXXXXXXX`), national (`0XXXXXXXXXX`),
  or with `00` (`0090XXXXXXXXXX`).
- Your carrier must not require SIP registration (REGISTER). Carriers that
  only accept registered trunks are not supported yet.

## Create the connection

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/sip-connections \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "name": "My carrier",
    "country": "US",
    "codecs": ["PCMU", "PCMA"],
    "outbound": {
      "host": "sip.carrier.example",
      "port": 5060,
      "transport": "udp",
      "auth": {"mode": "digest", "username": "your-sip-user", "password": "your-sip-password"},
      "dial_format": "e164",
      "caller_format": "e164"
    },
    "inbound": {
      "source_cidrs": ["203.0.113.0/28"],
      "number_formats": ["e164_plus", "e164"]
    }
  }'
```

- `outbound` is how Skysay places calls through your carrier.
  - `dial_format` is the format of the number being called. `national`
    applies only to numbers in the connection's `country`; a call to another
    country is refused rather than sent in the wrong format.
  - `caller_format` is the format of your number as caller ID.
- `inbound` is how your carrier's calls reach Skysay.
  - `source_cidrs` are your carrier's public IPv4 networks, at most 16, none
    wider than `/16`. Host names, private ranges and malformed entries are
    refused.
  - `number_formats` lists every format your carrier may send the called
    number in.
  - Add `"auth": {"mode": "digest", ...}` when your carrier can send a SIP
    username and password with its calls. Use it when your carrier's
    networks are shared with other customers.
- Either block can be left out for a connection that only receives or only
  places calls.

Passwords are write-only: no response contains them. To change a connection,
send `PATCH /v1/sip-connections/{connection_id}` with the full settings and
the `expected_revision` the last response returned. Leave a password out to
keep the stored one; changing a username needs its password again.

The response's `skysay` block lists what to configure at your carrier: the
Skysay SIP host to send calls to, its port and transports, and the addresses
Skysay's calls come from.

## Point your carrier at Skysay

In your carrier's portal, route each number's incoming calls to the Skysay
SIP host from the `skysay` block, on port 5060. If your carrier uses an IP
allow-list for outgoing calls, add Skysay's `signalling_addresses`.

Skysay admits your carrier's networks automatically once a number is
attached for incoming calls. The connection's `setup.network` reads
`admitted` when every Skysay SIP host has applied them.

Until one of the connection's numbers passes its ownership check, the
networks stay admitted for 24 hours after you last save the connection,
attach a number or check again. After that `setup.network` reads
`not_admitted`; save the connection or check again to admit them for
another 24 hours. A confirmed number keeps them admitted.

## Attach a number

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/sip-connections/$CONNECTION_ID/numbers \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"e164": "+12025550123", "agent_id": "'$AGENT_ID'"}'
```

- `agent_id` is a published agent that answers the number's incoming calls.
- `inbound` and `outbound` default to what the connection carries.
- A number can be attached to one connection in all of Skysay. If it is
  already connected in another Skysay workspace, the request is refused;
  contact support.
- Premium-rate, shared-cost and unrecognised number types cannot be
  attached.
- Skysay charges no monthly fee for the number: your carrier bills it. Calls
  are charged for Skysay's platform and AI components only; the carrier
  component is zero.

## Ownership check

Before Skysay places calls with your number as caller ID, it checks that the
number's incoming calls really reach this connection.

1. When you attach the number, Skysay calls it over the public phone network
   through its own carrier.
2. Your carrier routes the call to Skysay, as configured above. Skysay
   answers it by itself and listens for a one-time code it plays on the
   call. Nobody needs to pick up, and the call lasts a few seconds.
3. When the code is heard on this connection, the number's
   `ownership.status` becomes `confirmed` and `outbound_allowed` becomes
   `true`.

Until then, outgoing calls with that caller ID are refused with
`sip_ownership_not_confirmed`. Incoming calls work as soon as the number is
attached.

If the check fails, `ownership.last_check` reads `failed` with a reason:

- `not_received`: the check call did not reach this connection. Make sure
  your carrier sends the number's incoming calls to the Skysay SIP host from
  the networks on the connection.
- `code_not_heard`: the call arrived but the code did not. Make sure your
  carrier passes keypad tones on the call, either as RFC 2833 events or as
  audio tones.

Fix the route, then check again:

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.skysay.ai/v1/sip-connections/$CONNECTION_ID/numbers/$NUMBER_ID/ownership-checks \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY"
```

Checks are rate limited per number and per workspace; a refusal says when
to try again. Changing the connection's source networks or carrier host
means every number needs a new check.

A number attached for outgoing calls only cannot be confirmed by a call,
because its incoming calls go elsewhere. Contact support to confirm it.

During a check's two-minute window, a call to the number from a withheld
caller ID or from Skysay's check number is treated as the check call.

## Limits

- Transports: UDP and TCP. No TLS or SRTP.
- Codecs: PCMA and PCMU.
- Source networks: public IPv4 only, at most 16, none wider than `/16`.
- Ports: 5060, or 1024–65535.
- No custom SIP headers, no P-Asserted-Identity and no username-based From.
- `channel_limit` is recorded and shown; your carrier enforces it. A carrier
  that refuses for capacity answers with busy or unavailable.
- The connection's number of numbers is limited to 200, and a workspace can
  have 20 connections.

## Paused connections

A connection that sends Skysay far more calls a minute than a real phone line
would is paused: its calls are refused, and `setup.network` reads `paused`
with the reason `unusual_call_volume`. Contact support to resume it.

## Detach or disable

`POST /v1/sip-connections/{connection_id}/numbers/{number_id}/detach`
disconnects one number; it stays in your workspace and can be attached
again. `POST /v1/sip-connections/{connection_id}/disable` detaches every
number and removes the connection's call routing. Neither changes anything
at your carrier.

The exact request and response fields are in the
[API reference](/docs/api-reference) under **Numbers**.
