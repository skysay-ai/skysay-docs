You can use a number you already own at your carrier. Skysay connects to your
SIP trunk, checks the setup, and adds the number to your workspace. Your carrier
keeps carrying the number and billing its monthly fee. Your wallet pays only for
usage and AI.

## What a number can do

Every number, bought or imported, has its own `capabilities`:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `voice_inbound` | The number receives calls. |
| `voice_outbound` | The number places calls. |
| `sms` | The number sends and receives SMS. |
| `declared_by` | Who decided each one: `carrier`, `customer`, or `unknown`. |

Each value is `true`, `false`, or `null` when nobody has said.

Skysay decides each capability in this order:

1. **The carrier's statement for that number.** If your carrier tells us a
   number cannot receive calls, it cannot, and you cannot change it.
2. **Your declaration**, where the carrier says nothing.
3. Otherwise the capability is **unknown**.

A number's capabilities are its own. Your trunk being able to take inbound
calls does not make every number on it inbound-capable.

<Callout title="Unknown is not yes">
  A number takes incoming calls only when it is declared able to receive them.
  An outbound-only number and a number nobody has declared are treated the same
  way: no AI agent can be attached to answer it, its number rules cannot answer
  calls, and an incoming call to it is refused.
</Callout>

## Import a number

In the workspace, open **Numbers**, choose **Import a number**, pick your
carrier and paste its API token. The token is used once to list your numbers
and is never stored. For each number, say whether it **receives and places
calls**, is **outbound only**, or is **inbound only**. Skip the choice only
when your carrier already states it.

Over the API, `POST /v1/byo/sip-trunk/dids` lists the numbers on your carrier
account (`numbers:write`). Each number carries the `capabilities` your carrier
states for it; a missing key means the carrier said nothing. Then import one:

```bash
curl -X POST "$SKYSAY_API_URL/v1/byo/sip-trunk/import" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "organization_id": "org_x",
    "project_id": "proj_x",
    "provider": "didlogic",
    "e164": "+15550100",
    "api_token": "<your carrier API token>",
    "voice_inbound": false,
    "voice_outbound": true
  }'
```

- `provider` is required.
- Send `voice_inbound` unless your carrier states it. An import that nobody
  declares is refused, and nothing is imported.
- `agent_id` attaches an AI agent to answer the number. It is refused on a
  number that does not receive calls.
- Skysay activates the number only after it verifies your trunk. For a number
  that receives calls, that includes checking your carrier sends its calls to
  Skysay. A number your carrier says cannot receive calls skips that check.

## Change it later

If your carrier does not state a capability, change your declaration on the
number's page in the workspace, or with the API:

```bash
curl -X PATCH "$SKYSAY_API_URL/v1/phone-numbers/num_x/capabilities" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"voice_inbound": false}'
```

- A capability your carrier states is locked: changing it returns `409`.
- SMS is your carrier's to state, so `sms` is refused with `400`.
- Declaring a number outbound only switches its incoming rule to `disabled`, so
  it stops advertising an AI agent that could never answer it.

To stop answering calls on a number that *can* receive them, change its
[number rules](/docs/wire-an-agent#3-point-a-number-at-it) instead. That is a
routing choice, not a capability.
