Calendar booking gives a hosted AI agent two permitted tools: check available
times and submit one confirmed appointment. It uses a calendar connection owned
by your workspace and project. The caller cannot change its destination,
credentials or booking rules. Existing [webhook Integrations](/docs/integrations)
continue to work independently.

## Connect and select a destination

Open **Integrations → Calendar booking** and choose the provider. A signed-in
workspace user completes consent in that provider's account. Application setup
must be provisioned securely by the platform operator first; never paste an
application secret into a chat or an issue.

Only configured providers offer a Connect action. If no provider is configured
and your workspace has no calendar connections, the Calendar booking section
is absent. A still-linked account whose provider is unavailable stays visible
with an explanation and **Disconnect**; configuration and attachment are
unavailable until the provider returns. An already disconnected account stays
listed as disconnected, with **Disconnect** unavailable. Setup availability does not certify
that a particular account can complete a booking.

Google sign-in and calendar consent are separate. Signing into Skysay with
Google does not grant access to your calendar; approve that access when you
connect the account here.

### Google data and AI processing

Skysay’s use and transfer of raw, aggregated, or derived user data received
from Google Workspace APIs adheres to the
[Google API Services User Data Policy](https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy),
including the Limited Use requirements.

We use Google Calendar data only to provide the scheduling features you request:
listing calendars, checking availability and creating appointments after the
caller confirms. We do not sell this data or use or transfer it to create,
train, fine-tune or improve foundational or generalized AI or machine-learning
models. Customer permission to train on other content does not override this
restriction for Google data.

Relevant calendar availability and booking results may be processed by the AI
and speech providers configured for the call to generate replies and complete
scheduling.

These Google-data restrictions apply whether you use
Skysay-managed credentials or your own provider account. Provider training and
data-sharing controls depend on the applicable account configuration and terms.
Training exclusion is separate from operational or security retention. See our
[Privacy Policy](https://skysay.ai/privacy) for retention and deletion details.

Choose a writable Google or Outlook calendar, or an individual Calendly/Cal.com
event type. For Google and Outlook, select any other calendars to check for
conflicts. The destination is always checked. A previously selected calendar
missing from the current list must be explicitly deselected, or its account
access restored, before saving. For Calendly and Cal.com, configure
the connected calendars and conflict rules in your scheduling account.

Calendly direct booking uses its paid Scheduling API; unsupported accounts can
refuse booking. Payment, approval, seated and unsupported event types are
rejected. Real-account proof is required separately for each provider; support
for one account is not proof for the other three.

The main settings are the booking destination, appointment length and timezone
(for example `Europe/Tallinn`). Google/Outlook show a summary of your working
hours with an expandable editor. **Advanced** holds buffers, notice periods,
booking horizon, location, invitations and test permissions. Closing Advanced
preserves those settings.

For Calendly/Cal.com, choosing an event type prefills its duration when available;
it must match that event type. New connections use the provider's availability
across the week with no extra notice, within Skysay's 60-day maximum horizon.
Existing custom hours and limits are preserved; additional Skysay hours are
optional under Advanced. Slots must fit within one local calendar day; an appointment may end exactly at midnight when working hours close at 24:00. Optional email invitations require the caller's
confirmed email. Scheduling providers require an email and may apply their own
notification rules even when a Skysay invitation checkbox is off.

Tokens are encrypted and refreshed server-side. A failed or interrupted refresh requires account reconnection because a rotating refresh token might already have been consumed. They are never included in an
AI agent prompt or a workspace response. **Disconnect** erases the locally
stored credentials and invalidates offers. Revoke the application in the
provider account as well if you want to remove its consent grant there.

## Give an AI agent permission

Open the hosted AI agent and choose **Tools → Calendar booking**. The section
is visible before any calendar tools are assigned. Select a configured workspace
calendar. This selects **Check availability** and **Book appointment** together;
an AI agent can use one calendar connection at a time. Account connection and
booking preferences remain in **Integrations** because they can be shared by
several AI agents.

Selections remain local to the editor until its existing save or publish action
succeeds. A successful save can update the AI agent's live configuration. Review
the confirmation dialog before applying changes. **Remove calendar tools** clears
the selection for this AI agent without disconnecting the workspace account.
Other tools are retained.

A partial saved calendar tool set is shown rather than silently repaired. Choose
a configured calendar to complete the pair, or explicitly remove the selection.
A disconnected, unavailable or unlisted saved connection remains visible for
removal. Limited lists do not silently delete saved selections outside the list.

If maintenance blocks a save, the editor keeps your selections and explains that
they were not saved. Try again after maintenance. A tool-list conflict means
another edit was saved since this editor opened: reload and review the latest
configuration before saving again. Neither failure requires new Google consent
merely because the save failed.

Native tools become available once the managed voice workers support both
operations. The AI agent's [What runs on calls](/docs/what-runs-on-calls) shows
where they run, and why not when they do not.

These are agent tools within Skysay's existing execution path; a generic
external MCP client is not required. Their definitions are part of the
[generated API reference](/docs/api-reference).

## During a conversation

1. The AI agent checks a future UTC range against your calendar and rules. It
   receives up to five offers with local time, timezone and a three-minute expiry.
   If the range is in the past or outside your booking window, Skysay refuses it
   and tells the AI agent the current time, so it can work out "tomorrow" or
   "next Tuesday" and ask again. The result also says whether the caller will
   get a confirmation email, and which details a booking needs.
2. The caller chooses a time. The AI agent collects the caller's name, any
   required email and event-type answers. It must not invent an email or fill
   in the owner's.
3. The AI agent asks to book. Skysay does not book yet: it returns the booking
   to read back. The AI agent reads the whole booking back in one sentence —
   what (an appointment and its length), when (date, time and timezone), for
   whom (the caller's name) and where the confirmation goes (the caller's email,
   or that no email is sent) — and asks one yes-or-no question about exactly
   that booking.
4. Only after a clear, explicit yes does the AI agent ask again with the same
   details and the caller's confirmation. An unclear answer gets one
   clarifying question ("Just to be sure, is that a yes?"). Any change to the
   details means a new read-back. Silence, a no or a hang-up never books. Skysay
   refuses a booking request that arrives within a few seconds of its read-back,
   before the read-back could have been spoken and answered.

   A read-back stops counting as soon as the conversation moves on to other
   booking details: when the AI agent checks availability for another time or
   date (even if that check fails or finds nothing free), or asks to book
   different details. A "yes" the caller gives after that may answer the other
   question, so the next booking request — even for the earlier details — is a
   new read-back that needs its own yes. Re-checking exactly the slot that was
   read back does not reset it.
5. Skysay checks the offer again and records an appointment intent before
   contacting the provider. An expired offer, changed configuration, ended call
   or concurrent overlapping claim is refused.
6. The AI agent reports a booking only when the result has `booking_succeeded: true` and `state: booked`. A completed tool request by itself does not mean an appointment exists.

Skysay cannot hear the caller: the read-back step guarantees that a booking is
read back before it is written, and the AI agent judges whether the answer was
a clear yes. Review the call's transcript in **Calls** when you need to see
exactly what was agreed.

The same rules apply to every AI agent and every conversation engine, on phone
calls and in the browser.

Daylight-saving time is handled using UTC instants and the selected IANA
zone. An old offer cannot be moved to another call. A definite conflict before
submission can use a fresh offer; Skysay allows at most two booking attempts per
call. A submitted, successful or uncertain result cannot produce a second
appointment for that call.

Skysay serializes its own overlapping submissions, including duplicate
connections to the same destination. Google Calendar's free/busy check and event
insertion cannot prevent an unrelated external application from writing between
those requests.

## In a workflow

An AI agent with a published [workflow](/docs/agent-workflows) books through
the workflow's own steps: a **Read tool** step that checks availability and
one that books, as in the **Appointment booking** starter template. They use
the calendar the AI agent has, so choose **Enable booking** for it here first;
nothing is attached for you. Without it, those steps take their error routes.
How to build them is in
[Tools on a workflow call](/docs/agent-workflows#tools-on-a-workflow-call).

The read-back rule is the same as above, with the question asked by the
platform rather than the AI agent:

- The booking step's first request books nothing. The call goes to the
  platform's **read-back question**, in fixed wording for the workflow's
  language: the date and time with the time zone, the length, the caller's
  name, and whether and where a confirmation email goes, then "Shall I book
  it?". You cannot edit it. It exists in English, Estonian, German, Spanish,
  French and Russian today; a workflow in another language cannot publish a
  booking step.
- Only an explicit, unconditional yes to that question books, and it books
  exactly what was read back. No, silence, hanging up or an interruption never
  books.
- Any change ("yes, but at three") takes the workflow's change route, and the
  new details need a new read-back and a new yes.
- A hesitant or unclear answer ("I guess", "mm") gets exactly one
  clarification that starts "Just to be sure" and repeats the details. A
  second unclear answer counts as no.
- A yes within a few seconds of the read-back is asked again, not booked.
- The caller can pick only from the times the reply they are answering said
  ("the second one"); otherwise the booking step takes its error route.

The platform works out what the steps need from what the caller said: the
time range to check, the chosen time, and the caller's name and email. The
model that works them out never decides that the caller agreed. The offered
times and the read-back say dates and times in the call's language with the
calendar's time zone. A booking that is refused, or whose result at the
provider is uncertain, takes the step's error route and is never announced as
booked; its state is in **Calls** like any other.

The rule is the same on every engine, GPT-Live included, and the same as for an
AI agent without a workflow: the engine speaks the platform's read-back line,
the caller's explicit answer is taken as a yes, a no, a change or unclear (with
the one clarification), and only a yes books exactly the details read back.
Skysay's own calendar checks, described above, apply unchanged. In a workflow
the caller hears at most three offered times.

A call that holds a protected value runs no calendar step. The **Talk** test in
the workflow builder is a browser test and books only under the test rules
below. The builder's **Chat** test books nothing.

## Read the result

The selected **Calls** detail shows the appointment's interval, timezone and
state:

| Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Booked | The provider confirmed the appointment. |
| Unavailable | Skysay refused before sending a booking request. Request fresh availability. |
| Submitting | The provider may still commit the request. Its interval stays reserved; resolution and retries are refused, even after a timeout. |
| Unconfirmed | A request may have reached the provider, but success is not proven. Check the provider account before doing anything else. |
| Not booked — verified | An authorized workspace user checked the provider account and recorded that no appointment was created. The same call is not retried. |

Skysay never automatically repeats a booking POST after an uncertain response.
Google appointments may recover through a read-only check of their deterministic
event identity and exact interval. Other provider uncertainties remain visible
for account inspection. After verifying the provider account, an authorized workspace user can record the result with `POST /v1/calendars/{connection_id}/appointments/{appointment_id}/resolve`: supply `project_id`, `provider_checked: true`, and either `state: booked` with the verified `provider_id`, or `state: not_created` for a request verified not to have created an appointment. This command is audited and sends no provider request or retry. A not-created resolution does not permit the same call to resubmit another offer. Still-submitting requests are refused even after a timeout; they might commit remotely. A crash during submission can leave the interval reserved pending investigation. Elapsed time alone never proves that no appointment will be created. Identical repeated resolutions return the same state, while contradictory resolutions are refused. Do not tell a caller their appointment is booked while
its state is unconfirmed.

## Test without accidental appointments

Attaching a calendar to an AI agent is what lets its callers book there,
including the anonymous visitors of Skysay's homepage demo, which runs normal
AI agents of the demo workspace with their attached calendars and knowledge
bases. A workspace browser test, the workflow builder's **Talk** test included,
can write only when the owner has selected a
**dedicated test connection** and explicitly enabled **real calendar writes in
browser tests**.
These settings do not themselves authorize someone to run real test bookings;
obtain the account owner's permission first.

Required validation includes consent, token refresh, an authenticated browser
call and a PSTN call with the appointment visible in the real selected calendar
or event type. Keep real-calendar validation separate from mock-provider tests.

The first release supports individual, immediate, unpaid scheduling events with text questions. Calendly multiple, caller-chosen and outbound-phone locations, seated events and approval flows are refused. Cal.com uses its event type’s provider-selected location; validate that contract with the connected account. Reconfigure a connection after changing the provider event type. Cal.com uses a public OAuth client with PKCE; its token exchange stays server-side. Provider reads share a ten-second adapter budget, with five-second HTTP timeouts. Each booking POST has its own five-second timeout. An expired pre-write recheck refuses as unavailable; it does not create an uncertain write. Google read reconciliation handles one stale appointment per minute in the execute-enabled realtime worker; other unknown outcomes need account verification.

Expired unused offers and OAuth attempts older than 24 hours are removed in bounded worker batches of at most 1000 rows per table per minute. Linked offers and all appointment history, including uncertain outcomes, are retained.

Read-only Google reconciliation holds a 60-second fenced lease. Account resolution is refused while that lease is active. A confirmed result and lease release commit together; a read that finds no proof leaves the interval unconfirmed for account inspection. An expired read lease permits another read or verified resolution, never a repeated booking POST. Verified not-created resolutions also record one safe `call.appointment_not_created` entry in the call event history; identical resolutions do not duplicate it. These appointment events are not currently subscribable webhook events. A stuck submitting result requires support investigation, even after its local request timeout.

The Calls page and its row-detail response include the same safe appointment state and interval. Opening a call does not discard its appointment status.
