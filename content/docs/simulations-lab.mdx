A simulation suite proves an AI agent before a caller does. You author realistic
caller scenarios once, pin an exact agent version, and replay them as often as
you like. Every attempt leaves a transcript, the exact workflow path it walked,
and a verdict against rules you wrote — not an impression, and not a score you
have to interpret.

Simulation suites never dial a phone number. Scripted and chat attempts use
simulation-only tool mocks. Voice attempts make a real call in a test room,
using your AI agent's saved engine. Business writes such as sending an SMS,
transferring a customer or changing an integration are blocked. Voice tests
retain conversation controls such as ending the call and switching language.
Provider usage is bounded; tests are not charged to your workspace balance.

## The shape of a suite

A **suite** belongs to one AI agent and holds **scenarios**. A scenario is one
caller: a script of turns, the tool fixtures that turn should see, and what must
(and must not) happen. Each scenario declares how many **attempts** it
contributes to a run.

Editing a suite never overwrites what it used to say. Every accepted edit
appends a new immutable **revision**, so a run that executed revision 11 keeps
reading revision 11 no matter how far the suite moves afterwards. That is also
what makes concurrent editing safe: a `PATCH` carries the revision your edit is
based on, and a stale write is refused with `409` carrying the revision that
beat it, rather than silently replacing someone else's work.

## Runners

The runner is a property of each scenario. One suite can mix these runners
when Simulations Lab is enabled:

| Runner | Workspace name | What it tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Deterministic** | Scripted | Exact workflow routes, mocked tools and terminal outcomes, without model calls. |
| **Adaptive chat** | AI caller · chat | An AI caller conversing with the workflow in text. |
| **Adaptive voice** | AI caller · voice | A synthetic caller speaking to the real AI agent in a live test room. |

Voice tests support cascade, GPT-Live, Gemini Live and Eleven Realtime agents.
They use the agent's own configuration. They do not substitute a different
transcriber or voice engine to make a test run. The caller has its own voice
and listener, selected for the scenario language.

The **Run this suite** panel checks the selected configuration and explains
whether the test will be a workflow call or a prompt call. A prompt voice test
can run without a workflow. Scripted and chat scenarios still require one.
See [Live-call voice tests](#live-call-voice-tests) for the mode and rule details.

### Caller-input environments

An `adaptive_voice` scenario may carry an optional strict
`caller_environment` object:
`{ preset_id, asset_version, noise_level }`. Choose its preset and version from
the [audio-environment catalogue](/docs/audio-environments#discover-the-catalogue).
This is a **synthetic caller microphone** condition, not an agent output
setting: it uses `noise_level`, never `volume`, and it never changes the
agent's TTS, outgoing ambience, or production STT configuration. Off with
`noise_level: 0` is the quiet baseline; an enabled preset needs a positive
whole-number level. The scenario must also give a non-blank explicit
`language`; Skysay does not silently fall back to English.

The condition applies to caller audio in the live room. It does not qualify
real microphones, all accents, echo cancellation or a carrier's telephone
network. Test those separately before relying on them in production.

## The adaptive caller

An adaptive scenario uses a **persona** instead of a fixed sequence of turns: — who the caller is, what they want, how they behave, and bounded
lists of when they cut in and when they change direction — and a caller model
writes each turn by reading the AI agent's last reply.

The caller may do exactly three things: **say** something, **interrupt**, or
**hang up**. That is the whole vocabulary. If the caller model answers with
anything else — a tool call, a transfer destination, an extra field, or
something that is not valid JSON — that attempt ends as a typed **error**, not
as a failure. A caller that misbehaved tells you nothing about your AI agent,
and recording it as a verdict would let a bad caller make a healthy agent look
broken. Chat attempts do not retry a malformed caller response. Voice attempts have
the bounded test-side retry policy described below.

Two things the caller can never do, by construction:

- **It does not decide anything.** Your AI agent's own model still answers every
  routing and interrupt question — that is the thing being tested. The caller
  can influence what your agent hears, never what it decides.
- **It cannot reach a real tool.** Chat attempts use the same
  test-only mocks as scripted attempts. Voice calls block business writes and
  stop at workflow tool steps, as described below.

Repeating an adaptive scenario measures **consistency, not luck** — set
`attempts` above 1 when you want to know whether a behaviour holds up, rather
than whether it happened once.

Every adaptive attempt is bounded by a hard turn ceiling, independent of any
assertion you write. A scripted scenario ends when its written turns run out; an
adaptive one needs a limit of its own.

## Quality rubrics

A rubric asks a question no assertion can settle: was the reply clear, was the
tone right, did it actually help. Each scenario may carry up to **five** — each
one is a separate evaluator call on every attempt, so that ceiling is a
multiplier on what a run costs, not a formatting preference.

A rubric carries what to judge, a passing score on a 1–5 scale, and optional
examples that should score high or low. The examples matter: an instruction plus
a bare scale is not a rubric, and two runs are only comparable if the evaluator
was anchored the same way.

Rubrics are evaluated **last**, and three rules bound what they can do:

- A rubric below its threshold **fails** that attempt — you asked for something
  and did not get it — but it can never make the failure *critical*. Quality may
  add a failure. It may never rescue or escalate one.
- When an attempt has already failed a critical rule, the evaluator is **not
  called at all**. Its answer could not change the outcome, so you are not
  charged for it; those rubrics record `skipped` with the reason.
- When the evaluator cannot answer — unreachable, rate-limited, or incoherent —
  the result is **`unknown`**, which is distinct from both a pass and a fail.
  An evaluator failure never becomes an approval.

Every verdict stores the evaluator's provider, model, prompt version, prompt
hash and the rubric revision it graded. "Same model" and "same question" are
different claims, and comparing two scores means having both.

Attempts whose quality could not be graded leave the **quality** average — not
the run. A workspace showing a clean score beside an evaluator outage would be
telling you something it does not know.

## What a run may spend

Deterministic attempts make no model call and genuinely cost nothing. Adaptive
chat attempts are metered, and a run pins its whole spending envelope before the
first attempt starts: an upper-bound cost estimate and a ceiling on model calls.

The estimate is an **upper bound**, not a forecast — it assumes every attempt
uses its whole turn budget and every rubric is judged. Being charged less than a
stated ceiling is a better surprise than the reverse.

For chat tests, every model call is claimed against that ceiling *before* it is made, including
your AI agent's own calls: they are the thing being tested, but they are still
calls the run caused. If a run exhausts its budget it stops and ends with status
`budget_exceeded` — every verdict already recorded is kept and final, and the
attempts that had not started simply never run. That is a terminal state of its
own, neither a pass nor an agent failure.

Your workspace also has ceilings of its own: how many runs may be queued or
running at once, and what those runs may commit to spend together. Exceeding
either is refused when you start the run, with a typed code, before anything is
queued.

## Grouping failures and comparing runs

A run's results group failing attempts by **cause**. Attempts that failed the
same critical rule are one cluster even if what happened afterwards differed, so
forty failures usually resolve to two or three real problems.

You can designate any finished run of a suite as its **baseline** by sending
`baseline_run_id` on the suite `PATCH`. Later runs then report deltas against
it: pass rate, critical failures, duration, and which failure clusters are new
since. A baseline must belong to the same suite, must have finished, and must
have recorded attempts — each of the three is refused because it would make the
comparison meaningless rather than merely imprecise. Send an empty string to
clear it.

Where a delta cannot be computed it is reported as null rather than zero, and a
comparison drawn over the most common causes says so rather than implying it saw
every one.

## Authoring in the workspace

A suite's page in **Simulations Lab** has three tabs: **Scenarios**, **Pass rules &
budget** and **Run history**.

**Scenarios.** Add or edit a scenario of any type your workspace can run. The
**AI caller · voice** type is available when Simulations Lab is enabled. Each
scenario takes a name, how many attempts it makes per run (1 to 25), whether it is
critical (a failed critical scenario fails the run), and a language. A scripted
scenario takes the caller's lines in order; an AI caller takes a goal, first words,
who the caller is, how they behave, when they cut in and when they change direction.
A voice scenario also sets the background noise at the caller's microphone. Every
type can declare the tools, steps and routes the AI agent must and must not use, an
order its steps must happen in, a turn limit and a duration limit, and up to five
judge questions, each a separate metered evaluator call on every attempt.

Timing rules (when the AI agent starts replying, how fast it stops when interrupted,
how fast it hangs up) can be set on a voice scenario only. A timing rule kept on a
scripted or chat scenario is labelled **not provable** and never counts as a pass.

Fields the workspace does not show, such as tool mocks or per-turn routes written
through the API, are kept exactly as they are when you edit a scenario.

**Pass rules & budget** sets the advisory quality pass rate, a spend limit per run,
how long recordings and transcripts are kept, whether runs test the draft or the
published AI agent, and which finished run later runs are compared against.

Every save in the workspace is a suite edit on the revision you opened. If someone
else saved the suite in the meantime, nothing is overwritten: the page names the
revision that won, keeps your changes on screen, and offers to reapply them on top
of it.

**Unfinished edits.** Scenario and policy fields save automatically in the current
browser tab as you change them. **Draft saved in this tab** means your unfinished
work can be recovered after navigating away or refreshing. **Close** keeps a
scenario draft; **Resume draft** opens it again. **Discard draft** asks before
removing unsaved changes. Drafts expire 24 hours after their last change, are
cleared on sign-out, and do not sync across devices or survive closing the tab.
If browser storage is unavailable, the editor warns you to keep it open until
saving.

Click **Save scenario** (or the policy save button) to save the completed changes
to the workspace. A browser draft does not count as a saved scenario and cannot
be run. Recovering a draft preserves its original revision: if someone else saved
in the meantime, review and explicitly reapply your changes. If you left during
a save, check the saved version before retrying; the editor keeps the new
scenario’s identity so a retry cannot silently add a duplicate.

**Duplicate suite** creates a copy at revision 1 with the same scenarios and rules.
Run history and the baseline are not copied.

## Removing a suite

Use the suite’s **⋯** menu in the workspace or AI agent list, or beside
**Duplicate suite** on its detail page.

- **Delete suite** is available when the suite has never had a run. Confirming
  permanently removes its scenarios, settings and revisions. It cannot be undone.
- **Archive suite** is offered when run history exists. Runs already queued or
  running keep their pinned content, and results remain available. Starting or
  rerunning tests is blocked until you choose **Restore suite**. Archived suites
  remain clearly marked in the list and still count toward the suite limit.

The confirmation warns if removing or archiving the suite disables its publish
check. Restoring it re-enables that configured check. If someone changes or runs
the suite while the confirmation is open, the command refuses the outdated
operation; review the refreshed suite before choosing again.

API clients can delete a never-run suite with
`DELETE /v1/agents/{agent_id}/simulation-suites/{suite_id}?revision=N`, using its
current revision and `agents:write`. A stale revision or any run history returns
409. Archive or restore through the existing PATCH endpoint with `revision` and
`status: "archived"` or `"active"`. MCP clients can use `delete_simulation_suite`
for never-run suites; history is protected by the same command.

## Running a suite

From the workspace, the suite page carries a **Run this suite** panel: the attempts each
runner will make, what the run is estimated to cost, the ceilings it will be given, and a
start button. Every figure there comes from the same calculation the run is created
against, so the estimate you read before starting is the allowance the run enforces.

If no scenario has been saved, the panel says **Save at least one scenario to start a run** and offers **Continue editing**. Saving a valid scenario refreshes readiness; workflow and runtime requirements still apply.

The panel explains refusals such as an archived or empty suite, an invalid
saved configuration, or a workflow that cannot be admitted. Voice setup is
checked against the same admission decision as Start. It displays the call
mode, planned calls and retries, unsupported rules, and any workflow steps at
which testing will stop. GPT-Live and other live engines are supported; there
is no separate offline speech-stack requirement.

Submitting twice returns the same run. The key that guarantees that belongs to the page
you are looking at, so a retry after a failed or lost response reuses it rather than
paying again.


`POST /v1/agents/{agent_id}/simulation-suites/{suite_id}/runs` returns `202`
immediately with a durable queued run. The request does not wait for execution,
so its latency never depends on how long the suite takes; you can close the page
and come back.

At that moment the run pins everything a verdict will depend on: the suite
revision and its content hash, the saved voice configuration and, when applicable, the **compiled workflow contract itself**.
Editing the agent's draft while the run waits for a worker therefore cannot
change what the run proves.

An organization-scoped `idempotency_key` is required. Replaying the same request
returns the same run rather than queueing a second one; reusing one key for a
*different* request is refused with `409` rather than answered with someone
else's run.

```bash
curl -X POST \
  https://api.skysay.ai/v1/agents/AGENT_ID/simulation-suites/SUITE_ID/runs \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer sky_..." \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"idempotency_key": "nightly-2026-08-25", "pinned_source": "published"}'
```

Other typed refusals you may see, all before anything is queued:
`422 no_scenarios` (a suite with nothing to run), `422 suite_archived`,
`422 active_run_limit` (this workspace already has the maximum runs in flight),
`422 organization_budget` (too much spend is committed to active runs), and configuration-specific refusals explained in the run panel.
A workflow that cannot be run keeps its `404` (no workflow, or nothing published for a
suite pinned to the published version) or `400` (a draft with validation errors) and
adds `workflow_state`: `missing`, `unpublished`, `invalid` or `unknown`. The workspace
shows each of these as its own message; only a failure it cannot recognise, such as a
lost response, reads **Could not start the run**, and a retry from that message reuses
the same key.

## Reading verdicts

`GET /v1/agents/{agent_id}/simulation-suite-runs/{run_id}` returns the run, its
per-scenario rollup, and one bounded page of attempts in a stable order with an
explicit `has_more` and a cursor. Attempts grow every time you re-run, so the
page size is fixed by contract — a bounded slice never reads as a complete one.

Verdicts come in two levels, and the order matters:

- **Critical** assertions are the safety envelope: the terminal state, required
  and forbidden routes, tools and steps, disclosure ordering, and duration
  policy. These must hold on every attempt.
- **Business** assertions are what the conversation was supposed to achieve:
  the expected outcome, turn limits, and expected mocks.

- **Quality** rubrics are last and always advisory. A score can explain an
  attempt and can fail it against its own rubric; it can never override a failed
  critical assertion, and an evaluator that could not answer records `unknown`
  rather than a pass.

An attempt that **errored** is a third thing, distinct from a pass and a fail. A
malformed scenario or a harness failure ends that attempt alone — the rest of
the run continues — and it is recorded as an error rather than being scored for
or against the AI agent. Every attempt is persisted independently, so one bad
attempt never takes a suite down with it.

Voice attempts grade the actual conversation and its ending. Missing caller
or room evidence is an error, never a pass. The live-call rules below explain
how an established violation can still fail a call stopped by the test.

## Reruns and regressions

`POST .../simulation-suite-runs/{run_id}/rerun-failed` queues a **child** run over the
failed cases, reusing the parent's pinned revision, content hash and compiled
workflow — so it asks exactly the question the original asked. The parent and
every verdict it holds are untouched.

`POST .../simulation-suite-runs/{run_id}/cases/{case_result_id}/save-as-regression`
promotes one attempt into a permanent critical scenario by appending a new suite
revision. The historical run keeps pointing at the revision it executed.

**Cancel run** stops remaining work and closes live voice attempts. Completed
verdicts remain unchanged. **Rerun failed** opens a child run using the frozen
configuration; editing today's agent does not silently change the rerun.

Runs made by the retired offline voice runner remain read-only history. They
cannot be rerun or saved as new regressions. Start a new run from the suite to
use live-call testing. The old `min_recognition_bps` rule is no longer accepted;
the cutover removes it from current suites in a new revision while preserving
past revisions.

## Suite activity is not production activity

Every workflow run an attempt produces carries immutable synthetic provenance.
Synthetic runs are excluded by default from your call and message lists,
analytics, live success metrics, billing, and provider-usage reporting — so
running a suite a hundred times a day never distorts what your workspace
reports about real callers. This holds for adaptive attempts too — a synthetic
conversation is more convincing than a scripted one, which makes marking it
synthetic more important, not less.

Suite cost is reported only by the suite itself and is never charged to your
balance. Deterministic attempts genuinely cost nothing.

### Finding a lab call in Calls

Excluded by default is not the same as hidden. An attempt that opens a real
room books a real call, and that call has the same evidence as any other: a
transcript, the server-ordered timeline with the tool calls your AI agent
finished and the language switches it made, and the workflow path it took.

The Calls page has a second view for them. "Real calls" is what it opens on
and what it has always shown — the count, the totals and everything that asks
for your attention are about real callers only. Switching to "Simulations Lab"
asks the workspace the same question about lab calls instead, and the drawer
that opens on one of those rows is the drawer you already know. The two are
never mixed into one list: one view answers for one kind of call, and the row
says which kind it is.

The view is in the address, as `?source=simulation_suite`, so a link to a lab
call is a link somebody else on your team can open.

This is a workspace view, not a change to the API. `GET /v1/calls` still
returns only calls a customer or a caller actually made, exactly as before.

## Live-call voice tests

An **AI caller · voice** test is a live call: a synthetic caller talks to your
real AI agent out loud, on the AI agent's own engine (cascade, GPT-Live, Gemini
Live or Eleven Realtime), in a test room. No phone number and no phone network
are involved.

### Workflow call or prompt call

A test runs in the mode a real call of your AI agent would run:

- **Workflow call** when the engine can run workflow calls and the suite is
  pinned to a workflow (published, or the draft).
- **Prompt call** when the AI agent has no workflow yet, or when its engine
  cannot run workflow calls. The run panel then says
  *"Prompt call (this engine cannot run workflow calls)"*, and lists the real
  calls that configuration refuses, such as outbound calls with the published
  workflow.

If a phone pin on the AI agent's numbers sends inbound calls to a workflow its
engine cannot run, a real call would be refused, so the test is refused too,
and the run panel says why before you press **Start run**. The run panel checks
everything starting a run checks, so the same holds when a real call would
refuse your AI agent's setup (for example a missing voice, prompt or greeting),
when a call variable it requires has no test value, or when its prompt or
greeting can't be filled in from its call variables. One step is checked only
when the run starts: a draft workflow's lines in a language they were not yet
written in are translated then. A prompt test never needs a workflow.

### Conversation only, for now

This release tests the conversation only. Business tools and knowledge
search are switched off in prompt test calls. Conversation controls remain
available. In a workflow call, knowledge steps still run, and the test stops if the workflow reaches a step that would
run a tool (the run panel lists those steps). Tool rules
(`required_tools`, `forbidden_tools`) are **not applicable**: they neither pass
nor fail. In a prompt call the workflow rules (routes, steps, order, turns and
`terminal_status`) are not applicable either. Timing rules and judge questions
apply in both modes.

### The built-in check: "ended normally"

Every voice test checks that the call ended normally: your AI agent or our
caller hung up, with no crash and no time limit reached. It is the first rule
of every attempt, so a scenario with no rules of its own is still a test.

| Built-in check | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Pass | The call ended normally; the attempt's own rules decide. |
| Fail | Your AI agent's side ended the call: its engine session dropped, it stopped responding, its speech provider failed, it failed to start, or it treated our caller as a machine or a phone menu. The attempt fails. |
| Not settled | Our side ended or stopped the call (our caller went silent, the test room failed, the time limit was reached, or the test stopped itself). The attempt is an error, unless a rule was already broken before the call was cut short. |

### Ceilings and cost

Each test call lasts at most the call ceiling shown in the run panel (150 seconds
by default) and at most 20 caller turns. The run panel shows the run's planned
test calls, one per attempt. A call that fails on our side is retried once
automatically, for at most two retries per run (one when the run plans a single
call); a retry is never retried. Voice tests are **not charged** in this
release: the run's estimate leaves them out and no money line is shown for them.

### What an attempt shows

Open **Test call detail** under an attempt to see:

- the **verdict**: pass, fail, or error (an error is never counted for or
  against your AI agent), with the reason in plain words and its code;
- **how the call ended** and, when the test stopped the call itself, **why**
  (for example, *the workflow reached a step that would run a tool*);
- every **rule**, the built-in check first, with its state: pass, fail, not
  settled, not exercised or not applicable;
- the **judge questions**, with each score and its passing score, when they were
  judged (a verdict already decided by the call's ending or a stop needs no judge);
- the **measured timings**: when your AI agent started replying after each of
  our caller's lines, how quickly it stopped when interrupted, how soon it hung
  up after its last words, and the call's length;
- the **recording** state;
- **what our caller heard** beside **what your AI agent recorded**, so a word
  one side misheard is visible;
- every failed or unsettled rule's evidence, such as *expected at most 500 ms,
  got 640 ms*, with the rule's code;
- the cost: not charged.

An attempt still in progress, being scored, or waiting for its automatic retry
says so; it is never shown as a pass, fail or error.

An attempt can fail on evidence gathered before the call was cut short: a
timing bound already broken or a forbidden route already taken still fails it.

### In the API

`GET /v1/agents/{agent_id}/simulation-suite-runs/{run_id}` returns, for a
live-call voice attempt, a `live_call` object: `mode`, `state`, `verdict`
(`status`, `code`, `marks`, `retry`), `built_in_check` (with the ending's code
in `reason`), `ending`, `stop`,
`timings`, `recording`, `charge` (`not_charged`) and `agent_recorded` (your AI
agent's own transcript of the call, at most 200 lines, with
`agent_recorded_page.has_more`). Its `facts` is empty, and its rubric rows carry
`id`, `result`, `score`, `threshold`, `reason` and `rationale`. Without the
`recordings:read` scope, `agent_recorded`, the transcript and rubric rationales
are omitted; the verdict stays. A live-call run also carries `run.live_call`
with its mode, call ceiling and the workflow steps it stops at.

`GET /v1/agents/{agent_id}/simulation-suites/{suite_id}` returns
`run_setup.live_call`: the mode a run started now would get, any refusal that
start would meet, the rules that mode cannot apply, the built-in check, the
call ceiling, `planned_calls`, `retry_allowance` and `max_calls`, and that voice
tests are not charged. Voice rows of `run_setup.by_runner` are not metered.

## Voice proof: two different things

New voice attempts produce **real-time** evidence from the caller in the test
room. Old runs labelled **Offline robustness** remain historical speech round
trips. They measured no live conversation and cannot prove interruption,
response or teardown timing. They are not an alternative execution mode.

Each timing rule needs its own measured observation. A `realtime` label alone
is not proof. Missing observations remain unsettled or not exercised; an
authored applicable rule cannot become a pass without evidence. A completed
recording is also separate from the timing measurements.

### Shared capacity and recording availability

Real-time simulation callers use bounded capacity shared across workspaces.
When capacity is busy or paused for a voice release, tests wait within a
bounded retry window. They do not take the last reserved customer slot or fall
back to offline execution. If the wait expires, the result explains that
capacity was unavailable. Homepage demos use a separate capacity limit.

A room's measured timing and its recording are separate evidence. A recording
can become available after the timing result has arrived. Reopen the existing
result to check its recording; starting another run creates another test and can
incur new provider costs.

Missing audio cannot establish that the voice sounded correct. Recording
delivery does not change measured timing facts or repeat the run's usage charge.
Expired or deleted recordings remain unavailable, including when a delayed copy
arrives later.

## When a speech stack cannot serve a scenario

Check the run panel before starting. It identifies an invalid agent setup or
an unavailable caller voice/listener for a scenario language. Correct the
configuration or choose a supported scenario language; the test never silently
changes the agent's providers. GPT-Live and Gemini Live do not need a separate
TTS or STT configuration for the tested agent.

The platform operates the shared caller and room workers. A workspace does not
need to deploy its own dispatcher, upload a recorded-line pack or install host
credentials. A saved scenario still needs a successful, evidence-bearing run
before it proves any behavior.

## Retention

Each suite sets how long its heavy artifacts live: `audio_retention_days`
(default 7) and `transcript_retention_days` (default 30). The windows are pinned
onto a run when it starts, so editing a suite never retroactively shortens how
long a past run's evidence was promised to live.

When a window closes, that artifact is released. **Verdicts, assertions, rubric
results and the step trace are permanent** — a run from a year ago still tells
you exactly what happened; you simply cannot play it back.

Results distinguish an artifact that **expired** from one that never existed,
because those are different facts. In the automation API each attempt carries
`artifacts.recording` and `artifacts.transcript`, each `available`, `expired` or
`none`.

## From an AI agent: MCP

Everything on this page is reachable over [MCP](/docs/mcp), so an agent can
author a suite, run it and read the verdicts without a human at a screen. Eight
tools, listed with their scopes in the [tool reference](/docs/tool-reference).

**Importing a pack is one call.** `create_simulation_suite` takes the whole
`scenarios` array, so a scenario pack you keep in version control becomes a
suite in a single tool call rather than one call per scenario.

```json
{
  "name": "create_simulation_suite",
  "arguments": {
    "organization_id": "org_...",
    "agent_id": "agent_...",
    "name": "Payment reminder regression",
    "scenarios": [
      {
        "id": "promise_to_pay",
        "runner": "deterministic",
        "turns": [{ "caller_input": "Yes, I'll pay on Friday." }],
        "expected": { "terminal_status": "completed" }
      }
    ]
  }
}
```

**Ask what a run costs before you start one.** `preview_simulation_run` is
read-only: it creates no run, queues nothing and reserves no budget. It returns
the attempt counts, the text-run estimate and the model-call ceiling, plus
`supported_runners`. Voice tests are excluded from the customer cost estimate
in this release. Read the suite detail for the live-call configuration preview.

**Starting an adaptive run incurs metered provider costs.** These are tracked
against the suite budget, not charged to the workspace balance, as described
above. `start_simulation_run` is
annotated `destructiveHint`, and it requires the same `idempotency_key` the REST
route does — never generated for you, because a key you choose is the only thing
that makes a retried start return the first run instead of paying for a second.
It is also annotated `idempotentHint`, so a client that lost a response can
safely repeat the call with the same key.

**Choosing offline or spoken.** The mode is a property of each scenario, not of
the run, so one suite can hold both. `start_simulation_run` takes `scenario_ids`
— run the deterministic scenarios on every commit, and the `adaptive_voice` ones
on the schedule your budget allows.

**Run metadata and conversation content.** `get_simulation_run` needs
`agents:read` and always returns status, verdicts and pagination. Transcripts,
recording references and facts additionally require `recordings:read`; without
it, assertion actual values, rubric rationale and linked-evidence notes are
also omitted because they can quote conversation content. The same field rule
applies to the REST run reads.
`get_simulation_run_results`
returns the redacted pass/fail projection described under
[Automation](#automation-reading-results-from-ci) and needs only
`simulation_suites:read` — the scope to issue a build server.

While the Simulations Lab is not enabled on a deployment, every one of these
tools answers not-found, exactly as the HTTP routes do. They still appear in
`tools/list`, which is a catalog of what Skysay can do rather than an
inventory of what one deployment has switched on.

## Automation: reading results from CI

`GET /v1/agents/{agent_id}/simulation-suite-runs/{run_id}/results` is built for a build
server rather than for a screen. It requires the narrow `simulation_suites:read`
scope — issue a key with only that scope for a CI job, rather than granting it
`agents:read` over every AI agent's configuration.

```bash
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $SKYSAY_API_KEY" \
  "https://api.skysay.ai/v1/agents/$AGENT_ID/simulation-suite-runs/$RUN_ID/results?limit=100"
```

The response opens with `contract_version`. Fields may be **added** under the
same version; nothing is removed or given a new meaning without it changing, so
pin the version you understand and ignore what you do not recognise.

Attempts paginate on a stable cursor with an explicit `has_more` and
`next_cursor`; the query cost does not grow with the number of attempts. Run
status is one of `queued`, `running`, `completed`, `failed`, `cancelled`,
`budget_exceeded`; an attempt is `pass`, `fail` or `error`.

Read `totals.errored_cases` separately from `totals.failed_cases`. An errored
attempt produced no evidence about your AI agent — a malformed synthetic caller
response, or an evaluator that could not answer — so failing a build on it means
failing your release for our outage.

Transcripts, recordings and rubric rationales are deliberately not included. Use
the workspace for those; the attempt id here is the link.

## Blocking a publish on a suite

A suite can refuse to let an AI agent's workflow be published. It is off unless
you turn it on, and then it is opt-in a second time for anything advisory.

- **Deterministic critical failures** block by default once the gate is enabled.
  Those are statements about safety, tenancy, forbidden tools and required
  paths: they either held or they did not.
- **Quality rubrics never block unless you name that rubric.** There is no
  "block on any failing rubric" switch, because a rubric is a model's opinion
  about tone and turning every opinion into a release gate should not be one
  click away.
- **`unknown` never blocks by default.** An evaluator that could not answer, or
  a rule that needed evidence the attempt could not carry, leaves publishing
  alone unless you explicitly ask otherwise. Blocking there means an outage on
  our side stops your release.
- **No qualifying run** does not block by default either.

The run consulted is the one that tested the exact draft being published,
matched on its compiled hash. A suite that passed last week against different
content neither blocks this release nor vouches for it.

A refusal is `422 publish_blocked_by_simulation_suite` and names the suite, the run
and a machine-readable `reason`.

## Linking a human test to an attempt

`POST /v1/agents/{agent_id}/simulation-suite-runs/{run_id}/cases/{case_result_id}/evidence`
attaches an existing Talk or Phone session to an attempt as corroborating
evidence. It **links**; it never runs anything. Automated suites do not place
calls, and this endpoint can only reference a session a person already created.

The linked session must belong to the same workspace and the same AI agent. A
phone session additionally requires that a phone test you armed actually matched
a real incoming call — Skysay has no phone-ownership proof, so a matched pin
is the closest honest substitute and no stronger claim is made.

The attempt's own verdict is unchanged. Linked evidence sits beside a verdict
and never replaces one.

In the workspace, **Link a real call** on an attempt lists this AI agent's most
recent Talk sessions and matched phone tests (up to 20, newest first; only sessions
that can be linked are listed) with an optional note.

## Availability

Simulation suites roll out per workspace. While the capability is not enabled for
yours, every route above answers `404`. If you expect access and see `404`,
contact support rather than retrying.

## Knowledge-search validation

Unlinking an AI agent’s knowledge sources does not remove steps from its
workflow. If Flow reports `AGENT_TOOL_NEEDS_SOURCE` for `search_knowledge_base`,
open that step. To keep knowledge search, choose the AI agent’s knowledge-search
tool: its declaration uses `source: "agent"` and an empty `projection`, and its
fixed result is `knowledge_answer`.

If you do not want knowledge search, remove its step and unused tool declaration,
reconnect the surrounding routes and remove references to its answer. Check
validation again before testing. Repairing a tool declaration makes it valid;
it does not remove the tool or change the workflow’s starting step.
