or use the feature.

## 23 September 2026

### A call whose AI agent stops answering is ended instead of staying open

- A conversation engine can stop answering mid-call without reporting anything — a provider outage leaves the connection open and silent — and such a call used to stay up, with the caller talking to nobody, until it reached its maximum length.
- After the caller finishes speaking, the agent now has **20 seconds** of quiet line to produce a reply, a tool call or the end of the call; the caller's own speech is subtracted, so nobody is cut off mid-sentence.
- The call reports `ended_reason` `model_session_lost` with a new `reason_detail` `model_unresponsive`, which is what tells it apart from the same reason reached because the engine reported its own failure. The list of ended reasons is unchanged.
- Anything the caller can hear from the agent clears the bound, so it never runs while the agent is audibly replying, while one of its tools is running, before the caller has said anything, or on a call whose replies come from a workflow; set `voice_polish.model_unresponsive.seconds` on `PATCH /v1/agents/{agent_id}/voice-stack` to any value from 10 to 120, or `0` to switch it off.
- [Post-call results](/docs/post-call-results#when-the-conversation-engine-is-lost) · [Voice behaviour](/docs/voice-behavior#when-the-agent-stops-answering-altogether)

### Voice library: provider and model columns, and faster pages

- The **Voice library** table gains **Provider** and **Model** columns; a voice offered on two text-to-speech models lists both, each with its own price and first-audio time.
- **All**, **Live** and **Text to speech** filters separate voice-to-voice voices from text-to-speech voices, with a short note on what an agent of each kind can run.
- The Voice library and the new-agent **Voice** step load several times faster; the voices, totals and filters they return are unchanged.
- [Voice library](/docs/voice-library) · [Conversation engines](/docs/conversation-engines)

## 22 September 2026

### Transfer a call to a person, and find callbacks on Calls

- An agent with `transfer_to_human` enabled now dials its saved destination into the live call once; the person who answers hears a short introduction, then the agent stays quiet.
- The AI model is only told the transfer is connecting and never sees the destination number; if nobody answers, or the caller hangs up while it rings, the agent comes back.
- When a transfer cannot connect, or the caller asks to be called back, the agent saves one callback on the call with the caller's name, the reason and a number it read back to them.
- **Calls** has a **Callback requested** filter, and the open call shows the name, number, reason and the number the transfer was offered to. The callback is not sent by text or email yet.
- [Transfer to a human](/docs/transfer-to-a-human) · [Tool reference](/docs/tool-reference)

### Calls on Gemini Live are no longer refused before they start

- Every call on a Gemini Live agent was refused before it started, with a `400` naming an unsupported TTS billing unit; inbound calls, outbound calls and **Talk to this draft now** were all affected.
- Those calls are now admitted and billed on the engine's call-duration meter, the same way as GPT-Live 1 calls.
- [Conversation engines](/docs/conversation-engines) · [Name the language of a Gemini Live call](/docs/multilingual-outbound#resolve-the-language-before-each-call)

### Calls are no longer refused while the voice service warms up

- Preparing a new call's voice session no longer makes the voice service report itself as full, so a call arriving at that moment is no longer refused and answered with silence.
- The voice service now admits calls by how many it is already running, not by a momentary processor reading; there is nothing to configure.
- [Read call evidence](/docs/read-call-evidence)

### The AI agent says it could not understand, instead of going silent

- A Standard agent whose caller says something the transcriber produces no words for — most often a language the agent is not listening for — now speaks one short line in its own language saying it could not understand and naming the languages it can help in, then keeps listening.
- It is spoken only after the caller stops talking, so it never interrupts, and at most twice per call; it never ends or transfers the call.
- On for every Standard agent, and skipped on an agent with **follow the caller** enabled, where a language switch answers instead. GPT-Live 1 and agent workflows are unaffected: the first has no separate transcription step, and the second decides every turn itself.
- Send `voice_polish.unrecognised_speech.enabled: false` on `PATCH /v1/agents/{agent_id}/voice-stack` to switch it off, or `phrase` and `max_per_call` to change the wording and the limit.
- [When the AI agent cannot understand the caller](/docs/unrecognised-speech) · [Follow the caller](/docs/multilingual-outbound#optional-follow-the-caller)

### Name the language of a GPT-Live 1 or Gemini Live call

- An outbound call on a voice-to-voice engine now accepts `runtime_config.language` and opens in that language; before this, only the agent's saved language was allowed and any other language was refused.
- These engines have one voice for every language, so there is no language profile to publish first: any language Skysay supports is accepted as given.
- A campaign on such an agent now dials its contacts in their own language instead of ending them `pre_dial_failed` with `language_not_published`.
- A language Skysay does not support is still refused before the call is placed, and agents whose engine picks voice and transcription per language are unchanged.
- [Resolve the language before each call](/docs/multilingual-outbound#resolve-the-language-before-each-call) · [Campaign languages](/docs/campaigns) · [GPT-Live 1 languages](/docs/language-qualification)


### Outbound call billing runs from call placement

- Billed call duration for an outbound call runs from the moment the call is placed, not from the answer, and includes the ringing time before it.
- [Billing and browser credit](/docs/billing) · [What you are billed](/docs/post-call-results#what-you-are-billed)


## 17 September 2026

### A call the media worker fails to start is ended by the platform, not left silent

- When Skysay's media worker fails after a call is admitted and before it reports that media started, the platform now hangs up the phone leg, closes the room and records one `call.failed` result with `technical_failure.failure_reason_code` `worker_entry_failed` and `failure_stage` `worker_entry`.
- The wallet reservation is released and nothing is charged; previously such a call stayed `dialing` with no result while the person who answered heard silence.
- No setting and no override: a call that never delivered media is never continued. The reason is published on the call resource, in `GET /v1/call-results` and on the `call.failed` webhook.
- [Read call evidence](/docs/post-call-results)


### Choose every language an AI agent answers in while you create it

- The new-agent **Identity** step now asks for languages rather than a language: the first opens the call, and the rest are added as chips beside it.
- The **Voice** step then offers only the voices and transcription that cover every language you picked, so a multilingual agent can be published without changing its stack afterwards.
- **Follow the caller** starts on for an agent created with two or more languages whose chosen transcriber declares it can listen for all of them at once; otherwise it starts off. Existing agents, and any language you add later in the editor, are unchanged — there it stays off until you enable and publish it.
- The Agents list names every language an agent answers in instead of only the one that opens the call, and an agent whose languages were never chosen explicitly still shows the language it has.
- GPT-Live 1 is unaffected: it has no language setting, so it shows the opening language only.
- [Create a multilingual agent](/docs/multilingual-outbound#when-you-create-the-ai-agent) · [Follow the caller](/docs/multilingual-outbound#optional-follow-the-caller) · [GPT-Live language following](/docs/language-following)


## 16 September 2026

### An agent listens for every language it is configured for

- An agent on Azure AI Speech or Google Chirp 2 now listens for all of its selected languages at once, with no setting to enable and nothing to publish.
- Speech in another selected language is transcribed as that language instead of being written into the call language's script, which used to make the agent answer a question nobody asked.
- The reply language is unchanged, and this is not automatic language switching: in **Choose before the call** the agent still answers in the language that call was published in.
- An agent with one selected language is unaffected, as is one on Deepgram Nova-3 or Soniox, and an agent with more languages than its transcriber can compare at once keeps listening for the language its call is in.
- [Language qualification](/docs/language-qualification#azure-ai-speech) · [Speaking several languages versus listening for them](/docs/language-qualification#speaking-several-languages-versus-listening-for-them)


### Sinhala transcription moves to Google Chirp 2; the older Google V1 model is retired

- Sinhala agents transcribe with Google Chirp 2 only. The older Google V1 model (`command_and_search`) is removed from the transcriber catalogue and can no longer be selected; it returned no transcript at all for many callers on noisy lines, which showed up as an agent that never replied.
- An agent that still stored the old model is refused before media with a clear reason rather than silently re-routed; re-select Chirp 2 in the agent editor.
- Pricing history for calls that used the old model is unchanged.
- [Language qualification](/docs/language-qualification) · [Transcribers and voices](/docs/conversation-engines)


### Outbound calls always listen to the person who answered

- The agent's audio input is now bound by name to the answered phone leg, so a call can no longer play its opening and then hear nothing while the other party talks.
- Every answered call records whether that binding succeeded and how many caller audio frames arrived in the first seconds, visible in the call's evidence, so a silent call can be told apart from a caller who said nothing.
- No setting changes and no call is ended by this; it only affects what the agent hears.
- [Read call evidence](/docs/post-call-results)


### An agent can be told to end a call nobody is on

- `answer_detection.non_human_audio` gains `end_call`: the agent stops speaking and the call is ended with `no_human_after_answer` when only a tone is heard after pickup, or when the far end holds the line for twenty seconds without ever pausing long enough for anybody to reply.
- A person stops it by pausing, which also brings the agent's voice back; the call is not restarted and they are not greeted again.
- `shadow` stays the default and changes nothing a caller experiences. `end_call` is opt-in per agent, in the Answer detection card of the agent editor or through the voice-stack API.
- A call ended this way is excluded from connect rate and talk time, takes the `no_answer` campaign disposition so the contact is dialled again, and keeps `termination_cause` `connected`, because that field describes the transport.
- [Answer detection](/docs/answer-detection) · [Why a call ended](/docs/post-call-results#why-a-call-ended)

## 15 September 2026

### GPT-Live 1 calls also listen for the other party's captions

- On a GPT-Live 1 agent, the engine's own live captions of the other party now count as caller speech for the no-answer hang-up rule, alongside voice activity detection.
- A talking customer on a GPT-Live call is no longer disconnected because the audio-only detector missed them.
- Cascade agents are unaffected; they still detect speech from audio only.
- [Read call evidence](/docs/post-call-results)

### GPT-Live 1 speaks its opening exactly as written

- The GPT-Live 1 opening message is now spoken exactly as written, in its own language and script, instead of being paraphrased.
- Automatic language following starts only after that opening has been spoken.
- A per-call opening set through the Calls API replaces the saved opening for that call.
- [Conversation engines](/docs/conversation-engines) · [Which languages the voice agent follows](/docs/language-following)

### Every answered call now says what answered it

- A call carries `answer_classification` (`human`, `non_human_audio`, `silent` or `unknown`) and `reason_detail` (`caller_tune`, `hold_music` or `no_audio`) on the call resource, in call group results and in the `call.completed` webhook.
- `ended_reason` gains `no_human_after_answer` for a call ended because only music or tones were heard after pickup.
- A call that ends that way will be excluded from connect rate and talk time and will take the `no_answer` disposition, so the contact is dialled again; its `termination_cause` stays `connected`, because that field describes the transport.
- The per-agent setting `answer_detection.non_human_audio` chooses `shadow`, which records the verdict on every answered call and changes nothing, or `off`, which collects nothing; unlike the other tuning settings an explicit choice is stored, so it survives a later change to the platform default. `end_call`, which acts on the verdict, followed the next day.
- Detection needs a pause to call something a person, so a recorded announcement or a menu prompt reads as a person and is left alone.
- [Answer detection](/docs/answer-detection) · [Why a call ended](/docs/post-call-results#why-a-call-ended)

### Workspace polish: voice descriptions and asking for an integration

- A long voice description in the Voice Library now clamps to one line with a **Show more** / **Show less** link, so rows stay aligned for comparison.
- The Voice Library no longer carries a standing note about a provider's search window; the incomplete-results note that already appears with a limited result set covers it.
- The Integrations page, with or without an integration connected, ends its systems list with **Need another integration? Message us**.
- [Search the catalog](/docs/voice-library) · [Integrations](/docs/integrations)

### Interruption presets apply in every language

- `interruption_sensitivity` (`balanced`, `low`, `high`) now applies on hosted phone calls in every language, not only English; `balanced` stays the default and changes nothing.
- The preset sets the interruption threshold (500/750/150 ms) and the end-of-turn hold: 200/520/260 ms in a language with the turn detector, 800/1100/600 ms in every other language.
- An explicit `min_speech_ms` or `silence_hold_ms` still takes precedence over the preset for its own setting.
- `barge_in_speech_rms` is deprecated: it is still accepted and returned, but the runtime does not apply it.
- [Interruptions and barge-in](/docs/wire-an-agent#interruptions-and-barge-in)

### The Agents workspace, rebuilt

- The Agents page is a table: one row per AI agent with its status, number, calls today and the one thing to do next.
- An agent reads as Needs a voice, Ready to test, Tested, Number activating or Live, and the same word appears in the editor.
- Creating an AI agent is three steps — Start, Identity, Voice — with presets, bring-your-own, and GPT-Live 1 offered as one engine.
- The editor groups its sections as Agent, Uses, Conversation, Ship and Advanced; Overview lists what is left to do and what blocks publishing, and Publish changes shows exactly which fields changed before it writes.
- [Wire an agent](/docs/wire-an-agent) · [Acknowledgements](/docs/voice-behavior) · [Conversation engines](/docs/conversation-engines)

### Explicit interruption thresholds down to 150 ms on phone calls

- An explicit `min_speech_ms` on a hosted phone call is now honoured down to 150 ms in every language; a value below 150 ms is raised to 150 ms.
- An agent that sets no value keeps the 500 ms default. `interruption_sensitivity` presets applied to English agents only at first release; later the same day they were extended to every language (see below).
- A wordless burst that clears the threshold pauses the reply for up to a second and resumes if nothing was said.
- [Interruptions and barge-in](/docs/wire-an-agent#interruptions-and-barge-in)

### A wrong party and a cease contact are now two different outcomes

- The payment-reminder starter template routes a wrong party and a request not to be contacted to two separate outcome steps, `wrong_party_stop` and `cease_contact_stop`, where both used to land on one `stop_and_suppress` step labelled "Wrong party or cease contact — immediate stop". Each label states its own consequence now: both stop the call, a cease contact also adds the number to your do-not-call register and a wrong party leaves it where it was. The dispute and hardship labels lost the word "suppress" for the same reason — neither ever registered anything either.
- Campaign exports and the contacts page follow the labels: `workflow_outcome` and `workflow_outcome_label` carry the new step id and the new wording. Campaign dispositions do not change — a wrong party is still `connected_wrong_party` and a cease contact still `do_not_call` — and the workspace still renders runs recorded against the old `stop_and_suppress` id as it did before.
- Validation now refuses three drafts it used to accept: one pointing both interrupts at a single outcome step, one letting anything other than the cease-contact interrupt reach the cease-contact outcome, and one carrying a starter-template label that claims the opposite of what the route landing on it does.
- Any draft whose two interrupts named one existing step was split for you on this release — one from the earlier starter template and one you wired yourself alike, bar a draft with no step for the new outcome to end on, which is left as it was. The shared step keeps its id, every other route into it and any label you wrote yourself; published versions are untouched. The repair is a write, so a client holding a `base_revision` from before the release gets one `409` on its first `PATCH /v1/agents/{agent_id}/workflow` or publish after it, with the current draft in the `409` body under `current`.
- [Wrong party and cease contact](/docs/agent-workflows#payment-reminder-identity-clarification) · [Do-not-call register](/docs/campaigns#do-not-call-register)

### Nothing promises a 48-hour carrier review that never started

- The `headline` on `GET /v1/end-users/{end_user_profile_id}/registration-status` no longer reads "Registration usually completes within ~48h, often faster". It counts how many of that profile's number registrations Skysay has actually submitted to the carrier, and is empty when none is — including for every profile before its first number order. The old sentence appeared whenever Skysay's own checks were incomplete, which never established that anything had been sent.
- That headline is also empty while the profile is rejected or needs more information, because that is what needs your attention instead. The per-number `headline` on `GET /v1/numbers/{number_id}/activation` reports the same submission for one number and adds the published activation time for that market when there is one — passed through, never composed, and described as the market's figure rather than as something the carrier said about your request.
- `next_action` on that same endpoint no longer tells you to wait for carrier approval on a number nothing was ever sent for. A blocking item still speaks first; with nothing blocking the number it is one of three sentences now — submitted and awaiting the carrier, not submitted yet, or a registration that exists but is not showing as open.
- `activation_estimate` on `GET /v1/numbers/requirements` is not always a duration and must be checked before it is shown as one. It holds the carrier's published activation time where registration is required and that market publishes one, and is now empty where it publishes none — Estonia among them — instead of substituting "~48h, usually faster". The other two values, `instant_after_payment_and_policy_checks` and `manual_review`, are routing states, not times.
- [How long a regulated number takes to activate](/docs/rest-endpoints#how-long-a-regulated-number-takes-to-activate)

### A reviewer can put a contact back in the queue

- Resolving a campaign review accepts `requeue_contact: true`, for when you disagree with the outcome and want the contact called again. The commonest case is a wrong party: that verdict comes from one reading of one caller reply and it closes the contact. Resolving dials nobody — the contact becomes due and the next dispatch decides, applying every rule it normally does.
- A requeue can be refused, and the refusal comes back on the `200` body rather than only in the audit trail: `requeue_requested`, `contact_requeued` and `requeue_refused_reason`, which is one of `do_not_call_register`, `contact_phone_unknown` or `campaign_run_not_dispatchable`. A refusal is still a successful resolution — the note and the audit event are recorded either way — and a `requeue_contact` that is not a boolean answers `400`.
- A contact a dispatcher has already re-claimed comes back `contact_requeued: false` with an empty `requeue_refused_reason`: nothing was declined by policy, that contact is simply already back in the queue. A reviewer cannot undo an opt-out, and an opt-out that lands while a resolution is being decided now refuses the requeue as well, where it could previously put that contact back in the queue as if the opt-out had not happened.
- A call classified as a wrong party now records a `call.wrong_party_signals` event, readable on `GET /v1/calls/{call_id}/events` and never delivered to a webhook endpoint. It carries `route_id`, `from_step_id`, `turn_index`, `utterance_characters` and `utterance_words` — the size of the caller's reply, never its text. `turn_index` is `-1` when the turn count could not be read, never `0`, because `0` is a real turn index.

### Voice settings are saved by the voice-stack route, and refused elsewhere

- Sending `turn_taking`, `voice_polish`, `audio_environment`, `follow_caller`, `languages` or `language_profiles` to `PATCH /v1/agents/{agent_id}` now returns `400` naming the route that does save them. Until this release the same request returned `200` and discarded the setting, so a limit you believed was saved was not and the call ran on the platform default.
- The MCP `update_agent` tool is the same route and behaves the same way. If you have code setting one of these through it that appears to succeed, it has never taken effect — move it to `PATCH /v1/agents/{agent_id}/voice-stack` or the `set_agent_voice_stack` tool.
- Unrelated extra fields your client happens to send are still accepted and ignored, as before. Only these six recognised-but-misplaced names are refused.
- `PATCH /v1/agents/{agent_id}/voice-stack` also returns `400` if you send a TTS provider and model with no `voice_id` where that provider has voices to choose from; such a save previously returned `200` and stored a stack with no voice, and the call then failed before the caller heard anything. ### A call whose media session disappears now says so

- `ended_reason` gains one value, `media_session_lost`, for a call whose media session went away underneath it — the room was removed, or the connection carrying the audio failed — so nobody hung up, no limit fired and the agent did not finish.
- It reports what was observed rather than guessing at a cause. If someone had just asked to end that call it is still not reported as `hangup_requested`; the hangup request is answered separately and says the call was already gone.
- Calls that ended this way previously carried no reason at all, so a count of reasons over time will show empty values becoming `media_session_lost` from this release rather than a new kind of ending appearing.
- Simulations Lab test calls can report it too, where a synthetic room closes before the synthetic caller's own departure is delivered. A Lab result is judged on its assertions, not on this field.

### A recipient who did not answer is no longer a technical rejection

- An outbound call the recipient never answered is now recorded as `no_answer` with the reserved `outbound_ringing_timeout` reason, where it was previously classified as a technical rejection by the far end.
- The misclassification fired whenever the ring deadline passed with no SIP response at all, which is the ordinary shape: on one measured batch 37 of 50 attempts landed in the rejection bucket, making a pilot look like a platform outage and hiding the failures that really were ours.
- No new vocabulary. `outbound_ringing_timeout` and the `no_answer` terminal status are both already published, and a call is still only reported `no_answer` when that durable event proves it, so nothing downstream changes.
- If you alert on technical-failure rates for outbound campaigns, expect that rate to fall and `no_answer` to rise by the same amount from this release.

### The agent's closing line is played to the end

- When the agent ends the call itself, its closing line is now played to the end before the call is torn down.
- Skysay learns that a line has finished playing slightly before the other party has actually heard it, and hanging up at that moment clipped the last word or two.
- The call is instead held open until the audio going to the caller has fallen quiet. That wait is short and bounded: it gives up after about a second and ends the call anyway, so a closing line that never finishes cannot hold a call open.

### A Simulations Lab room that delivered nothing no longer reports a pass

- A real-time attempt whose synthetic caller was admitted to the room and then delivered nothing now finalizes as `error` with the code `caller_delivery_failed`.
- Such an attempt previously kept the `pass` its offline evaluation had earned, so a suite reported green for a room in which no audio ever reached your AI agent.
- Nothing is newly broken by this: those attempts already delivered nothing, and a suite that starts reporting `caller_delivery_failed` is naming a fault it already had.

Ids that previously answered `500` for a record that does not exist answer `404` as well.

- Choose `off` (default, not stored), `sound` (a quiet looping keyboard-typing bed, wordless in every language), or `acknowledge` (a short reviewed phrase in the agent's own voice).
- A delay from 0.6 to 3.0 seconds controls how long a tool may run before feedback starts; most tools finish inside it and the caller hears nothing.
- The control lives in the agent editor's Voice & behaviour page, next to Acknowledgements, with **Hear the phrase** and **Hear the typing sound** previews.
- The Calls page now shows a **Tool waits** section on call detail, listing the setting the call ran with and each start, cut, or skip with its reason and offset.
- [Turn on tool-wait feedback](/docs/voice-behavior#during-tool-waits)

## 5 September 2026

### Delivery profiles for agent voices

- An agent's voice can now perform its speech in one of four ways: `natural`, `lively`, `calm`, or `precise`.
- Set `voice_polish.delivery` on `PATCH /v1/agents/{agent_id}/voice-stack`, through the MCP `set_agent_voice_stack` tool, or per request on `POST /v1/voice-previews` to hear a profile before saving.
- Each profile drives one allowlisted control per provider and model, and the preview response reports what was actually applied.
- Leaving `delivery` unset keeps the provider's own defaults, so an agent that never chooses a profile sounds exactly as before.
- Fish and Azure voices accept and store a profile but do not change their audio yet.
- [Choose a delivery profile](/docs/delivery-profiles#set-it-through-the-api) · [Hear it before you save](/docs/delivery-profiles#hear-it-before-you-save)

### Acknowledgements while a slow reply is prepared

- An agent can now say one short phrase in its own voice while a slow answer is still being written, instead of leaving the caller in dead air.
- Enable `voice_polish.acknowledgements` on `PATCH /v1/agents/{agent_id}/voice-stack` or the MCP `set_agent_voice_stack` tool, with up to six phrases of your own and a delay of 600 to 3000 ms.
- The phrase plays on its own audio track and is cancelled the moment the real reply or the caller starts, at most once per turn and never during a goodbye.
- It is off unless you enable it, is not a transcript line, and a language with no reviewed phrases stays silent rather than falling back to English.
- [Turn on acknowledgements](/docs/voice-behavior#enable-acknowledgements-through-the-api) · [Set the delay and phrases](/docs/voice-behavior#how-the-worker-uses-it)
