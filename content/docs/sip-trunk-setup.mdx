title: Set up your own SIP trunk
description: Connect a SIP trunk from any carrier in the Skysay workspace, add your numbers and confirm they are yours.

Use your own SIP trunk when your numbers live at a carrier Skysay has no
automatic import for. You keep your carrier account, and your carrier keeps
billing the line and its minutes. Skysay charges platform and AI minutes only;
there is no monthly number fee.

Every carrier uses the same form. If your carrier can send calls to an address
and accept calls with a SIP username and password, or from a fixed IP address,
it can connect.

A SIP trunk carries voice only. Numbers on it can't send or receive SMS.

To do the same through the API, see
[Bring your own SIP trunk](/docs/bring-your-own-sip-trunk).

## What you need from your carrier

- The carrier's SIP server (a host name or a public IPv4 address), its port,
  and whether it uses UDP or TCP. Encrypted trunks (TLS and SRTP) aren't
  supported yet.
- How Skysay signs in for calls out: a SIP username and password, or an
  allow-list of Skysay's addresses.
- The public IPv4 addresses your carrier sends calls from. Ask your carrier
  for them if they aren't in its portal.
- The format your carrier uses for numbers: with `+` and the country code,
  the country code without `+`, the national format, or `00` and the country
  code.
- A trunk that doesn't require registration. Carriers that only accept a
  registered (logged-in) trunk aren't supported yet.

## Set it up in the workspace

Open **Numbers → Import a number** and choose **Any carrier: your own SIP
trunk**, then **Set up your SIP trunk**.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Connect your carrier

- Give the connection a name and choose your carrier's country. The country
  is used for numbers your carrier writes without the country code.
- Choose what the trunk carries: calls out, incoming calls, or both.
- For calls out, enter the carrier's SIP server, port and transport, and how
  Skysay signs in. A password is stored encrypted and never shown again.
- For incoming calls, enter your carrier's IP addresses, one per line: a
  single address, or a range such as `a.b.c.d/29`. Only calls from these
  addresses reach the connection.
- **Number formats and audio (advanced)** sets the number format for calls
  out, the caller ID format, the formats your carrier sends on incoming calls,
  the audio codecs (PCMA and PCMU) and an optional channel limit. Not sure?
  Keep the defaults, or ask your carrier which formats it uses.

Select **Save and check connection**.

</Step>

<Step>

### Check the connection

Each check stands on its own; a passed check doesn't promise that a call
works.

- **Settings** shows whether Skysay saved and set up the connection.
- **Skysay is open to your carrier** opens automatically for your carrier's
  addresses once you add a number for incoming calls. Until one of the
  connection's numbers is confirmed, it stays open for 24 hours after you last
  save the connection, add a number or check again.
- **Calls out** shows where Skysay sends your calls.
- **Skysay can reach your carrier:** select **Check reachability**. Within a
  few minutes the row shows the status your carrier's SIP server answered, in
  its own words, and says so if your carrier seems to want Skysay to register.
  What each answer means is in
  [Reachability](/docs/bring-your-own-sip-trunk#reachability).

</Step>

<Step>

### Add your numbers

Add each number you hold at your carrier, choose the Skysay-hosted AI agent
that answers its incoming calls, and choose whether it calls out. Agents
hosted outside Skysay (external agents) can't be used on your own trunk's
numbers yet.

When you add a number, Skysay calls it once through the phone network to
confirm it's yours. Nobody needs to answer: the call comes back to Skysay
through your carrier, and the check usually takes under a minute. The
**Ownership** column then shows one of:

- **Confirmed:** the number can call out. Calls out always show that number
  as caller ID.
- **Checking…:** the check call is on its way. The page updates by itself.
- **Not confirmed:** the check didn't succeed. The row says why; fix it at
  your carrier, then select **Check again**.
- **Needs support:** the number takes no incoming calls on this connection,
  so Skysay can't call it to confirm it. Contact support to confirm it.

Incoming calls work as soon as the number is added. Calls out with a number
start once it's confirmed.

</Step>

<Step>

### Test calls

Optional, but this is how a number gets its **Verified** badge. Test calls
are ordinary calls, billed like any other.

- **Call out through your carrier:** choose the number to call from, the
  Skysay-hosted AI agent on the call, and a phone of your own, then select
  **Place test call**. The page shows **Calling…**, then **Answered** or
  **Not answered** with the status your carrier returned. After a call that
  wasn't answered, select **Try** with the next number format; after one that
  was, select **Save this number format** to use it for every call.
- **Call in to your number:** choose the number, select **I'm calling now**,
  and call it from any phone within 3 minutes. The page shows the call once
  it arrives, with only the last four digits of the caller's number.

Each number then reads **Verified** or **Ready, not tested**. The rules,
limits and refusals for test calls are in
[Readiness checks](/docs/bring-your-own-sip-trunk#readiness-checks).

</Step>

<Step>

### Done

The summary lists each number, the AI agent that answers it, whether it
calls out, and whether it's **Verified**. Your numbers also appear on the **Numbers** page with a **Your SIP
trunk** badge. To change the connection later, open the number and select
**Manage SIP trunk**.

</Step>

</Steps>

## At your carrier

The **At your carrier** panel beside the steps shows what to set in your
carrier's portal:

1. Find the SIP trunk (or SIP forwarding) settings for your numbers.
2. Send incoming calls to the Skysay SIP server shown in the panel, on port
   5060, with the transport you chose.
3. Send the called number in one of the formats you chose on the connection.
4. Copy your SIP username and password into the form, or allow Skysay's
   addresses shown in the panel.

### Where to find it in your carrier's portal

Carriers name these settings differently. Most portals use labels like
these:

| Your carrier's portal | Where it goes in Skysay |
| --- | --- |
| **SIP trunk**, **SIP forwarding** or **SIP routing**: target, destination or forwarding address, and port | Enter the Skysay SIP server and port from the **At your carrier** panel there |
| **Called number prefix**, **DID format** or **number format** for incoming calls | Choose the matching format under **Number formats your carrier sends on incoming calls**; with a `+` and the country code is the most common |
| **SIP server**, **proxy**, **registrar** or **outbound proxy** | **Carrier SIP server** and **Port** |
| **SIP username**, **auth ID** or your account number | **SIP username** |
| **SIP password** or **secret** | **SIP password**; enter it exactly as your carrier shows it |
| **IP authentication**, **trusted IPs** or **allowed addresses** | Choose **By IP address (no password)** in Skysay, and add Skysay's addresses from the panel at your carrier |
| **Signalling IPs**, **source addresses** or **media servers** your carrier sends calls from | **Your carrier's IP addresses** |
| **Registration** or **register trunk** | Leave it off. Trunks that must register aren't supported yet |

If your carrier's portal doesn't list the addresses it sends calls from, ask
its support for them.

## When something doesn't work

- **A refused IP address:** use your carrier's public IPv4 addresses. Host
  names, ranges wider than `/16`, and private, reserved or documentation
  addresses are refused, as are addresses Skysay's own carriers use. A range
  starts at its first address (`a.b.c.0/24`, not `a.b.c.1/24`).
- **Not confirmed, "Our call didn't reach this connection":** make sure your
  carrier sends the number's incoming calls to the Skysay SIP server, from the
  addresses on the connection, in a number format the connection lists. Then
  select **Check again**.
- **Not confirmed, "its code wasn't heard":** the call arrived, but the keypad
  tones on it didn't. Ask your carrier to pass keypad tones (DTMF) on calls,
  then check again.
- **Too many checks:** checks are limited per number and per workspace; the
  message says when to try again.
- **Already connected:** a number can be on one connection in all of Skysay.
  If it's connected somewhere you don't recognise, contact support.
- **Changing the carrier's addresses or SIP server:** every number is checked
  again after the change, and calls out with a number wait for its new
  confirmation.

## Paused calls

If a connection's numbers receive far more calls a minute than a real phone
line would, Skysay pauses the connection: its calls are refused and it places
no new calls. The workspace shows the reason and when it resumes by itself:
10 minutes after the calls stop, then 30 and 60 minutes if it happens again
within a day. More calls while it's paused extend the pause.

To resume it sooner, contact support, or resume it yourself through the API:
see [Paused connections](/docs/bring-your-own-sip-trunk#paused-connections).
